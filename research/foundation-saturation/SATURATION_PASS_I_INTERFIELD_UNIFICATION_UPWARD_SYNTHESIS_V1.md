**Status:** material changed-vocabulary pass after Pass H under issue #41. It changes the constructive objective from “find a small unowned residual” to “absorb parent sciences into a higher-order theory and test it against their strongest federation”. It resets the post-material clean-pass counter to zero. It adds no kernel coordinate, frozen law or paper identity.

## 1. Trigger

The previous foundation work correctly treated parent ownership as a novelty and fidelity constraint. However, an overly defensive reading could turn every close parent into a reason to contract.

That would miss a major mode of scientific advance: mature fields can be the prerequisites of a higher theory whose object is the relation among them.

The revised question is:

> Can Machine Epistemics absorb the valid theories and methods governing inquiry, recover their native successes, explain their relations and boundaries, and generate a more powerful and testable theory than their strongest existing federation?

## 3. Interfield theory

Darden and Maull analysed theories that bridge fields addressing different aspects of one phenomenon. Such a theory can:

- explain relations between fields;
- answer questions unavailable inside either field alone;
- redirect attention to neglected domain items;
- predict new items in one or both fields.

### ORION implication

The dense neighbourhood around Machine Epistemics is not merely a demarcation problem. Relations among formal epistemology, control, statistics, causal inference, measurement, assurance, cognition, social epistemology, distributed systems and scientific practice may themselves support an interfield science.

A software interface or common vocabulary is insufficient. The bridge must yield explanation, formal consequence, prediction, intervention or measurable decision value.

## 4. Explanatory unification

Kitcher's programme links understanding to deriving many accepted statements through a smaller economy of stringent argument patterns.

Potential donation:

- reduce ad hoc coordination rules across scientific workflow types;
- derive recurring transition/failure constraints from fewer principles;
- expose common structure across different scientific domains.

Limit:

Compression or derivational economy can erase causal relevance, mechanism, scale, measurement and context. A compact common schema is not automatically a better explanation.

### Disposition

Unification becomes one coordinate of theory quality, not a sufficient terminal.

## 5. Mechanism schemas

Mechanistic traditions analyse entities and activities organized so as to produce changes. Mechanism schemas can integrate explanations across fields or levels without one universal vocabulary.

### ORION implication

A candidate common mechanism is:

`scientific_state + epistemic_action + world_or_tool_response + interpretation + test_or_argument -> successor_scientific_state`.

The common schema must retain native entities, actions, measurement and validity conditions. Structural similarity creates a bridge hypothesis, not ownership or equivalence.

## 6. Integrative pluralism

Complex scientific systems can require multiple partial, idealized and perspectival models. Integration can occur without total unification or reduction. Criticism of integrative pluralism also shows that integration is not always possible or desirable.

### ORION implication

A higher theory may be a **typed federation**:

- several native views remain live;
- overlap and composition are explicit;
- conflicts and incommensurabilities remain representable;
- the sufficient view is selected relative to a decision;
- unresolved relations remain unresolved;
- no universal state language is assumed.

This is still higher-order if it explains and tests relations absent from the local sciences.

## 7. Reduction, emergence and multiple realization

A higher-level theory can remain explanatory even when lower-level theories exist. A cross-parent constraint may be multiply realized by distinct architectures and domains.

Candidate cross-parent constraints—still unfrozen—include:

- comparison identity conservation;
- authority non-amplification;
- approximation non-laundering;
- source/dependence visibility;
- error-detection adequacy;
- selective reopening;
- bounded closure;
- external witness requirements for machine-native internal computation.

These must still survive parent recovery, naturalistic restoration and component-value tests.

## 8. Theory virtues and progress

Accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity and fruitfulness are important but can conflict and admit different interpretations. Progressive research also requires more than retrospective accommodation: new predictions, discriminators, solved problems or reduced anomalies should receive independent support.

### ORION implication

Scientific superiority is represented as a non-compensatory partial order over:

- native fidelity;
- protected decision quality;
- prospective generativity;
- cross-domain scope;
- integrative/explanatory value;
- robustness/resilience;
- resource/implementation efficiency;
- auditability/authority honesty;
- recursive extensibility.

No single scalar or prestige judgment decides the theory.

## 9. Conservative extension and parent recovery

For each parent `P_i`, a higher theory `T` should normally provide:

`embed_i : P_i -> T`

and

`recover_i : T | context_i -> P_i`.

Registered parent judgments should be preserved. Failure of recovery must be classified as:

- justified parent revision;
- changed scope/context;
- approximation;
- mapping error;
- contradiction;
- `CANNOT_CHECK`.

A broad theory that silently destroys local validity is an overgeneralization, not an upward synthesis.

## 11. Four required foundation candidates

The post-saturation synthesis must compare:

1. `F0_STRONGEST_PARENT_FEDERATION`;
2. `F1_INTERFIELD_INTEGRATION_THEORY`;
3. `F2_ABSORPTIVE_SUPERTHEORY`;
4. `F3_DOMAIN_FEDERATION_WITHOUT_UNIVERSAL_LAWS`.

F2 is the aspirational target. F0, F1 and F3 remain necessary scientific controls and possible honest outcomes.

## 12. Upward theory obligations

A candidate F2 must:

1. reconstruct and credit every parent;
2. embed and recover native parent decisions;
3. explain scopes, failures and relations among parents;
4. preserve plural views when reduction loses protected information;
5. generate prospective cross-parent consequences;
6. outperform F0 on a protected cross-parent problem under matched resources;
7. survive a materially different domain;
8. expose cost, brittleness and authority limits;
9. defer locally when a parent remains superior;
10. remain revisable when new parents or source modes appear.

## 13. Prospective consequence requirement

Examples of evidence that could support upward theory status:

- a discriminator that no parent used separately or in the frozen F0 product proposes;
- a bridge/composition or impossibility theorem;
- a failure boundary revealed only by combining test sensitivity, dependence and transition semantics;
- a new naturalistic measurement requirement;
- a machine-native strategy predicted by the theory and independently validated;
- a prospectively useful research opportunity generated from cross-parent structure.

Retrospective explanation or literature compression alone cannot establish F2.

## 14. Cross-paper propagation

- **Flagship:** parent density becomes the intellectual substrate; final burden is recovery, bridge theory, generativity and dominance.
- **P-A:** native reconstruction and donor search become absorption machinery; MDL and automated-discovery parents remain strongest controls.
- **P-B:** embeddings, recovery maps and typed bridge/composition laws become the intertheory layer.
- **P-C:** tests whether higher-order transition/control laws improve scientific decisions and resilience.
- **P-D:** tests whether integrated dependence, severity, robustness, assurance and evaluator dynamics outperform F0.
- **P-E:** tests whether the theory is prospectively generative.
- **P-F:** tests whether the theory supports machine-native cognition beyond human imitation while preserving external scientific witnesses.

No new standalone paper is admitted by this pass.
