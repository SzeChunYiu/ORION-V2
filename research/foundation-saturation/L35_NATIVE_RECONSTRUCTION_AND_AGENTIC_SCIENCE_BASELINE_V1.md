## 1. Review cell

This tranche was cross-examined by six roles.

1. **Interfield-theory and history-of-science reviewer** — reconstructs why theories spanning mature fields can be scientifically new without misattributing their parents.
2. **Explanation and mechanism reviewer** — distinguishes derivational unification, causal/mechanistic integration and mere vocabulary compression.
3. **Pluralism and reduction reviewer** — protects domain-native models, scale and incommensurability from a universal ontology.
4. **Autonomous-science methods reviewer** — compares the proposed foundation against current agentic systems and rediscovery benchmarks.
5. **Evaluation and statistics reviewer** — asks whether benchmark outputs support scientific-transition claims, rather than only task completion.
6. **Hostile editor and parent-ownership reviewer** — seeks old-wine restatements, weak baselines, retrospective generativity and field inflation.

A finding is retained only if the parent remains recognizable after translation and the mapping changes a protected research or paper decision.

# Part I — Native reconstruction of upward scientific theory

## Card L35-A — Darden and Maull: interfield theories

### Native problem

Scientific fields can investigate different aspects of one domain while lacking a theory of their relationship. An interfield theory is not merely a multidisciplinary team or software interface. It is a scientific account of how items, processes or regularities in one field relate to those in another.

### Core structure

`InterfieldTheory = (Field_A, Field_B, shared_domain, bridge_problem, bridge_relations, new_questions, new_predictions)`.

### Positive mechanism

An interfield theory can:

- explain relations that neither field treats internally;
- identify domain items previously neglected by one field;
- make questions from one field answerable with concepts or observations from another;
- generate new predictions or research directions for both.

### ORION collision

Machine Epistemics has close parents because scientific-state change is already partitioned across formal epistemology, control, statistics, measurement, software validity, cognition, social epistemology and governance. The potentially new object is not an unowned local operation. It is the scientific relation among those operations when one executable research episode crosses their boundaries.

### Hostile non-application

If a workflow merely routes outputs between mature methods and adds no explanation, prediction, bridge theorem or protected decision gain, the result is engineering composition, not an interfield theory.

### Disposition

`PARENT_MODEL_FOR_F1_AND_F2_CONSTRUCTION`.

Primary anchor: Darden, L. & Maull, N. Interfield Theories. *Philosophy of Science* 44, 43–64 (1977). DOI: 10.1086/288723.

## Card L35-B — Kitcher: explanatory unification

### Native problem

Scientific explanation may gain power by deriving many accepted claims through a smaller and more stringent set of argument patterns.

### Core structure

`UnificationGain = broad_derivational_reach - ad_hoc_pattern_proliferation`.

### Positive mechanism

A unified account can improve understanding when it:

- reduces the number of independent explanatory patterns;
- shows apparently different phenomena as instances of one constrained structure;
- increases derivational scope without arbitrary auxiliary rules.

### Strong limitations

Compression is not automatically explanation. A formally compact derivation can erase:

- causal relevance;
- mechanism;
- scale;
- measurement conditions;
- context;
- intervention semantics;
- uncertainty and authority boundaries.

### ORION collision

A small set of machine-epistemic transition constraints would be scientifically valuable if they derive recurring failures across domains. But shorter receipt schemas or fewer module names do not establish unification.

### Hostile non-application

If two domains share a compact vocabulary but the common pattern does not preserve native decisions, the apparent unification is deceptive.

### Disposition

`THEORY_QUALITY_COORDINATE_NOT_SUFFICIENT_TERMINAL`.

Primary anchor: Kitcher, P. Explanatory Unification. *Philosophy of Science* 48, 507–531 (1981). DOI: 10.1086/289019.

## Card L35-C — mechanistic integration

### Native problem

Mechanistic explanation describes entities and activities organized so as to produce regular changes. Mechanism schemas can span levels or fields while retaining domain-specific parts and operations.

### Core structure

`Mechanism = (entities, activities, organization, start_conditions, termination_conditions, regular_change)`.

### Positive mechanism

Mechanistic integration can:

- relate different descriptions through organized production;
- identify missing components or activities;
- connect interventions to state changes;
- explain why a higher-level regularity holds;
- preserve local causal detail inside a broader schema.

### ORION collision

A candidate machine-epistemic mechanism is:

`scientific_state + epistemic_action + world/tool response + interpretation + validation -> successor_scientific_state`.

The common schema does not own the parent algorithms. It proposes a relation among their outputs and validity conditions.

### Hostile non-application

Not every scientific explanation is mechanistic in the same sense. Statistical, geometrical, symmetry, topological, functional or formal explanations can be scientifically adequate without being forced into entity–activity language.

### Disposition

`POTENTIAL_CROSS_PARENT_MECHANISM_SCHEMA__PLURAL_EXPLANATION_REQUIRED`.

Primary anchor: Machamer, P., Darden, L. & Craver, C. F. Thinking about Mechanisms. *Philosophy of Science* 67, 1–25 (2000). DOI: 10.1086/392759.

## Card L35-D — integrative pluralism

### Native problem

Complex systems can require several partial, idealized or perspectival models because no single representation supports every scientific purpose.

### Positive mechanism

Integration can proceed through:

- consistency checks;
- complementary scope;
- scale relations;
- shared boundary objects;
- coordinated interventions;
- local model selection;
- explicit unresolved relations.

Total reduction or one universal ontology is not necessary.

### ORION collision

The strongest candidate foundation may be a typed federation of native views rather than one monolithic machine-readable epistemology. A higher theory can remain scientifically superior if it explains when each view is sufficient, how views relate and where integration should stop.

### Hostile non-application

Pluralism cannot become an excuse to avoid contradiction, testing or relation claims. Two models that make incompatible predictions under the same registered conditions require discrimination, not a celebration of perspectives.

### Disposition

`F2_MUST_SUPPORT_NONREDUCTIVE_NATIVE_VIEWS`; `F3_REMAINS_SERIOUS_CONTROL`.

Anchors:

- Mitchell, S. D. *Biological Complexity and Integrative Pluralism* (Cambridge University Press, 2003).
- Mitchell, S. D. & Dietrich, M. R. Integration without unification. *American Naturalist* 168, S73–S79 (2006). DOI: 10.1086/509050.

## Card L35-E — reduction, emergence, multiple realization and conservative recovery

### Native problem

A higher theory can relate to lower theories through reduction, approximation, constraint, correspondence, emergence or coexistence. Higher-level regularities can remain explanatory even when lower-level realizations vary.

### Candidate relation families

- exact definitional reduction;
- limiting/correspondence relation;
- conservative extension;
- approximate reduction;
- coarse-graining;
- multiple realization;
- emergent constraint;
- domain-limited coexistence;
- incompatibility/`CANNOT_CHECK`.

### ORION collision

For every parent `P_i`, a candidate higher theory `T` needs:

`embed_i : P_i -> T`

and

`recover_i : T | context_i -> P_i`.

A higher theory is not upward when it cannot recover critical native judgments or explain the justified revision.

### Hostile non-application

Recovery cannot be demanded outside the parent’s legitimate scope. Conversely, claiming that all failed recovery is “emergence” would make the higher theory unfalsifiable.

### Disposition

`PARENT_RECOVERY_IS_HARD_GATE`; relation family remains context-specific.

## Card L35-F — theory virtues and scientific progress

### Native problem

Theories are judged through several virtues—accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity and fruitfulness—but these can conflict and be weighted differently. Research programmes can also appear active while progressing only through post-hoc repair.

### ORION reduction

Theory superiority must be a non-compensatory partial order, not one prestige score.

Candidate vector:

`(native_fidelity, decision_quality, generativity, cross_domain_scope, integration, robustness, efficiency, auditability, extensibility)`.

### Progressive programme signal

- prospective consequences created before outcomes;
- independent corroboration;
- resolved cross-parent anomalies;
- fewer ad hoc bridge rules;
- accurate predictions of the theory’s own failure scope;
- preserved parent capabilities.

### Degeneration signal

- growing exception and adapter count;
- repeated retrospective reinterpretation;
- no independently confirmed consequence;
- benchmark-specific success;
- hidden resource escalation;
- loss of local parent performance.

### Disposition

`THEORY_DOMINANCE_PROTOCOL_REQUIRED`; no scalar superiority.

# Part II — Current agentic-science and evaluation baseline

## Card AAS-1 — end-to-end and multi-agent scientific systems

Current systems increasingly integrate literature search, hypothesis generation, computation, experiment planning and analysis. Examples include:

- Robin, a multi-agent system reported in *Nature* in 2026 for hypothesis generation and data analysis in experimental biology;
- SPARK in cancer pathology, evaluated across multiple cohorts and cancer types;
- CellVoyager in computational biology, evaluated on published single-cell studies and expert-reviewed case studies;
- AgenticSciML for collaborative design of scientific machine-learning systems;
- AutoLabs for autonomous chemical experimentation with self-correcting agents;
- domain-specific multi-agent catalyst discovery and other closed-loop systems.

### What these systems establish

They establish that increasingly long scientific workflows can be executed by agent systems and that domain-specific useful outputs can be produced.

### What they do not establish by themselves

They do not establish:

- a general theory of scientific-state transitions;
- independent evidence merely through agent count;
- correctness of a scientific interpretation;
- safe transport across domains;
- novelty relative to all parent science;
- legitimate authority to adopt or execute a result;
- a superior machine-native way of thinking.

### ORION implication

The strongest baseline cannot be a fixed workflow or single LLM. It must include contemporary multi-agent systems, domain tools, human checkpoints and realistic resource budgets.

Selected current anchors:

- Ghareeb, A. E. et al. A multi-agent system for automating scientific discovery. *Nature* 655, 497–505 (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10652-y.
- Alber, S. et al. CellVoyager: AI CompBio agent generates new insights by autonomously analysing biological data. *Nature Methods* 23, 749–759 (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41592-026-03029-6.

## Card AAS-2 — current full-cycle benchmarks reveal a large reliability gap

### FIRE-Bench

FIRE-Bench evaluates autonomous agents on rediscovering established machine-learning findings from a high-level research question through idea generation, experimental design, implementation, execution and evidence-supported conclusion. Its reported strongest systems remain below 50 F1 and show high run-to-run variance and recurring experimental-design, execution and evidence-reasoning failures.

### AutoResearchBench

AutoResearchBench evaluates deep and wide scientific-literature discovery. Its reported strongest models achieve roughly single-digit accuracy/IoU, revealing that comprehensive source discovery remains far from solved even after progress on general web-browsing benchmarks.

### Implication

A field-defining theory cannot rely on claims that autonomous scientific research is already robust. Current evidence supports a programme built around:

- failure diagnosis;
- source and computation identity;
- coverage/censoring;
- repeated-run variance;
- oracle adequacy;
- evidence-based conclusion quality;
- resource accounting;
- `CANNOT_CHECK`.

Anchors:

- Wang, Z. et al. FIRE-Bench: Evaluating Agents on the Rediscovery of Scientific Insights. arXiv:2602.02905 (2026).
- Xiong, L. et al. AutoResearchBench: Benchmarking AI Agents on Complex Scientific Literature Discovery. arXiv:2604.25256 (2026).

## Card AAS-3 — environment engineering and system-level evaluation

Recent agent work increasingly treats the environment—not only the language model—as the scientific-control substrate. Environment engineering can bind permissions, artifacts, budgets and human intervention. This aligns with ORION’s system-level object, but it also creates a strong parent threat.

### Parent-owned capabilities

- bounded permissions;
- execution sandboxes;
- persistent artifacts and Git histories;
- budget-aware exploration;
- human checkpoints;
- multi-agent coordination.

### Candidate residual

The higher-order residual cannot be generic environment engineering. It must show that scientific-state and validity semantics change protected decisions beyond an engineered agent environment.

Current anchor: EurekAgent, arXiv:2606.13662 (2026).

## Card AAS-4 — editor-level expectations

Nature Machine Intelligence has explicitly warned that multi-agent systems need transparent motivations and explanations, partly because poorly justified architectures can waste computational and human resources. This matches ORION’s component-value and simple-control requirements.

### Editorial implication

A paper is not strengthened merely by:

- more agents;
- longer reasoning traces;
- more review rounds;
- more tools;
- a larger ontology;
- elaborate workflow diagrams.

Every component must identify the exact scientific decision it changes and the cost it adds.

Anchor: *Multi-agent AI systems need transparency*, *Nature Machine Intelligence* 8, 1 (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s42256-026-01183-2.

# Part III — Cross-card synthesis

## 1. What the proposed field must absorb

A credible Machine Epistemics foundation must absorb at least:

- reliable inquiry and learning limits;
- belief revision and diagnosis;
- action selection and experiment design;
- causal and measurement transport;
- numerical and software validity;
- evidence dependence and assurance;
- exploratory and constructive experimentation;
- social criticism and governance;
- machine-native search and representation;
- autonomous-science system architecture and benchmarks;
- interfield theory, mechanism, pluralism and theory progress.

## 2. What would be genuinely upward

The programme becomes more than a parent federation only when it can demonstrate at least one of:

- a bridge theorem or impossibility result connecting parent validity conditions;
- a cross-parent discriminator missed by the strongest parent federation;
- a naturalistic scientific decision improved under matched resources;
- a prospective research opportunity generated from the integrated theory;
- a machine-native strategy predicted by the theory and independently validated;
- a substantial reduction of ad hoc coordination while preserving native scientific decisions.

## 3. What would falsify the field-level supertheory

- F0 produces the same protected decisions and generative outputs more simply;
- the common abstraction disappears after native semantics are restored;
- parent recovery fails in critical domains;
- gains are explained by compute, hidden expert labour or benchmark design;
- naturalistic value does not transfer;
- the theory grows through adapters without prospective consequences;
- the public laws are only governance recommendations, not scientific regularities.

This deep reconstruction strengthens L35 and the current baseline but does not add a new kernel coordinate. Its material contribution is sharper parent recovery, generativity and publication-evidence obligations.
