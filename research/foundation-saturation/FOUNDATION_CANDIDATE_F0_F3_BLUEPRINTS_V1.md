# Foundation Candidates F0–F3 — Frozen Blueprint V1

**Canonical foundation issue:** #41  
**Upward-synthesis issue:** #43  
**Status:** pre-outcome theory blueprints. The four candidates are deliberately specified before any comparative outcome. None is selected, founded or publication-authorized by this document.

## 1. Why four candidates are necessary

A new field cannot be established by comparing one preferred theory against an undefined collection of prior work. The programme therefore freezes four distinct interpretations of the same parent-space evidence:

- **F0:** mature parent federation with no new cross-parent theory;
- **F1:** selective interfield integration theory;
- **F2:** absorptive supertheory of scientific-state transitions;
- **F3:** domain federation with interoperability but no universal transition laws.

The candidates receive matched source, tool, compute, evaluator and human-expert access. Parent wins, ties and `CANNOT_CHECK` remain valid outcomes.

## 2. Shared evaluation object

All candidates act on the same bounded episode contract:

`E = (P, F, S, O, A, R, M, V, X, H, K)`

where the tuple is a comparison interface, not a frozen ontology. Optional parent-facing state can include:

- epistemic action mode;
- experimental-system state;
- computational-validity receipt;
- oracle adequacy;
- uncertainty form;
- test sensitivity;
- calibration iteration;
- boundary/semantic context;
- governed-data/custody state;
- inductive-bias declaration.

Every candidate must specify whether each item is native state, a bridge receipt, a parent adapter or irrelevant to the case.

# F0 — Strongest Parent Federation

## 3. Scientific thesis

The relevant problems are already solved, in principle, by mature sciences. Reliable machine-mediated research requires careful engineering composition, not a new higher-order scientific theory.

## 4. Architecture

F0 retains native parent representations and routes cases to the strongest applicable subsystem.

### F0-A — inquiry and revision cluster

- computational/formal epistemology;
- reliable inquiry and learning theory;
- belief revision;
- TMS/ATMS;
- model-based diagnosis.

### F0-B — action and experiment cluster

- Bayesian experimental design;
- active learning;
- sequential analysis;
- POMDP/metareasoning;
- deep-uncertainty methods;
- robust control and adaptive pathways.

### F0-C — relation and scientific reuse cluster

- causal transportability;
- measurement invariance and metrology;
- abstract interpretation/bisimulation;
- stochastic simulation;
- ontology/semantic alignment;
- competence and procedural-transfer methods.

### F0-D — evidence and assurance cluster

- provenance;
- dependent-evidence/meta-analysis;
- robustness and triangulation;
- severe testing;
- assurance cases and formal argumentation;
- performative evaluation.

### F0-E — computation and implementation cluster

- numerical analysis;
- VVUQ;
- scientific-software verification;
- validated numerics;
- differential/metamorphic testing;
- benchmark and deployment stress testing.

### F0-F — discovery and machine intelligence cluster

- literature-based discovery;
- automated scientific discovery;
- program synthesis/theorem proving;
- self-play/open-ended/evolutionary search;
- agent environment engineering;
- human–AI collaboration.

### F0-G — social, institutional and authority cluster

- social epistemology and testimony;
- evidence-to-decision frameworks;
- research ethics;
- FAIR/CARE and data governance;
- standards, law and institutional authority.

## 5. Coordination rule

The federation uses explicit workflow and data contracts. When several parents apply, an expert-configured router selects or composes them. It does not claim universal cross-parent laws beyond compatibility rules necessary for implementation.

## 6. F0 strengths

- maximum native fidelity;
- access to mature algorithms and evidence;
- lower field-inflation risk;
- local specialists remain authoritative;
- no universal theory burden.

## 7. F0 expected weaknesses

- cross-parent conflicts may require ad hoc arbitration;
- evidence, relation, computation and authority states can be duplicated;
- a failure can cross subsystem boundaries without one owner;
- global closure and selective reopening may be inconsistently defined;
- machine-native architectures may exploit gaps between parent interfaces;
- new bridge questions may remain invisible.

## 8. F0 falsifier

F0 is insufficient if an independently frozen F1/F2 bridge or transition proposition repeatedly changes protected decisions or generates verified consequences beyond the strongest federation under matched resources.

# F1 — Interfield Integration Theory

## 9. Scientific thesis

Selected relations among mature parent sciences are themselves stable scientific problems. Typed bridge theories can solve these cross-boundary problems without requiring one universal theory of inquiry.

## 10. Core object

`InterfieldBridge = (Parent_A, Parent_B, shared_domain, registered_decision, relation, assumptions, witnesses, counterprobes, recovery, expiry)`.

## 11. Candidate bridge families

### B1 — execution-to-evidence bridge

Relates software/tool execution receipts to claim-relevant evidence under model, oracle and source conditions.

### B2 — relation-to-revalidation bridge

Relates abstraction/transport certificates to affected commitments and selective reopening.

### B3 — dependence-to-assurance bridge

Relates common-cause evidence graphs, robustness routes and argument/assurance structures.

### B4 — uncertainty-to-action bridge

Relates uncertainty form, value-of-information, deep uncertainty and reversible/adaptive action.

### B5 — exploration-to-confirmation bridge

Relates exploratory/constructive actions to later hypotheses, tests and evidential status.

### B6 — computation-to-scientific-validity bridge

Relates model adequacy, numerical validity, oracle adequacy and claim scope.

### B7 — scientific-support-to-authority bridge

Relates evidence certainty and expected consequences to externally supplied permission, consent and governance.

### B8 — machine-native-process-to-public-witness bridge

Relates nonhuman internal computation to claim-sufficient external evidence and uncertainty interfaces.

## 12. F1 strengths

- identifies genuine interfield questions;
- preserves parent languages;
- can yield bridge theorems and counterexamples;
- limits theory claims to tested parent pairs or clusters;
- avoids premature universal ontology.

## 13. F1 expected weaknesses

- bridge proliferation can become another adapter catalogue;
- pairwise relations may not compose globally;
- no common account of programme-level closure or recursive extension;
- higher-order contradictions can remain unresolved;
- generativity may stay local.

## 14. F1 falsifier

F1 contracts to F0 if bridges reproduce only engineering interfaces or existing parent compositions. It is insufficient relative to F2 if stable cross-bridge laws and programme-level gains survive.

# F2 — Absorptive Supertheory Candidate

## 15. Scientific thesis

Machine-mediated inquiry is a class of state-changing scientific systems. Mature parent sciences are recoverable native views inside a typed federation, while a small set of cross-parent transition constraints explains and predicts when scientific commitments may change, remain valid, reopen or stop.

## 16. Candidate object

`ScientificTransitionSystem = (`
`native_views, scientific_state, problem_and_criterion_identity, action_modes, world_tool_responses, interpretation_relations, evidence_and_argument, computation_and_oracles, uncertainty, authority, transition_history, reopen_and_closure_rules)`.

The object is deliberately architecture-independent and may be implemented by symbolic, neural, probabilistic, programmatic, human–machine or hybrid systems.

## 17. Candidate propositions — not frozen laws

### F2-P1 — Bound transition identity

A scientific transition has a recoverable subject, problem/criterion identity, source/evidence basis, representation epoch and authority scope. Silent mutation creates a new comparison or invalid transition.

**Main parents:** provenance, metrology, formal specification, benchmark custody, philosophy of measurement.

### F2-P2 — Warrant factorization and weakest-link ceiling

A claim supported through source, semantic interpretation, model, computation, test/oracle, argument, transport and authority cannot exceed the weakest materially necessary link. The factors remain separable because they fail differently.

**Main parents:** assurance, numerical analysis, software V&V, severe testing, epistemology, formal argumentation.

### F2-P3 — Typed relation and uncertainty conservation

Relation type, approximation, uncertainty form, dependence and unresolved distinctions cannot silently strengthen or disappear through composition.

**Main parents:** abstract interpretation, causal transport, measurement invariance, imprecise probability, metrology.

### F2-P4 — Error-detection adequacy

A pass supports only error classes the evaluator/oracle had a warranted capacity to expose under the registered case and selection history.

**Main parents:** severe testing, power/sensitivity, metamorphic testing, formal coverage.

### F2-P5 — Selective invalidation and reopening

When support, relation, computation or evaluator validity fails, only commitments whose complete sufficient support is invalidated must reopen; independent support remains standing.

**Main parents:** TMS/ATMS, belief revision, diagnosis, support hypergraphs.

### F2-P6 — Exploration/confirmation separation with constructive state change

Exploratory and constructive actions may legitimately expand observables, representations, instruments and problem frames, but their outputs do not inherit confirmatory authority without subsequent discrimination and testing.

**Main parents:** exploratory experimentation, automated discovery, abduction, scientific practice.

### F2-P7 — Parent recovery and local deference

A higher-order theory must recover native parent judgments in their valid domains or provide explicit counterevidence/scope revision. When a parent is sufficient and more efficient, the higher layer remains inactive.

**Main parents:** interfield theory, conservative extension, pluralism, component-value analysis.

### F2-P8 — Machine-native internal freedom with external scientific witness

Internal reasoning need not resemble human cognition or natural-language traces. Adoption of a scientific transition requires a claim-relative external witness sufficient for evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, relation, affected commitments and authority.

**Main parents:** automated reasoning, program synthesis, interpretability, scientific accountability.

### F2-P9 — Bounded closure with explicit censorship and reopening

Closure is relative to a declared search/evaluation universe, censored routes, residual uncertainty and omission challenge. A material new route or parent reopens the terminal.

**Main parents:** formal learning, systematic review, stopping theory, qualitative saturation, metascience.

## 18. F2 claimed generative mechanisms

F2 is expected to generate value through:

- cross-parent failure composition;
- automatic identification of the weakest warrant link;
- cross-domain discriminator construction;
- selective reopening rather than global reset;
- choosing the minimum sufficient scientific intervention;
- detecting cases where F0 parents individually pass but their composition is invalid;
- predicting when machine-native internal strategies require stronger external witnesses.

## 19. F2 strengths if supported

- common scientific-transition object;
- explicit parent recovery;
- reduced ad hoc arbitration;
- cross-parent failure diagnosis;
- extensibility to new domains and machine substrates;
- prospective generativity;
- a coherent field-level empirical programme.

## 20. F2 risks

- deceptive unification;
- oversized metadata and performance drag;
- hidden universal ontology;
- retrospective restatement of parent results;
- unfalsifiable “weakest link” language;
- over-conservatism;
- authority inflation through comprehensiveness;
- benchmark design tailored to F2 vocabulary.

## 21. F2 falsifier

F2 fails if F0/F1 reproduce all protected decisions and generative outputs with equal or lower cost; if parent recovery fails; if naturalistic value does not transfer; or if its propositions only restate governance good practice.

# F3 — Domain Federation Without Universal Transition Laws

## 22. Scientific thesis

The sciences of inquiry are too heterogeneous for universal Machine Epistemics laws. Useful progress comes from domain-native methods plus interoperable boundary objects, standards and audit interfaces.

## 23. Architecture

- domain-native state and evidence models;
- local instruments and evaluators;
- local authority and ethics;
- shared source/provenance identities;
- typed boundary interfaces;
- explicit translation loss;
- no universal transition semantics beyond minimal interoperability.

## 24. F3 strengths

- strongest protection of epistemic cultures and domain semantics;
- low risk of false universalization;
- local methodological innovation remains primary;
- authority/custody can remain institution-specific.

## 25. F3 expected weaknesses

- recurring cross-domain failures may be rediscovered independently;
- weak support for general machine-native research infrastructure;
- difficult programme-level evaluation and transfer;
- no shared theory of cross-domain closure or scientific-state change.

## 26. F3 falsifier

F3 is insufficient if stable cross-domain propositions repeatedly preserve native verdicts and improve protected decisions beyond local interfaces.
