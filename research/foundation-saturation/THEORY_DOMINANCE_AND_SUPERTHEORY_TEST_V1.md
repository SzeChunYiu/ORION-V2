# Theory Dominance and Absorptive Supertheory Test V1

**Status:** protected evaluation protocol for deciding whether an integrated Machine Epistemics theory is scientifically higher-order than its strongest parents. It grants no superiority before the specified evidence exists.

## 1. Why “superior” must be operational

A broader theory is not necessarily better. It may be vaguer, more expensive, less testable, less accurate in parent domains or merely a relabelling of existing knowledge.

Scientific superiority must therefore be stated relative to:

- registered problems and decisions;
- parent theories and their strongest composition;
- information and resource budgets;
- validity and authority constraints;
- domains and epochs;
- evidence and evaluator identities.

There is no context-free scalar “best theory”. The primary object is a non-compensatory partial order.

## 2. Objects

Let the mature parent family be:

`P = {P_1, ..., P_n}`.

Let `P*` be the strongest information- and resource-matched parent federation or composition available for the registered problem set.

Let candidate higher theory be:

`T = (native_views, embeddings, recovery_maps, bridges, transition_laws, policies, tests, costs, authority_limits)`.

Let the frozen evaluation contract be:

`C = (cases, decisions, domains, epochs, resources, evaluators, critical_failures, uncertainty_rule)`.

## 3. Hard parent-recovery gate

For each parent `P_i`, define registered judgments/actions `J_i` and contexts `D_i`.

Candidate `T` must provide:

`embed_i : P_i -> T`

and

`recover_i : T | D_i -> P_i`.

Define native fidelity:

`F_i(T) = preservation(recover_i(embed_i(P_i)), J_i)`.

### Required outcome

- exact preservation where the parent relation is exact;
- tolerance-bound preservation where approximation is declared;
- explicit counterevidence and revalidation when the higher theory rejects a parent verdict;
- `CANNOT_CHECK` when parent recovery cannot be evaluated.

A theory that loses critical native decisions is not higher-order; it is an overgeneralization or replacement proposal that needs separate evidence.

## 4. Strongest-parent lower bound

`P*` must be allowed:

- the same evidence and source access;
- the same tools and domain algorithms;
- the same compute/time budget or an explicit resource curve;
- the same evaluator information;
- expert routing and composition where realistically available;
- native parent-specific representations;
- no deliberate handicapping to make integration appear valuable.

The relevant comparison is not `T` versus one isolated weak parent. It is `T` versus the best defensible parent federation.

## 5. Scientific dominance vector

Evaluate:

`V_C(T) = (F, Q, G, X, I, R, E, A, Z)`

where:

### F — Native fidelity

Preservation of parent judgments, calibrations, invariants, competence and authority boundaries.

### Q — Protected decision quality

Justified-terminal rate, accuracy where meaningful, false completion, unsafe transport, calibration, selective reopening, false refusal and other case-specific scientific endpoints.

### G — Generativity

Prospectively frozen new predictions, experiments, bridge relations, theorems, impossibility results, measurements or opportunity discoveries.

### X — Cross-domain scope and transfer

Performance and validity across materially different domains without silent semantic flattening.

### I — Integrative/explanatory value

Ability to explain relations among parents, resolve cross-field questions, reduce ad hoc coordination rules and expose why local theories apply.

### R — Robustness and resilience

Sensitivity to assumption changes, evaluator/tool failures, resource limits, perturbations and recovery conditions.

### E — Efficiency

Latency, compute, memory, annotation, human adjudication, implementation burden and maintenance/failure surface.

### A — Auditability and authority honesty

Recoverable evidence/argument/transition witnesses and no authority amplification.

### Z — Recursive extensibility

Ability to absorb a new parent/domain/representation through explicit additions and revalidation without silently invalidating unrelated knowledge.

## 6. Non-compensatory failures

The following block superiority regardless of gains elsewhere:

- new critical false completion;
- evidence/source corruption;
- unsafe transport or silent approximation laundering;
- criterion drift;
- parent-native critical capability loss without justified revision;
- authority/custody violation;
- outcome leakage into prospective claims;
- unreported compute/resource advantage;
- inability to recover which theory or evidence supported the decision.

## 7. Dominance relation

`T >=_C P*` when, under contract `C`:

1. all hard validity and authority gates pass;
2. parent-native decisions are preserved or explicitly and validly revised;
3. `T` is no worse than `P*` on every registered non-cost scientific-quality coordinate within the frozen tolerance;
4. resource differences are declared and compared through matched budgets or Pareto curves;
5. no hidden evaluator/source advantage exists.

`T >_C P*` — **strict scientific dominance** — additionally requires at least one strict gain in a scientific-quality, generativity, integrative or cross-domain coordinate, not merely cosmetic documentation.

A compute-only speedup can establish engineering value but not by itself an upward scientific theory.

## 8. Absorptive supertheory status

A candidate earns `ABSORPTIVE_SUPERTHEORY_CANDIDATE` only if:

1. `T >=_C P*` on all frozen parent-domain controls;
2. `T >_C P*` on at least one protected cross-parent problem;
3. at least one gain is **generative** or **explanatory/integrative**, not only lower cost;
4. the gain survives a materially different domain or epoch;
5. component interventions identify a plausible mechanism for the gain;
6. independent evaluation confirms the semantic decision when no exact answer exists;
7. the theory states where native parents remain locally preferable;
8. the result survives a hostile “mere composition” reconstruction.

Stronger status `PROVISIONAL_SUPERTHEORY_SUPPORTED` additionally requires multiple independent cross-domain programmes and prospective consequences.

No internal ORION study grants an unqualified terminal `SUPERIOR_THEORY_PROVEN`.

## 9. Theory dispositions

### `ABSORPTIVE_SUPERTHEORY_CANDIDATE`

Parent recovery plus a protected cross-parent generative/decision gain is established, but replication and field authority remain open.

### `INTEGRATIVE_THEORY_ADVANCE`

The theory explains/coordinates parent relations or reduces ad hoc interfaces, but has not yet established new cross-domain laws or predictions.

### `INTERFIELD_SPECIALIZATION`

A valuable bridge exists between selected fields but does not justify a general Machine Epistemics theory.

### `FEDERATED_PARENT_EQUIVALENT`

The integrated theory matches the strongest parent federation with no material gain. It may remain engineering infrastructure.

### `PARENT_COMPOSITION_SUFFICIENT`

Existing parents reproduce the protected decisions more simply or efficiently.

### `DOMAIN_SPECIFIC_ADVANCE`

A real improvement exists only in one native field; ownership returns there.

### `DECEPTIVE_UNIFICATION`

Compression or vocabulary unification hides native failures, dependence, uncertainty or authority differences.

### `OVERGENERALIZED_THEORY`

Broader scope causes material parent-domain regressions.

### `REDUNDANT_DRAG`

The higher theory adds no protected value and increases cost/failure surface.

### `CANNOT_CHECK`

Required parent, evaluator, naturalistic or prospective evidence is unavailable.

## 10. Generativity test

A proposed novel consequence `g` is valid evidence only when:

1. derived or selected before outcome access;
2. content-bound to the theory version and assumptions;
3. not copied from prompts, training examples or hidden parent outputs;
4. compared against what `P*` can generate under the same information;
5. tested by an evaluator/observation independent of the generating route;
6. retained when negative or unresolved;
7. scoped to the domain in which it was tested.

Candidate generative outputs include:

- a previously unrecognized cross-field discriminating experiment;
- a theorem linking two parent relation families;
- a counterexample to a common parent composition;
- a new failure/observability boundary;
- a new source or measurement requirement;
- a prospectively valuable scientific opportunity.

## 11. Integrative compression test

The higher theory may reduce coordination complexity without forcing all parents into one language.

Measure, where meaningful:

- number of independent bridge rules;
- number of ad hoc human arbitration steps;
- duplicated state/evidence representations;
- unresolved mapping contradictions;
- proof/receipt size;
- model description length;
- implementation and maintenance cost.

Compression receives credit only when native fidelity and explanatory relevance are preserved. MDL is a parent baseline, not a universal truth criterion.

## 12. Causal attribution to the theory

Run:

- `FULL-T`;
- `MINUS-bridge-family`;
- `MINUS-transition-law`;
- `PARENT-replacement`;
- `FEDERATED-P*`;
- `SIMPLE-control`;
- pair interventions for suspected synergies;
- resource-matched curves.

A theory cannot claim the gain when:

- one domain algorithm produces it independently;
- more compute explains it;
- additional human expertise is hidden in the integrated arm;
- a benchmark was designed around its vocabulary;
- the improvement disappears after restoring native semantics.

## 13. Progress over time

A higher theory should be evaluated as a research programme:

`Progress_t = (`
`new_prospective_consequences, solved_cross_parent_problems, reduced_anomalies, parent_recovery, robustness, efficiency, ad_hoc_patch_rate, unresolved_critical_failures)`.

Warning terminal:

`DEGENERATING_SUPERTHEORY_PROGRAMME` — the theory expands by adapters and post-hoc qualifications without new independently confirmed consequences or improved decisions.

A temporarily unresolved programme is not automatically abandoned, but its progress claims must contract.

## 14. Minimum evidence package

Before the flagship calls Machine Epistemics a higher-order scientific theory rather than a field hypothesis, require:

1. frozen parent-space atlas;
2. strongest parent federation specification;
3. parent recovery suite;
4. at least two cross-parent known-answer/hostile families;
5. at least two materially different naturalistic domain families;
6. prospective generativity test;
7. component and interaction ablations;
8. resource Pareto curves;
9. independent semantic evaluation;
10. negative and parent-win cases;
11. explicit authority/non-claim boundary;
12. external hostile field review.
