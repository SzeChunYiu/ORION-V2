# Foundation Rival-Synthesis Decision V1

**Canonical issue:** #41  
**Evidence surface:** F01–F22, L01–L41, material Passes H–K, clean Passes L–N

## 1. Decision question

Which foundation best absorbs the reconstructed parent sciences while preserving native validity, supporting machine-native intelligence, enabling upward theoretical progress and remaining falsifiable against the strongest existing federation?

The candidates are:

- **F0 — strongest parent federation:** mature sciences plus expert routing and compatibility contracts; no new higher-order theory.
- **F1 — interfield integration theory:** explicit bridge theories among selected parent pairs/clusters.
- **F2 — absorptive supertheory candidate:** native parents as recoverable views plus a small set of cross-parent scientific-transition constraints.
- **F3 — domain federation:** domain-native methods and authority with minimal interoperability; no universal transition laws.

## 2. Synthesis cell

1. **Parent-fidelity reviewer** — prevents F0 from becoming a strawman and tests native verdict recovery.
2. **Interfield theorist** — identifies stable bridge objects and rejects mere adapter catalogues.
3. **Formalizer** — tests whether F2 commitments are precise enough for proof/countermodel obligations.
4. **Domain-federation reviewer** — protects domain semantics, apparatus and institutional authority.
5. **Machine-native reviewer** — prevents human process from becoming the constitutional architecture.
6. **Systems-efficiency reviewer** — tests deactivation, overhead and maintenance/failure surface.
7. **Hostile field editor** — seeks parent sufficiency, deceptive unification and overgeneralization.

## 4. Candidate dispositions

### F0 — strongest empirical null and default operational baseline

F0 is the correct default for scientific claims because native parent sciences already own nearly every mechanism. It has the strongest current evidence base and lowest unification burden.

F0 remains insufficient **as a complete foundation candidate** only in the following provisional sense: expert routing and ad hoc compatibility do not yet supply one tested account of cross-parent identity, validity composition, selective reopening, machine-native witness and bounded closure. Whether this gap has measurable value is exactly the protected discriminator question.

**Disposition:** `STRONGEST_NULL_AND_REQUIRED_COMPARATOR`.

### F1 — minimum justified theoretical advance

The atlas found real interfield questions:

- computation to scientific validity;
- uncertainty to action;
- relation expiry to selective reopening;
- dependence to assurance;
- exploration to confirmation;
- machine-native process to external witness;
- evidence to authority.

These are not captured by a citation list alone. Typed bridges are therefore scientifically justified as research objects even before F2 wins.

**Disposition:** `MINIMUM_JUSTIFIED_INTERFIELD_THEORY`.

### F2 — selected upward candidate, not established supertheory

F2 is the only candidate designed to answer the user's upward objective: recover the parent sciences, unify their cross-boundary constraints and generate new protected scientific decisions. It has a coherent testable architecture and clear falsifiers.

However, F2 currently has no executed strict-gain evidence over F0/F1/F3. Calling it a superior theory now would violate its own parent-recovery and theory-dominance constraints.

**Disposition:** `SELECTED_BOUNDED_ABSORPTIVE_FOUNDATION_CANDIDATE__SUPERIORITY_UNEARNED`.

### F3 — constitutional native/domain layer

F3 correctly protects native domain semantics, apparatus, institutional authority, custody and local scientific practice. It is not merely a competing external theory; it is a necessary constitutional lower layer for any acceptable F2.

An F2 that cannot represent F3-style local views or deactivate in their favor is deceptive unification.

**Disposition:** `REQUIRED_DOMAIN_NATIVE_CONSTITUTIONAL_LAYER`.

## 5. Selected layered architecture

The bounded foundation is a **federated absorptive stack**, not a replacement hierarchy:

```text
Layer 0 — Domain/native scientific practices and authority (F3)
    Each domain retains its objects, instruments, methods, evaluators,
    validity criteria, source/custody rules and legitimate decision rights.

Layer 1 — Strongest mature parent federation (F0)
    Native formal, statistical, computational, cognitive, social,
    engineering, governance and machine-intelligence methods operate directly.

Layer 2 — Typed interfield bridges (F1)
    Explicit relations connect selected parent outputs without erasing
    their assumptions, uncertainty, losses, dependence or authority.

Layer 3 — Absorptive transition metatheory candidate (F2)
    A small set of constraints governs cross-parent scientific-state
    transitions, parent recovery, selective reopening, local deference,
    machine-native external witnessing and bounded closure.
```

F2 receives no permission to bypass Layers 0–2. Its role is to expose and test composition-level scientific structure.

## 6. Why this is upward rather than merely additive

The architecture is upward only if the F2 layer eventually satisfies all of:

1. **Recovery:** reproduces critical native parent verdicts within scope.
2. **Compression:** explains several cross-parent failures with fewer independent ad hoc rules than F0/F1 routing.
3. **Strict protected gain:** changes at least one important scientific decision beyond F0 under matched resources.
4. **Prospective generativity:** predicts a discriminator, failure or opportunity before outcome access.
5. **Transfer:** survives at least two materially different scientific domains.
6. **Local deference:** remains inactive when a parent or direct method is sufficient.
7. **Efficiency/resilience:** does not impose universal drag or create a larger critical failure surface.
8. **Independent evaluation:** wins under outcome-blind external adjudication.

Until these conditions are met, “upward” denotes the architecture's research ambition, not an achieved ranking.

## 7. Selected commitment set

Nine F2 candidate propositions are contracted into seven internally coherent commitments:

1. bound transition identity and externally rooted authority;
2. layered warrant with claim-relative detection adequacy;
3. typed conservation of relation, uncertainty, dependence and loss;
4. plural sufficient support with selective invalidation and reopening;
5. constructive exploration separated from confirmation and bounded closure;
6. parent recovery, local deference and theory-dominance testing;
7. machine-native internal freedom with external scientific witness and governed use.

No commitment is frozen as a universal law. Each remains typed as a candidate formal, engineering, programme or governance constraint.

## 8. Rival falsifiers retained

### F0 wins when

native parents plus expert routing reproduce all protected decisions and generative outputs with equal or lower cost.

### F1 wins when

stable bridge theories improve selected cross-boundary problems but no programme-level F2 constraint adds value.

### F3 wins when

the shared abstraction loses native semantics or cross-domain propositions fail after domain restoration.

### F2 advances when

it recovers F0/F3, uses F1 bridges, produces prospective cross-parent strict gains, deactivates locally and survives naturalistic evaluation.

### No candidate wins when

required outcomes, evaluator identity, authority, sources or resource matching cannot be established. Terminal: `CANNOT_CHECK`.
