## 1. Constructive objective

The field programme is not searching for an empty territory with no parents. It seeks to build a higher-order science from the valid knowledge already distributed across mature fields.

The target is:

> Reconstruct and credit the parent sciences; preserve their native strengths and boundaries; explain and formalize their relations; derive prospective cross-parent consequences; and test whether the resulting theory is scientifically stronger than their best federation under matched information, resources and authority constraints.

Parent ownership is therefore:

- an attribution requirement;
- a fidelity/recovery requirement;
- a strongest-lower-bound requirement;
- a source of mechanisms, laws, counterexamples and methods.

It is not an automatic stopping rule.

## 3. Four required foundation candidates

### F0 — Strongest parent federation

Mature parents retain native languages, algorithms and evaluators and are coordinated through the strongest practical interfaces.

F0 is the lower bound. It is not a strawman and may be the correct final theory architecture.

### F1 — Interfield integration theory

Typed bridge theories explain selected relations among parent fields and solve cross-boundary questions. Universal laws are not required.

### F2 — Absorptive supertheory

A federated higher-order theory that:

- embeds and recovers native parents;
- explains why and where they apply;
- preserves plural views where one model is insufficient;
- defines cross-parent bridge and transition constraints;
- generates prospective predictions, experiments, theorems, failures or measurements;
- improves protected decisions beyond F0;
- allows machine-native internal computation with claim-sufficient external witnesses;
- absorbs new parents through explicit extension/revalidation;
- defers locally to specialised parents when they remain superior.

F2 is the aspirational target, not the preselected result.

### F3 — Domain federation without universal transition laws

Native sciences remain primary. Shared standards support interoperability, audit and comparison, but no general Machine Epistemics laws survive.

`embed_i : P_i -> T`
