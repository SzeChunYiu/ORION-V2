# Absorptive Upward Synthesis Principle V1

**Status:** foundation-construction principle under issue #41. It corrects an overly defensive interpretation of parent ownership. It does not establish that Machine Epistemics is already a superior theory or field.

## 1. Central correction

The existence of close parents is not a reason to abandon a new field. Mature parents are the material from which a stronger theory can be built.

ORION should distinguish:

1. **misattribution** — claiming ownership of a mechanism already established elsewhere;
2. **mere collection** — placing many parent ideas beside one another;
3. **engineering composition** — connecting parent modules without a new explanatory or predictive theory;
4. **interfield integration** — explaining relations between fields and solving questions no one field can solve alone;
5. **higher-order unification** — recovering valid parent results while deriving new cross-field laws, predictions, interventions or impossibility results;
6. **absorptive supertheory** — a higher-order theory that preserves parent-native validity where appropriate, explains why and when the parents apply, coordinates their conflicts and boundaries, and demonstrates protected scientific gains beyond their strongest composition.

Machine Epistemics should aim at levels 4–6. Parent ownership prevents false novelty, but it must not become an intellectual ceiling.

## 2. Historical and philosophical parent pressure

### Interfield theories

Darden and Maull analysed theories that bridge two fields sharing different aspects of the same phenomenon. An interfield theory can answer questions that neither field can answer alone, redirect attention to previously neglected domain items and predict new items for one or both fields.

This is a direct model for legitimate upward construction: the higher theory is valuable not because the lower fields were absent, but because their relationship was itself an unsolved scientific problem.

### Explanatory unification

Kitcher's unification programme links scientific understanding to deriving many accepted statements through a smaller economy of argument patterns. The donation is important but incomplete. Compression or a common derivation pattern is not sufficient when it erases causal relevance, mechanism, scale, measurement or context.

### Mechanistic integration

Mechanistic traditions treat explanation in terms of entities and activities organized so as to produce regular changes. Mechanism schemas can unify phenomena across domains without forcing all descriptions into one reductive vocabulary.

### Integrative pluralism

Mitchell's integrative pluralism argues that complex, multilevel and evolved systems can require multiple partial models. Integration can be scientifically valuable even when total unification or reduction is neither possible nor desirable.

Therefore “move upward” does not mean “flatten everything downward”. A superior theory may be federated, typed and plural while still explaining relations that the local theories leave unresolved.

### Theory choice and progress

Kuhn's theory-choice virtues—accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity and fruitfulness—are plural and can conflict. Lakatosian and problem-solving traditions add prospective progress, novel consequences and the resolution of anomalies or conceptual problems. No one virtue supplies an automatic total ranking.

ORION should therefore represent scientific superiority as a **partial order under a declared criterion**, not as a prestige label or one scalar score.

## 3. What it means to absorb a parent

For each parent theory or method `P_i`, a candidate higher theory `T` must provide:

### 3.1 Native reconstruction

Recover `P_i` in its own language, domain, assumptions, evidence standards, valid decisions and known failures.

### 3.2 Embedding or interpretation

Define a mapping:

`embed_i : P_i -> T`

that states which parent objects, relations and judgments correspond to which higher-theory objects.

### 3.3 Recovery

Define a recovery or projection:

`recover_i : T | context_i -> P_i`

and test whether registered parent judgments and actions are preserved.

The higher theory is not automatically better when it contradicts a parent. It must identify whether the parent was invalid, its domain was exceeded, its assumptions failed, or the higher mapping is wrong.

### 3.4 Boundary account

Explain why `P_i` works in its proper domain, where it fails, and which observation or intervention distinguishes its scope from neighbouring parents.

### 3.5 Bridge relations

State relations among parents that are absent or only informal in the local theories:

- correspondence;
- transport;
- composition;
- conflict;
- shared dependence;
- scale relation;
- evidence conversion;
- authority/custody boundary;
- transition or failure relation.

### 3.6 Generative consequence

Produce at least one consequence not available from a list of parent summaries:

- new testable prediction;
- new discriminating experiment;
- new formal theorem or impossibility result;
- new cross-domain transfer condition;
- new failure mode;
- new measurement or benchmark;
- more efficient justified action;
- explanation of an unresolved relation among fields.

Without generativity, the result is an atlas or interface, not yet a new theory.

## 4. Levels of upward construction

### Level U0 — Native parent sciences

Local theories, methods and practices retain their own standards and domains.

### Level U1 — Typed translation and interoperability

Objects can be related without claiming one universal ontology. This is mainly P-A/P-B territory.

### Level U2 — Interfield theory

The theory explains relations between parent fields and solves cross-boundary questions.

### Level U3 — Scientific-transition theory

The theory identifies recurring laws or constraints on how claims, representations, evidence, tests, methods and authority may change across different sciences.

### Level U4 — Machine-native implementation theory

The abstract transition theory constrains but does not prescribe machine cognition. Machines may discover nonhuman representations and strategies while producing valid scientific witnesses at the interface.

### Level U5 — Empirical field science

The proposed laws survive strongest-parent comparisons and naturalistic tests across materially different sciences, with measurable improvement and explicit falsifiers.

The Machine Epistemics field claim requires evidence at U3–U5. U1–U2 alone may still be valuable but do not establish the full field.

## 5. Upward synthesis obligations

A candidate higher theory must satisfy all seven obligations.

### A1 — Parent fidelity

Registered native parent decisions remain recoverable unless explicit counterevidence justifies revision.

### A2 — Non-reductive option

The higher theory may preserve several incomparable or partially overlapping parent models. Unification is not mandatory when pluralism changes the correct decision.

### A3 — Cross-parent residual

At least one protected problem genuinely requires relations among parents rather than one parent in isolation.

### A4 — Generativity

The synthesis yields prospective predictions, experiments, theorems, failures or methods not inserted manually after outcomes are known.

### A5 — Scientific dominance

Under matched information, tools and resources, the higher theory improves a registered scientific-quality or efficiency coordinate without critical regressions.

### A6 — Recursive extensibility

A new parent field or source mode can be absorbed through explicit relations and revalidation rather than forcing silent rewriting of the whole theory.

### A7 — Self-contraction

When a parent or parent product is sufficient, the higher theory must defer locally. A superior theory is stronger partly because it knows where not to replace specialised science.

## 6. Candidate higher-order object

The first candidate is not one giant ontology. It is a federated object:

`AbsorptiveTheory = (Parents, NativeViews, Bridges, TransitionLaws, Tests, RecoveryMaps, Costs, Falsifiers, AuthorityLimits)`.

Where:

- `Parents` preserves ownership and native identities;
- `NativeViews` preserves domain-specific state and evidence semantics;
- `Bridges` relate parent views under registered contexts;
- `TransitionLaws` constrain cross-view scientific change;
- `Tests` distinguish valid bridges from false analogies;
- `RecoveryMaps` return results to native fields;
- `Costs` expose implementation and cognitive burden;
- `Falsifiers` permit field/theory contraction;
- `AuthorityLimits` prevent epistemic integration from minting institutional permission.

## 7. Candidate cross-parent theory questions

These are research questions, not yet foundation laws:

1. Which scientific-state variables must be observable for justified cross-field transition?
2. What is conserved when evidence, representation, model, evaluator or problem frame changes?
3. Which parent relations compose, and which lose meaning or authority through composition?
4. How should dependence, test sensitivity and assurance arguments combine?
5. When does local specialised control suffice, and when is an interfield representation necessary?
6. How can a system remain plural while coordinating action and closure?
7. How should machine-native reasoning expose enough boundary evidence without imitating human internal process?
8. What makes an integrated programme progressive rather than a growing collection of ad hoc adapters?

## 8. Prohibited interpretations

This principle rejects:

- `nearest_parent_found -> stop thinking`;
- `many_parent_citations -> new field established`;
- `common vocabulary -> genuine unification`;
- `shorter description -> truer science`;
- `one universal representation -> superior theory`;
- `human reasoning -> normative architecture`;
- `machine difference -> automatic superiority`;
- `broader scope -> permission to overrule domain experts`;
- `integration -> institutional authority`.

## 9. Paper consequences

### Flagship

The field paper should not present parent overlap as an embarrassment. It should present Machine Epistemics as an attempt to build an absorptive higher-order theory of scientific transitions, with parent composition as its strongest lower bound and falsifier.

### P-A/P-B

These papers supply native reconstruction, embeddings, recovery maps, bridge relations and counter-probes.

### P-C/P-D

These papers test whether transition and assurance laws improve decisions beyond parent control/evidence products.

### P-E

Opportunity discovery tests the generative capacity of the theory.

### P-F

Machine-native inquiry tests whether the theory supports scientifically valid cognition that is not limited to human process imitation.

No new paper identity is earned merely by adopting the word “supertheory”.
