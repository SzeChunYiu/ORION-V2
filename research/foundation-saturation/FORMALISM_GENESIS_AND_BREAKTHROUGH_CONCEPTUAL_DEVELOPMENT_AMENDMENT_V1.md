# Formalism Genesis and Breakthrough Conceptual Development Amendment V1
## Detecting when the current language is too weak, and constructing a better one

**Framework role:** candidate extension of Conceptual Development / Transfer Discovery.  
**Scientificity:** S1 operational interfaces + S2 discriminating mechanism hypotheses.  
**Authority:** non-authorizing. Historical breakthrough patterns are donors, not universal laws of discovery.

## 1. Core problem

Transfer Discovery assumes that at least one useful representation already exists. Some frontier problems are harder: every available representation may systematically hide a distinction, fail to close under a required operation, make a necessary object undefined, or require repeated ad-hoc patches.

The scientific question is therefore:

> Can a machine detect a registered representational deficit, construct a minimal new formal language that repairs it, recover the valid predecessor theory, and derive prospectively useful consequences that strongest existing parents cannot obtain at equal or lower cost?

This is **formalism genesis**. It is not symbol invention. New notation without semantic, inferential or decision residual receives zero credit.

## 2. What recurring historical cases suggest

The cases below motivate tests; none is encoded as a discovery law.

### 2.1 From solving objects to studying transformations and structure

Algebra evolved from solving equations toward studying fields, automorphisms and groups. The history of Galois theory is particularly clear: later formulations shifted attention from constructing roots to structural relations between fields and automorphism groups. Group theory itself arose by abstracting common structure from geometry, number theory and permutations rather than from one isolated source.

**Candidate discriminator:** repeated computational dead ends become tractable after changing the object of study from instances to transformations, invariants or relations among instances.

### 2.2 Counterexamples refine both theorem and concept

Lakatos-style proof analysis distinguishes counterexamples to a conclusion from counterexamples to particular proof premises. A counterexample can force a missing condition into the theorem or motivate a revised concept boundary.

**Candidate discriminator:** a new primitive/scope condition is justified only if it separates previously aliased positive and negative cases and survives new hidden counterexamples.

### 2.3 Useful informal objects can precede their rigorous formalism

Dirac's delta calculus worked operationally before it had a rigorous mathematical home. Schwartz's theory of distributions enlarged the notion of function so such operations could be represented consistently and used far more broadly.

**Candidate discriminator:** a repeated, productive but formally illegal operation may signal that the object domain is too narrow; a generalized object is justified only if it restores lawful operations while recovering ordinary cases.

### 2.4 Notation and representation can change the reachable search space

Leibniz's differential/integral notation exposed reusable operator structure and scaled better to later multivariable development than rival notation. Notation is therefore not cosmetic: it can change which transformations and compositions are easy to express.

**Candidate discriminator:** compare equivalent semantics under alternative representations and measure proof/search complexity, transfer and error—not aesthetic preference.

### 2.5 New mathematics can become the missing language of physics

Einstein's equivalence principle created pressure that Euclidean/linear descriptions could not satisfy globally. Riemannian geometry and the Ricci–Levi-Civita tensor calculus supplied a language in which the gravitational field could be represented geometrically. The crucial move was not “use tensors everywhere”; it was recognizing that the current representational assumptions could not express the physical constraints.

**Candidate discriminator:** a physical/scientific constraint set has no faithful representation in the active formalism but becomes jointly expressible under a new structure whose old-regime limit recovers prior results.

### 2.6 Rival formalisms can reveal a higher common structure

Matrix mechanics and wave mechanics appeared different before their relation was clarified; von Neumann's Hilbert-space treatment provided a rigorous common setting. In QFT, Feynman's and Schwinger/Tomonaga's different methods were shown by Dyson to be equivalent for the relevant theory.

**Candidate discriminator:** apparently incompatible successful representations may motivate a higher abstraction only when an explicit equivalence/recovery map exists.

### 2.7 Pathologies can trigger reinterpretation rather than immediate rejection

QFT divergences were initially a profound defect. Renormalization and later effective-field-theory reasoning changed how parameters, scale and validity regimes were understood. The important lesson is not “renormalize every theory”; it is that repeated pathology can reveal a missing scale/representation distinction.

**Candidate discriminator:** a pathology is productive only if the revised formalism yields new finite/valid predictions, preserves successful old predictions and states its scope.

### 2.8 New formalisms can fail

Von Neumann explored continuous geometries partly as a broader framework for quantum theory, but the hoped-for generalization did not survive the required structure. A proposed new formalism can therefore collapse back into an existing parent or add no residual.

**Framework rule:** attempted generalization is a hypothesis, not evidence of progress.

## 3. The recurring breakthrough pattern — as a testable hypothesis, not a law

A useful provisional sequence is:

`problem pressure`
`-> registered representational deficit`
`-> residual/counterexample collection`
`-> object-of-study shift or primitive invention`
`-> relations/operations and invariants`
`-> axioms/calculus/proof rules`
`-> semantics/models`
`-> predecessor recovery`
`-> hidden generativity`
`-> equivalence/transfer`
`-> stabilization or rejection`.

Different discoveries may skip, reorder or reject stages. V2 must test which stages causally matter.

## 4. Formal trigger: representational collision

Let `q_F(x)` be the description of case `x` under current formalism `F`, and `J(x)` the registered valid decision. Define

\[
\mathcal C_F=\{(i,j):q_F(x_i)=q_F(x_j)\land J(x_i)\neq J(x_j)\}.
\]

A non-empty collision set means the representation is too coarse **for that registered decision family**. It does not prove the need for a wholly new theory; a missing variable, parent formalism or simpler patch may suffice.

Given candidate distinctions `Z`, define an extended representation `q_{F+Z}`. A finite reference oracle searches for the smallest `Z` such that

\[
\mathcal C_{F+Z}=\varnothing
\]

on the development cases, followed by hidden-case tests. This becomes an exact baseline for primitive invention.

## 5. Other formal-deficit signatures

These are diagnostic classes, not universal causes:

1. **unexpressible obligation** — a scientifically necessary distinction cannot be represented;
2. **non-closure** — a repeatedly required operation leaves the current object class;
3. **non-compositionality** — locally valid maps compose into an invalid global map;
4. **representation collision** — same formal state, different valid decision;
5. **path dependence loss** — identical endpoint state hides materially different histories;
6. **equivalence confusion** — multiple representations are treated as distinct evidence or one representation is treated as universally privileged;
7. **local-to-global obstruction** — all local pieces look valid but no global object exists;
8. **pathology concentration** — divergences/singularities/undefined cases cluster around a missing structural distinction;
9. **patch proliferation** — many ad-hoc exceptions suggest an absent abstraction;
10. **proof-search explosion** — a representation is semantically valid but creates unnecessary derivational complexity.

Each signature must be compared against the strongest existing parent explanation before formalism invention is credited.

## 6. Candidate formalism object

Represent a proposed formalism as

\[
\mathfrak F=(\Sigma,\mathcal T,\mathcal R,\mathcal O,\mathcal A,\mathcal D,\mathcal M,\mathcal E,\mathcal K),
\]

where:

- `Sigma` — symbols/primitives;
- `T` — types/sorts;
- `R` — relations;
- `O` — operations/composition laws;
- `A` — axioms/constraints;
- `D` — derivation/proof/calculation rules;
- `M` — semantic models/interpretations;
- `E` — embeddings/recovery/equivalence maps to predecessor formalisms;
- `K` — provenance/authority/scope constraints.

A candidate is not admitted because `Sigma` is novel. At minimum it needs semantic realization, consistency/model checks appropriate to its logic, predecessor recovery where required, and prospectively useful derivations.

## 7. Formalism value is non-compensatory

For predecessor `F` and candidate `F'`, report

\[
\Gamma(F'\mid F)=
(-|\mathcal C_{F'}|,E_{expr},R_{old},C_{cons},M_{sem},G_{new},H_{hidden},-C_{resource}),
\]

where the coordinates are remaining representational collisions, expressibility, old-case retention, consistency/model status, semantic-model adequacy, prospective generativity, hidden-problem success and cost.

Critical inconsistency, semantic failure or predecessor corruption cannot be compensated by compression or novelty.

## 8. How a machine may construct a new formalism

### FG-A — freeze the deficit

Before invention, record the cases/obligations the current parents cannot jointly resolve. Do not allow a new language to redefine the failure after outcomes.

### FG-B — search existing mathematics and science first

Search strongest parent formalisms, including structures the current team did not anticipate. `PARENT_SUFFICIENT` is a successful terminal.

### FG-C — generate candidate distinctions

Candidate primitives may be induced from collision-separating features, recurring counterexamples, transformations, invariants, hidden state/history, scale/regime coordinates, generalized objects, or newly useful quotient/equivalence relations.

No candidate source is privileged.

### FG-D — synthesize relations and operations

A primitive becomes scientifically useful only through constraints and operations. Search for closure, composition, inverse/adjoint/dual structure, order, topology, algebraic operations or other native relations only when justified by the cases.

### FG-E — axiom/rule synthesis

Infer a minimal constraint/rule set that admits positive models and rejects registered countermodels. Compare theorem synthesis, SAT/SMT/model-finding, inductive logic/program synthesis and strongest domain-native methods.

### FG-F — bind semantics

Every formal object must have at least one interpretation/model or a clearly stated syntactic role. Formal consistency alone does not establish empirical meaning.

### FG-G — recover predecessors

Where the candidate generalizes an earlier valid formalism, require an embedding/projection/limit/equivalence relation that recovers the old judgments in their scope.

### FG-H — demand generativity

Freeze at least one consequence, proof obligation, prediction, representation simplification or decision **before** hidden outcome access. Retrospective elegance is insufficient.

### FG-I — seek counterexamples and simpler explanations

Run obstruction search, independence checks, alternative axiomatizations and simpler-patch controls. If one added variable solves the problem, do not promote a large new formalism.

### FG-J — stabilize only after transfer

A formalism that solves one constructed family may remain a local device. Broader promotion requires a second independent problem/domain or a formal universality result appropriate to the claim.

## 9. Interaction with existing V2 components

No new `K7` is added.

- **K0 / ProblemFrame:** formalism identity/version becomes part of the frame; changing formal language creates a new comparison identity.
- **K1 / Knowledge Forms:** store primitives, axioms, models, proof rules and failed formalism attempts.
- **K2 / Relations:** store embeddings, equivalences, functors, approximations and obstruction witnesses.
- **K4 / Self-model:** estimate whether failure is likely local computation versus representation inadequacy.
- **K5 / Surprise:** repeated anomaly can trigger formalism search but supplies zero confirmation by itself.
- **K6 / Saturation:** search mathematical/formal parents before claiming a new language.
- **Knowledge Metabolism:** decompose failed formalisms and recycle useful primitives/counterexamples.
- **Conceptual Development:** formalism change is a special high-burden conceptual transition.
- **Adaptive Revision #47:** post-result formalism invention is a successor hypothesis, never a retroactive rescue.

## 10. Scientific kill conditions

Formalism Genesis contracts or fails if:

- an existing parent formalism resolves the deficit;
- a simpler extra variable or local patch resolves the collision;
- new symbols have no semantic model;
- the system becomes inconsistent or overgenerates counterexamples;
- predecessor-valid cases are lost;
- hidden problems do not improve;
- the formalism only compresses already-known cases without new consequence;
- gains vanish under notation/permutation changes;
- proof/search gains are merely extra compute;
- the mechanism only reproduces historically famous formalism inventions;
- no independent second problem/domain supports the claimed scope.

Valid terminals include `PARENT_SUFFICIENT`, `LOCAL_PATCH_SUFFICIENT`, `NO_GENERATIVE_RESIDUAL`, `PROTECTED_FORMALISM_RESIDUAL`, `CONTEXTUAL_FORMALISM`, `MERGE_INTO_P_A/P_B/P_C`, and `CANNOT_CHECK`.

## 11. Paper role

This amendment does not create a new paper. Formalism Genesis should initially strengthen:

- **P-A:** discovering remote structural/formal parents and missing distinctions;
- **P-B:** relation/equivalence/obstruction and conservative formal transport;
- **P-C:** deciding when representation change is worth the cost;
- **Flagship:** conceptual development as changing not only beliefs but the language in which scientific states are expressible.

A standalone paper requires an independent protected residual beyond those manuscripts and beyond strongest formal parents.
