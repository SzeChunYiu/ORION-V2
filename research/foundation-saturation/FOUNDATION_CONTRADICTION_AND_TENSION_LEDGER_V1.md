# Foundation Contradiction and Tension Ledger V1

**Canonical owner:** issue #41  
**Pass-H additions:** issue #42

**Status:** initial contradiction ledger. Tensions must be resolved by context, formal relation, protected evidence or explicit pluralism—not averaged away.

## T1 — hypothesis-led inquiry versus exploratory experimentation

- Fixed hypotheses/obligations enable efficient discrimination.
- Exploratory work can precede stable hypotheses and help create concepts or phenomena.
- False resolution: narrate every exploration as a test after the fact.
- Candidate reconciliation: typed epistemic action modes; exploration opens obligations but carries no confirmatory authority.

## T2 — frozen criteria versus legitimate epistemic iteration

- Frozen criteria prevent outcome-conditioned gaming.
- Standards, instruments and concepts can improve iteratively.
- False resolution: prohibit all change or silently reuse the old comparison identity.
- Candidate reconciliation: immutable comparison identity plus explicit calibration iteration and cross-epoch revalidation.

## T3 — universal schema versus epistemic cultures

- Executable research requires shared machine interfaces.
- Local practices use different meanings, evidence and authority structures.
- False resolution: shared label means shared semantics; or no coordination is possible.
- Candidate reconciliation: minimal common invariants, local projections, translation loss and prohibited cross-projection inferences.

## T4 — human-readable process versus machine-native computation

- Scientific decisions need evidence and auditability.
- Useful computation may occur through latent, programmatic, graph or parallel processes unlike human reasoning traces.
- False resolution: human narration defines correctness; or opacity/novelty defines intelligence.
- Candidate reconciliation: internal freedom plus claim-sufficient external scientific witness.

## T5 — exploration versus bounded closure

- Finite resources require conservative stopping.
- new sources, representations and surprises can remain valuable after local flatness.
- False resolution: never stop; or route stability equals global saturation.
- Candidate reconciliation: declared-universe closure, exploration budgets and explicit reopening triggers.

## T6 — internal coherence versus empirical support

- proof, simulation, counterfactuals and self-critique can improve inquiry.
- they remain conditional on specifications, models and shared assumptions.
- False resolution: model-generated state becomes world observation.
- Candidate reconciliation: typed formal/proposal states and external validation obligations.

## T7 — universal intelligence method versus inductive bias

- a foundation seeks transferable principles.
- learning/search performance depends on representation, prior, problem distribution and resources.
- False resolution: one human or machine method is neutral.
- Candidate reconciliation: inductive-bias declarations and cross-stratum resource fronts.

## T8 — evidence versus values and authority

- evidence can estimate consequences and uncertainty.
- objectives, trade-offs, consent and legitimate rights are not determined by evidence alone.
- False resolution: confidence or utility self-grants authority.
- Candidate reconciliation: support, recommendation, values and authority remain separately typed.

## T9 — exact replay versus robust replication

- exact replay is essential for custody and debugging.
- scientific robustness often requires changing site, instrument, population or implementation.
- False resolution: replay equals independent replication; or heterogeneous failure globally invalidates the original narrow result.
- Candidate reconciliation: replication modes and explicit transport/reopening.

## T10 — theory priority versus apparatus-mediated phenomenon construction

- theory/model guides observation.
- experimental systems can reveal or stabilize phenomena not anticipated by current theory.
- False resolution: apparatus is neutral; or every artifact is a genuine target phenomenon.
- Candidate reconciliation: experimental-system state, artifact hypotheses and system-to-target transport.

## T11 — minimal kernel versus expressive coverage

- stable architecture should be small and replaceable.
- omitted distinctions can create false completion or unsafe transport.
- False resolution: every donor becomes a primitive; or minimality is declared without removal tests.
- Candidate reconciliation: contextual parent-facing receipts and mandatory component-value interventions.

## T12 — saturation as convergence versus saturation as self-confirmation

- a finite programme needs a stop.
- repeated same-taxonomy search can flatten because all routes share one blind spot.
- False resolution: citation count or local repetition equals saturation.
- Candidate reconciliation: declared universe, source-dependence graph, changed vocabulary, hostile routes and three post-material clean passes.

## T13 — evaluator identity versus evaluator sensitivity

- evaluator custody prevents outcome-conditioned substitution.
- a correctly bound evaluator may be incapable of detecting the error relevant to a broad claim.
- False resolution: evaluator identity is treated as evaluator adequacy.
- Candidate reconciliation: claim-relative test sensitivity/error-class profile.

## T14 — obligation control versus construction of new problem spaces

- obligations make action choice inspectable and prevent drift.
- some inquiry must construct the observables/representations from which future obligations can be stated.
- False resolution: uncontrolled creativity; or current obligations define all legitimate inquiry.
- Candidate reconciliation: bounded exploratory/constructive actions with source, cost and transition receipts.
