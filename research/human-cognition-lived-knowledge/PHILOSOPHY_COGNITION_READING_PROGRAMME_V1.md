# Philosophy, Cognition and Human Practice — Deep-Reading Programme V1

**Status:** reading/reconstruction plan created because the previous donor programme relied too heavily on papers, summaries and formalized disciplines. No book title below is treated as correct by prestige or age; every work must be reconstructed, criticized and reduced against current empirical/formal knowledge.

## Reading rule

For books/monographs, do not extract slogans. For every work produce:

1. native problem;
2. definitions and distinctions in the author's own system;
3. positive mechanism or method;
4. strongest internal argument;
5. historical context and later criticism;
6. modern empirical/formal nearest neighbors;
7. ORION/Machine-Epistemics collision;
8. machine-executable generalization if one survives;
9. hostile case where the idea should *not* apply;
10. disposition: parent-owned / absorbed / contradicted / historically useful / new coordinate / `CANNOT_CHECK`.

## Tier A — direct foundations of reflexive inquiry

### Charles S. Peirce — selected writings on abduction, pragmatism and scientific method

Extract surprise-driven abduction, deduction/induction cycle, fallibilism and economy of research. Compare with Bayesian model discovery, active learning, diagnosis and P-E opportunity discovery.

### John Dewey — *How We Think*; *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry*

Extract reflective inquiry, problematic situations, inquiry into inquiry and the relationship between thought/action/environment. Compare with metareasoning, adaptive control and solver self-modeling.

### Michael Polanyi — *Personal Knowledge*; *The Tacit Dimension*

Extract tacit knowing, personal participation, skill, scientific judgment and community. Test whether `TacitDependency` is operationally useful or merely a warning label.

### Gilbert Ryle — *The Concept of Mind*

Extract knowing-how/knowing-that, anti-intellectualism and intelligent performance. Test against procedural learning and demonstration-based ML.

### Gregory Bateson — *Steps to an Ecology of Mind*

Extract levels of learning, feedback, pattern, context and epistemology/ecology of mind. Reduce against second-order cybernetics, meta-learning and distributed cognition.

### Heinz von Foerster — *Understanding Understanding* / observing-systems essays

Extract second-order cybernetics, self-reference and observer inclusion. Test against performative prediction, evaluator dynamics and self-modeling.

## Tier B — change, failure and pluralism in science

### Karl Popper — *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*; *Conjectures and Refutations*

Extract fallibilism, falsifiability and methodological complications of counterevidence. Compare with ORION falsifiers and plural responsibility.

### Thomas Kuhn — *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*; *The Essential Tension*

Extract normal science, exemplars, anomaly, crisis, paradigm/lexicon change and Kuhn-loss. This is a direct parent threat to Jump, cross-generation transport and exemplar-based scientific competence.

### Imre Lakatos — *The Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes*

Extract programme-level progress/degeneration and protected vs adjustable commitments. Compare with framework generations and longitudinal research-policy state.

### Paul Feyerabend — *Against Method*

Extract limits of universal method and theoretical pluralism. Use as a hostile control against over-formalized single-method V2 governance, not as permission for arbitrary action.

### Helen Longino / scientific pluralism and social epistemology traditions

Extract criticism, background assumptions, social objectivity, plural perspectives and community-level epistemic checks. Compare with dependence-aware evidence and authority without reducing disagreement to voting.

## Tier C — practice, embodiment and the distributed mind

### Jean Lave — *Cognition in Practice*; with Etienne Wenger, *Situated Learning*

Extract cognition in everyday activity, apprenticeship, social participation and transfer limits. Deliberately study arithmetic/shopping and non-academic practices as counterweights to lab-style cognition.

### Edwin Hutchins — *Cognition in the Wild*

Extract distributed computation across people and artifacts. Compare with multi-agent/tool systems and evidence-dependence topology.

### J. J. Gibson — *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception*

Extract affordances and organism-environment action structure. Test an affordance-based action model against generic tool lists.

### Maurice Merleau-Ponty — *Phenomenology of Perception*

Extract embodiment and experienced-world structure. Compare cautiously with embodied cognition and do not translate phenomenology directly into neuroscience claims.

### Donald Schön — *The Reflective Practitioner*

Extract reflection-in-action, professional knowing and response to surprising situations. Compare with runtime solver reflection and repair; distinguish retrospective explanation from online skilled adjustment.

### Lucy Suchman — *Plans and Situated Actions*

Extract limits of plan-centric models and situated interaction. Pressure fixed workflow/planning assumptions.

## Tier D — design, problem solving and artificial intelligence

### Herbert Simon — *The Sciences of the Artificial*

Extract design as a legitimate science, bounded rationality, problem spaces and artificial systems. This is a major parent both for Machine Epistemics as a proposed field and for the solver architecture.

### Newell & Simon — *Human Problem Solving*

Extract problem-space search and production-system accounts, then compare against modern cognition and representation-change results.

### Donald Campbell — evolutionary epistemology / blind variation and selective retention

Extract variation-selection models of creativity while testing modern criticisms of how 'blind' variation actually is.

## Tier E — language, rules, interpretation and social knowledge

### Ludwig Wittgenstein — *Philosophical Investigations*

Extract rule following, forms of life, practice and public criteria. Pressure the assumption that formal instructions determine their own correct application.

### Testimony and social epistemology

Deep-read work on testimony, trust, epistemic dependence and distributed knowledge. Relevant to source authority, expert delegation and the difference between receiving testimony and independently corroborating it.

### Epistemic injustice / standpoint traditions

Study how social structures affect which testimony and knowledge are recognized. Use carefully: the framework-level question is whether source-selection/authority mechanisms systematically erase valid knowledge, not to encode one political theory as constitution.

## Tier F — empirical science of thinking

Deep-read modern reviews/primary literatures on:

- metacognition and confidence calibration;
- meta-reasoning and effort allocation;
- performance/error monitoring;
- prediction error and structural learning;
- insight/restructuring;
- curiosity and active sampling;
- causal learning/intervention;
- memory reconsolidation/forgetting;
- cognitive control and task representation;
- embodied cognition;
- social learning;
- creativity and analogy.

For every philosophical claim with an empirical interpretation, seek modern evidence/counterevidence rather than treating the philosopher as an experimental authority.

## Tier G — lived and ordinary knowledge sources

Run deliberate donor studies outside books/papers:

1. cooking recipes + expert cooking demonstrations;
2. mechanical/household repair manuals + troubleshooting forums;
3. craft apprenticeship (pottery, woodworking, tailoring or equivalent);
4. field guides and observational naturalist practice;
5. incident/accident reports;
6. laboratory notebooks and historical discovery notes when available;
7. aviation/medical/safety checklists;
8. open-source issue/debugging histories;
9. oral-history or expert-interview material;
10. everyday estimation/navigation/shopping/planning practices.

For each, ask what the formal text omits that competent action supplies.
