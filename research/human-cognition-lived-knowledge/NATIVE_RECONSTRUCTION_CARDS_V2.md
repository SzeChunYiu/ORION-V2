## V2-A — Gregory Bateson: learning levels, context and category error

**Evidence:** P2 through the book contents and scholarly reconstruction of *The Logical Categories of Learning and Communication*; primary full-essay reconstruction remains open.

### Native problem

Bateson tries to distinguish changes **within** a given corrective system from changes in the premises, contexts or classes that organize those corrections. His hierarchy of learning is built from logical-type distinctions: a correction of one response is not the same kind of event as learning which class of response or context applies.

### Distinctions to preserve

- fixed response/correction without system change;
- change in response selection inside a stable context;
- change in the way contexts or alternatives are classified;
- change in the higher-order premises by which those classifications are learned;
- message versus meta-message that determines how the message is to be interpreted.

### ORION collision

A machine can report “learning” because its output changed while leaving the underlying problem frame, representation and action family fixed. Conversely, a representation or criterion may change while the system reports an ordinary parameter update. These are different transition identities.

### Executable reduction

Add a research-only `LearningLevelReceipt`:

`(object_changed, context_changed, classification_rule_changed, criterion_changed, evidence, epoch)`.

Use it to test type-confusion failures such as:

- parameter repair reported as representation change;
- changed evaluator criterion reported as model improvement;
- changed problem frame reported as success on the original problem;
- a meta-message such as “this is only an example” lost during transfer.

### Hostile non-application

Do not force every learning algorithm into Bateson's hierarchy. The hierarchy is a diagnostic parent for transition-type confusion, not a universal neuroscience or ML ontology.

### Disposition

**Parent-owned; absorb into K0/K2/K4/K6 identity and change-level tests. No new kernel coordinate.**

## V2-B — Heinz von Foerster: the observer inside the observing system

**Evidence:** P1 for “Ethics and Second-Order Cybernetics” (1990 address; English text published/reprinted in 1995); full *Understanding Understanding* reconstruction remains open.

### Native problem

Von Foerster distinguishes an observer imagined as external to a system from a participant whose observing, describing and acting occur inside recursive relations. A theory of observing must account for the observer's own activity rather than deleting it in the name of objectivity.

### Distinctions to preserve

- first-order description of an observed system;
- second-order description of observing/participation;
- decidable questions under a fixed framework;
- choices about frameworks or other underdetermined questions;
- responsibility for choices versus hiding responsibility behind hierarchy or alleged necessity.

### ORION collision

The framework already models performative evaluators, but this donor generalizes the issue: a probe, representation, benchmark, publication or self-model may change the system and the future observations from which it learns. Evaluator neutrality is an assumption, not a default.

### Executable reduction

`ObserverCouplingReceipt = (observer, observed_system, observation_or_action, predicted_coupling, observed_change, self_model_delta, criterion, authority)`.

Known-answer cases should distinguish:

- passive observation under a validated stable regime;
- measurement intervention changing the target state;
- publication changing model/author behaviour;
- self-monitoring changing allocation policy;
- circular self-validation with no external root.

### Ethical boundary

Von Foerster's responsibility argument is a philosophical parent, not a machine authorization rule. ORION can expose choice, affected parties, authority and consequences; it cannot derive legitimate social authority from recursion or from the imperative to increase options.

### Disposition

**Absorb into K3/K4/K6 observer/evaluator coupling and non-self-authentication. Parent-owned.**

## V2-C — Herbert Simon: near-decomposability and the sciences of the artificial

**Evidence:** P1 for “The Architecture of Complexity” (1962 reprint); P2 for *The Sciences of the Artificial*; full-book reread remains open.

### Native problem

Simon asks how complex systems can be understood and designed when they contain structured subsystems and cross-cutting interactions. Near-decomposability is an empirical/approximate claim: interactions within subsystems may be stronger or faster than interactions among them, permitting useful partial analysis without asserting complete independence.

### Distinctions to preserve

- decomposable versus nearly decomposable versus strongly coupled;
- descriptive science of natural systems versus design science concerned with artificial systems and goals;
- local subsystem optimization versus global system consequence;
- bounded rationality under practical resource limits.

### ORION collision

ORION's decomposition and workflow machinery can become overconfident. Splitting a research problem is valid only if the ignored cross-couplings do not change the registered scientific decision.

### Executable reduction

Every decomposition proposal should bind:

`DecompositionReceipt = (parts, interface_variables, ignored_couplings, coupling_tests, local_decisions, global_decision, error_bound, expiry)`.

Hostile cases:

- apparently modular evidence with one decisive global constraint;
- local green tests that conflict after composition;
- a coupled human–instrument–model state lost at handoff;
- a nearly decomposable regime that ceases to be so after environment change.

### Disposition

**Direct parent of K4 decomposition and K2 composition. Strengthen coupling tests; no new component.**

## V2-D — Newell and Simon: problem spaces, heuristic search and resource-bounded next action

**Evidence:** P1 for “Computer Science as Empirical Inquiry: Symbols and Search” (1976); full *Human Problem Solving* reconstruction remains open.

### Native problem

Newell and Simon describe problem solving through a problem space, move generators and tests under severe resource limits. Their paper explicitly identifies synthesis of the right problem space and generators as an open frontier rather than assuming the representation is given once and for all.

### Distinctions to preserve

- problem-defining test versus generator of candidate solutions;
- problem space versus search policy inside that space;
- weak heuristic search versus recognition/knowledge that reduces search;
- resource-bounded choice of what to do next;
- empirical hypothesis about intelligent architecture versus theorem.

### ORION collision

P-C cannot claim generic problem-space search, heuristic action selection or resource-bounded control. Its residual, if any, lies in scientific obligations, evidence/authority gates, explicit representation insufficiency and preservation/reopening when the problem space changes.

### Executable reduction

Add baseline arms that receive:

- the same problem space and move generators;
- a stronger parent problem-space synthesis method;
- recognition/case-based shortcuts;
- an ORION obligation controller.

A claimed ORION gain that vanishes when the parent receives the same representation is not a solver innovation.

### Disposition

**Major parent threat to P-C and P-E; retain as strongest baseline, not a kernel primitive.**

## V2-E — J. J. Gibson: affordances as actor–environment relations

**Evidence:** P1 excerpt from “The Theory of Affordances” in *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception* (1979); full-book reconstruction remains open.

### Native problem

Gibson defines affordances through the complementarity of animal and environment. What an environment offers cannot be read from an object alone or from an agent alone; it depends on their relation.

### Distinctions to preserve

- abstractly conceivable action;
- physically available action in the current environment;
- action available to this actor's capabilities;
- scientifically admissible action;
- authorized action.

### ORION collision

Tool lists and action schemas can contain actions that the current system cannot actually perform because access, calibration, material geometry, competence or authority is missing.

### Executable reduction

`AffordanceReceipt = (state, actor_capability, environment, resources, calibration, safety, authority, feasible_actions, unavailable_reasons)`.

This object should prevent both false execution claims and unnecessary deletion of conceptually useful but currently unavailable actions.

### Hostile non-application

Affordance language should not replace domain physics, safety or capability tests. It is a relational interface for those native checks.

### Disposition

**Absorb into K0/K4 resource/action admissibility. Parent-owned.**

## V2-F — Lave and Wenger: learning through legitimate participation

**Evidence:** P2/P1-authoritative publisher summary and chapter structure for *Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation* (1991); full-book reconstruction remains open.

### Native problem

Learning is treated as participation in sociocultural practice. Mastery involves changing relations among newcomers, experienced practitioners, activities, identities, artifacts and communities—not merely transferring propositions into an isolated learner.

### Distinctions to preserve

- instruction exposure versus legitimate participation;
- peripheral low-risk access versus full responsibility;
- learning a statement versus becoming able to perform in a practice;
- individual state versus community/artifact-supported competence;
- access to practice versus exclusion from the learning trajectory.

### ORION collision

An agent can be evaluated on static answers while lacking the ability to enter, observe, ask, repair and progressively assume responsibility in a real scientific practice. Conversely, a human–AI system may succeed because the community and artifacts carry essential state.

### Executable reduction

Represent apprenticeship episodes through staged competence receipts:

`ParticipationEpisode = (role, allowed_actions, supervision, artifacts, feedback, state_access, demonstrated_range, responsibility_transition)`.

Do not infer full autonomous competence from supervised peripheral success.

### Disposition

**Absorb into K3 distributed cognition, K4 competence acquisition and benchmark design.**

## V2-G — Donald Schön: reflection-in-action and backtalk from materials

**Evidence:** P1 for Schön's 1996 interview “Reflective Conversation with Materials”; P2 for *The Reflective Practitioner* and *Educating the Reflective Practitioner*; full monographs remain open.

### Native problem

Skilled practice often proceeds without an explicit complete model. Surprise during action can trigger reflection-in-action, while drawings, materials, users or other media produce “backtalk” that changes the practitioner's understanding of the problem. Reflection can reframe both means and ends.

### Distinctions to preserve

- smooth skilled action;
- surprise during action;
- reflection-in-action changing further action;
- reflection-on-action after the episode;
- reflection-on-practice identifying recurrent patterns;
- intended effect versus side effect/backtalk.

### ORION collision

A generic `SELF_REFLECT` step is too coarse. The important trigger is a source-bound mismatch between expected and observed consequences, followed by an action or frame change whose effect is tested.

### Executable reduction

Extend `FailureLesson` and encounter tests with:

- expected versus observed material/system response;
- whether reflection changed the subsequent policy;
- whether the change resolved the mismatch;
- whether a new problem was opened by a side effect;
- whether retrospective narration fabricates a rationale not present in the episode.

### Hostile non-application

Reflection is not automatically beneficial. Repeated verbal self-analysis without calibrated change is a dependent output and may waste resources.

### Disposition

**Absorb into K4 failure lessons and K5 encounter/problem-reframing tests. Parent-owned.**

## V2-H — Pedagogical sampling: teaching can suppress exploration

**Evidence:** E/P1-paper for Bonawitz et al., “The double-edged sword of pedagogy: Instruction limits spontaneous exploration and discovery” (Cognition, 2011; DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2010.10.001).

### Native empirical problem

Learners do not treat demonstrations as random samples. A pedagogical demonstration can communicate that the teacher selected an informative or sufficient function. In the reported experiments, preschoolers who received or overheard direct pedagogical instruction explored the toy more narrowly than learners in non-pedagogical or interrupted conditions.

### Structural donation

Training data generated by a teacher, curator, benchmark designer or instruction-following trace is **selected data**. Absence of demonstrated alternatives is not ordinary evidence that no alternatives exist.

Candidate object:

`PedagogicalSampleReceipt = (teacher, learner, selection_intent, demonstrated_items, claimed_scope, hidden_alternatives, exploration_after_instruction, dependence)`.

Candidate failure:

`INSTRUCTION_INDUCED_SEARCH_SUPPRESSION` — the learner treats an instructional sample as exhaustive and stops discovering valid alternatives.

### Hostile non-application

The finding does not imply that instruction is bad. Teaching can be efficient and safety-critical. The machine question is whether the teacher's selection process and claimed completeness are represented.

### Disposition

**Material new benchmark coordinate for P-C/P-E and training-data semantics; still absorbed by K3/K4/K5.**

# Cross-card deductions

## 1. Change level must be typed

Parameter update, policy update, context-classification change, representation change and criterion change cannot share one undifferentiated “learning” status.

## 2. The observer and teacher are causal participants

Observing, measuring, publishing, teaching and reviewing can change the system or learner. Their dependence and selection processes belong in the episode.

## 3. Decomposition and problem spaces are hypotheses

A workflow, problem representation or subsystem boundary is a model that can fail. ORION must compare against parents given the same representation and expose cross-couplings.

## 4. Action availability is relational

Abstract action existence differs from capability-, environment-, safety- and authority-conditioned affordance.

## 5. Learning can be participation

Static answer correctness does not establish competence to operate in a distributed practice or assume full responsibility.

## 6. Reflection requires backtalk and uptake

A reflective system must show the mismatch, changed policy/frame and resulting evidence—not merely produce a fluent critique.

## 7. Instruction can narrow the search universe

Curated demonstrations and benchmarks can suppress discovery of unshown alternatives. Pedagogical sampling must not be treated as i.i.d. evidence.
