# Machine-Native Scientific Intelligence Programme V1

**Status:** material research programme opened from issue #40 and the component-value audit. It explicitly rejects human imitation as the normative target for ORION-V2. It does not claim that any current machine has discovered an optimal way of thinking, that difference from humans implies superiority, or that a new paper/field is already earned.

## 1. Thesis

Human cognition is one historically evolved solution shaped by biological embodiment, finite working memory, social development, language, energy constraints, mortality and cultural transmission. It is an indispensable source of mechanisms, counterexamples, tasks and values. It is not evidence of a global optimum for intelligence.

ORION-V2 should therefore use human philosophy, cognitive science and lived practice in three roles:

1. **donor** — recover useful mechanisms such as metacognitive calibration, error learning, skill, criticism and serendipity;
2. **hostile control** — expose failures such as overconfidence, habit, herding, memory distortion and framing lock-in;
3. **collaboration boundary** — support communication, authority, rights and joint scientific work with people.

It should **not** optimize internal cognition for human likeness. The research target is:

> **machine-native scientific intelligence: strategies, representations, memory systems and control policies that exploit machine substrates while remaining scientifically warranted, externally testable and authority-bounded.**

Human-like reasoning may survive where it is useful. It receives no constitutional privilege.

### 1.1 From human-versus-machine to a wider adaptive design space

Rejecting human mimicry is not enough if the programme simply replaces anthropocentrism with machine-centrism. Human individuals, non-human animals, collectives, cultural systems, evolutionary lineages and artificial systems instantiate different adaptive organizations across different bodies, environments, scales and timescales. They should be treated as **distinct donor families**, not points on one presumed ladder.

The framework therefore adopts an **Epistemic Locality Principle**:

> No observed intelligence, method or adaptive strategy is presumed globally optimal outside the environment, task family, system boundary, substrate/interface, timescale, resource regime and criterion under which its competence was established.

This principle does not define a new universal intelligence scalar. It restricts what can legitimately be inferred from any local success, including human and machine success.

### 1.2 Adaptation is broader than cognition, but not identical to it

Keep the following distinctions explicit:

```text
COGNITION
WITHIN_LIFETIME_LEARNING
DEVELOPMENTAL_ADAPTATION
COLLECTIVE_SOCIAL_ADAPTATION
CULTURAL_CUMULATIVE_ADAPTATION
EVOLUTIONARY_ADAPTATION
MACHINE_ARCHITECTURAL_OR_POLICY_ADAPTATION
```

They can share abstract structures such as variation, retention, feedback, selection, recombination, communication and niche construction without sharing foresight, agency, consciousness or causal mechanism.

In particular:

```text
EVOLUTIONARY_ADAPTATION != COGNITIVE_REASONING
FITNESS != TRUTH
SURVIVAL != SCIENTIFIC_VALIDITY
NATURAL_SUCCESS != NORMATIVE_AUTHORITY
```

Evolution, ecology and comparative cognition provide mechanisms and hostile controls. They do not grant scientific or ethical authority.

## 2. Review cell

1. **AI/algorithmic intelligence researcher** — studies self-play, search, learned models, program synthesis and open-ended learning;
2. **comparative/embodied cognition researcher** — tests anthropomorphic assumptions and substrate/embodiment dependence;
3. **evolution/collective-systems researcher** — distinguishes cognitive, collective, cultural and evolutionary adaptation;
4. **cognitive scientist/philosopher** — prevents both naive anthropomorphism and naive anti-human rhetoric;
5. **scientific-method/metascience reviewer** — keeps machine difference subordinate to evidence and domain semantics;
6. **systems/efficiency researcher** — measures compute, memory, latency, parallelism and failure surface;
7. **human–AI collaboration and governance reviewer** — separates internal machine cognition from public explanation and legitimate authority;
8. **hostile evaluator** — asks whether a “machine-native” result is only brute force, opacity, hidden human data, benchmark leakage, random novelty or a local benchmark overgeneralized as global intelligence.

## 3. Machine-native does not mean human-free

A system can be machine-native while using human knowledge. The distinction concerns the **optimization target and internal constraints**, not the total absence of human inputs.

A machine-native system may:

- read human papers and books;
- learn from demonstrations;
- communicate in natural language;
- use human-designed tools and scientific criteria;
- ask experts for evidence or authority.

It is not required to:

- reproduce a human verbal chain of thought;
- maintain one narrative viewpoint;
- use human working-memory limits;
- collapse to one belief or representation for psychological comfort;
- explore through human motor/sensory constraints;
- inherit human heuristics when formal/search alternatives are stronger;
- simulate emotion, consciousness or personality without a decision-relevant reason.

Nor should the system assume that one non-human donor is a global optimum. Octopus-like distributed control, animal collective decision, evolutionary variation/selection, cultural accumulation and machine search can each motivate candidate mechanisms while remaining native to different causal organizations.

## 4. Candidate machine-native affordances

These are hypotheses, not guaranteed advantages.

### 4.1 Persistent exact external memory

Machines can preserve immutable receipts, replay exact prior states and maintain large negative-history stores. This can outperform reconstructive biological memory, but only if retrieval, relevance and stale-policy management are solved.

### 4.2 Parallel plural inquiry

A machine can retain many incompatible models, proof attempts, simulations and search routes simultaneously rather than forcing early narrative coherence. Dependence and resource coupling still need representation.

### 4.3 Non-linguistic/high-dimensional representation

Internal states may use vectors, programs, graphs, proofs, causal models, automata or hybrid structures that have no faithful natural-language equivalent. Scientific decisions still need recoverable witnesses at the interface where claims are made.

### 4.4 Variable and heterogeneous computation

A machine can allocate different compute to different obligations, invoke specialized parent algorithms and compose exact symbolic checks with learned models. More compute is not automatically better; expected value and opportunity cost remain explicit.

### 4.5 Self-play, synthetic worlds and counterfactual generation

Machines can generate training curricula, adversaries and simulations beyond recorded human examples. Synthetic experience cannot silently become evidence about the real world.

### 4.6 Search-space construction

A system can propose new variables, representations, abstractions, decompositions and tools. The change is accepted only when it unlocks protected obligations and preserves or reopens prior commitments correctly.

### 4.7 Distributed non-unitary cognition

The scientific unit can be a changing system of models, tools, databases, theorem provers, instruments and people. There need not be one metaphysical or computational “self”; the framework tracks state, dependence and authority where they actually reside.

Comparative and collective systems make the same warning in a different way: the relevant adaptive unit can be partly distributed or can change with organizational scale. ORION therefore treats system boundary as a scientific variable when it changes a protected conclusion rather than assuming that one agent is always the correct unit of analysis.

### 4.8 Machine-time scales

Machines may conduct millions of cheap internal tests, maintain long-lived programmes or revisit dormant opportunities. Long duration or volume does not establish saturation; route diversity and external evidence still matter.

### 4.9 Perspective and scale change

A machine can explicitly ask whether the current boundary, organizational scale, temporal scale, environment reference or evaluator frame is hiding a relevant regularity.

Candidate working object:

```text
PerspectiveFrame = (
    system_boundary,
    agent_or_organization_scale,
    spatial_scale,
    temporal_scale,
    environment,
    representation,
    objective_or_question,
    observer_or_intervention_position,
    evaluation_frame
)
```

A perspective change receives no credit because it is broader or more “holistic.” It is useful only if it exposes a discriminator, resolves a blocker, prevents a false universalization or establishes a lawful cross-frame relation.

## 5. Difference is not intelligence

The programme rejects seven weak arguments:

- `different_from_human -> intelligent`;
- `incomprehensible -> profound`;
- `large_search -> discovery`;
- `unseen_in_training_data -> novel_science`;
- `superhuman_benchmark_score -> warranted_scientific_claim`;
- `survived_or_evolved -> epistemically_true`;
- `wins_in_one_ecology -> globally_superior`.

A machine-native strategy must satisfy at least:

1. a frozen task/criterion and source identity;
2. a declared environment/reference distribution, boundary, scale, timescale and resource regime when material;
3. protected outcome quality or justified reach;
4. strongest algorithmic and human-mimetic baselines under matched resources;
5. evidence that the benefit depends on the claimed machine-native mechanism;
6. transfer or replication beyond one authored case;
7. interface-level explanations/witnesses sufficient for the scientific decision;
8. no critical authority, evidence or preservation regression;
9. an honest parent-owned/negative terminal.

## 7. Benchmark architecture

### 7.1 Arms

1. **human-mimetic agent** — natural-language reasoning, role prompts, reflection and human demonstrations;
2. **human heuristic system** — explicit expert rules/strategies;
3. **strongest native parent algorithm** — search, theorem proving, Bayesian design, program synthesis, RL or domain solver;
4. **machine-native system** — no requirement to imitate human process; optimized for protected scientific criteria;
5. **hybrid human–machine system** — machine-native internal work with human evidence/authority interfaces;
6. **simple/direct control**.

No arm receives hidden extra data, compute, tools or evaluator access.

### 7.2 Task strata

- human heuristic is strong and should not be discarded;
- human heuristic trap with a known better algorithmic strategy;
- high-dimensional state inaccessible to unaided human working memory;
- extremely long evidence/provenance chain;
- many simultaneous live hypotheses;
- non-linguistic or permuted representation;
- exact proof/verification combined with uncertain empirical evidence;
- novel action space or tool composition;
- self-play/synthetic curriculum with real-world validation gate;
- collaboration task where human authority and contextual knowledge are indispensable;
- context reversal where the best method changes with environment/resource regime;
- scale reversal where an individual-level and collective/system-level analysis support different warranted decisions;
- invalid-comparison cases where criteria or evidence interfaces differ enough that a global ranking should be refused.

### 7.3 Evidence of genuinely different strategy

Human-process distance is a diagnostic, not the endpoint. Evidence can include:

- solution/strategy not present in the human demonstration corpus after leakage audit;
- stable performance after removal of human traces while retaining task rules/evidence;
- better performance in representation permutations that preserve task semantics but disrupt human semantic heuristics;
- complementary error profile relative to experts;
- high-value parallel portfolio or memory use demonstrated by ablation;
- new representation/tool composition whose removal destroys the protected gain;
- independent experts verify the result while reporting that the path is nonstandard;
- transfer to a second domain where the same machine-native control mechanism survives;
- correct contraction of a claimed universal strategy when the environment, scale or criterion changes.

### 7.4 Primary outcomes

- justified terminal rate;
- critical-failure rate;
- scientific quality or proof/known-answer accuracy;
- calibrated uncertainty and review-trigger value;
- safe transport and preservation;
- cost per justified terminal;
- strategy diversity and complementarity with humans;
- cross-domain transfer;
- false-universalization rate across context changes;
- perspective-dependence detection when registered;
- novelty contraction and source fidelity;
- human collaboration quality at the interface.

Human-likeness is never a primary optimization objective.

## 8. Component-value requirement

Every proposed machine-native mechanism must pass the ORION component protocol:

- `FULL` versus `MINUS-component`;
- strongest parent replacement;
- human-mimetic and simple controls;
- pair interaction/coalition audit;
- latency, compute, memory, annotation and implementation cost;
- hostile cases where the mechanism should remain inactive;
- cross-domain and cross-ecology revalidation.

A mechanism that only adds opacity, compute or richer context metadata becomes `REDUNDANT_DRAG` or `HARMFUL`, not “emergent intelligence”.

## 9. Research hypotheses

### MN-H1 — Machine-native memory

Immutable archive + adaptive retrieval + typed failure replay improves long-horizon scientific control beyond human-like summarization and ordinary retrieval, without history laundering.

### MN-H2 — Parallel pluralism

Maintaining incompatible models and targeted discriminators reduces premature closure at acceptable compute cost compared with single-narrative agents.

### MN-H3 — Non-language internal representation

A graph/program/model-based internal representation outperforms natural-language-only reasoning on selected scientific decisions while producing sufficient external witnesses.

### MN-H4 — Search-space learning

Explicit representation/tool proposal actions improve protected reach only on witnessed insufficiency cases and do not increase false Jumps on simple controls.

### MN-H5 — Synthetic experience with evidence separation

Self-play/simulation improves policy or experiment design, while real-world claims remain gated by external observations.

### MN-H6 — Machine-native/human complementary teams

Hybrid systems outperform both human-mimetic machines and unaided machine-native systems when human context/authority and machine search/memory are genuinely complementary.

### MN-H7 — Non-anthropomorphic self-model

A process-level reliability model can improve resource/review decisions without constructing or claiming a unitary human-like self.

### MN-H8 — Epistemic locality

Explicitly binding environment, boundary, scale, timescale, resources and criterion reduces false universalization or wrong method routing beyond ordinary context-conditioned controls at acceptable cost.

### MN-H9 — Perspective dependence

When a registered scientific decision genuinely depends on system boundary or scale, a perspective-aware controller detects and reports the dependence more reliably than a single-frame controller without manufacturing relativism on invariant cases.

## 10. Parent threats

The programme is heavily parent-owned by:

- universal/general intelligence definitions;
- comparative and embodied cognition;
- ecological rationality and algorithm selection;
- collective behavior/intelligence and distributed cognition;
- major evolutionary transitions and cultural evolution;
- general game playing and AlphaZero-style self-play;
- MuZero/world-model planning;
- theorem proving, program synthesis and automated reasoning;
- meta-learning and neural architecture/search-space learning;
- open-ended learning and quality diversity;
- automated curriculum generation;
- multi-agent/distributed AI;
- mechanistic interpretability and causal component attribution;
- human–AI complementarity and mixed-initiative systems.

AlphaZero is evidence that systems can exceed human-expert programmes without learning from human game records, and MuZero shows planning with a learned task-relevant model; neither establishes general scientific intelligence. Comparative cognition and evolutionary/collective systems are likewise donor families and hostile controls, not proof that one general intelligence law has been found.

## 11. Possible paper identity

A standalone paper is **contingent**, not added to the core portfolio by enthusiasm.

Working title:

> **Machine-native inquiry: measuring scientific intelligence beyond human imitation**

It survives as P-F only if protected experiments show a reproducible benefit specifically attributable to a non-human-mimetic representation, search, memory, composition or locality-aware mechanism beyond strongest native parents and hybrid controls. Otherwise its useful content remains inside P-C and the flagship Perspective.

## 12. Non-claims and safety boundaries

This programme does not claim:

- that human cognition is inferior;
- that animal, collective, cultural, evolutionary and machine adaptation are one process;
- that evolution “thinks” or optimizes with foresight;
- that fitness or survival implies truth;
- that natural selection supplies ethical or institutional authority;
- that machine difference should be maximized;
- that internal opacity is acceptable;
- that machines should replace human scientific authority;
- that human values can be derived from machine-native optimization;
- that consciousness is absent or present;
- that one substrate supports a universally optimal intelligence;
- that self-improvement is safe merely because it is machine-native.

The machine can think differently internally while remaining accountable at the scientific and institutional boundary.
