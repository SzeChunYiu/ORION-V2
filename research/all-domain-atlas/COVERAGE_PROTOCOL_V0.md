# All-Domain Structural Donor Atlas — Coverage Protocol V0

**Status:** protocol design; no saturation claim.

## Objective

Construct a defensible, machine-readable atlas of canonical scientific and intellectual problem structures across organized human knowledge. The atlas is used to re-derive ORION-V2 atoms, workflows, solver policies and publication claims.

## Independent coverage skeleton

Use multiple non-identical field classifications to avoid one taxonomy's blind spots. At minimum cover:

- mathematics and statistics;
- computer and information science;
- physics, chemistry and Earth/environmental science;
- biology, ecology, agriculture and veterinary science;
- medicine, public health and measurement;
- all engineering branches, design, manufacturing and control;
- economics, finance, operations and decision science;
- business, management, accounting, organizational science and supply chains;
- political science, public administration, international relations and institutions;
- law, regulation, procedure and evidence;
- sociology, anthropology, demography, geography and communication;
- psychology, cognitive science and education;
- linguistics, language, literature and textual scholarship;
- history, archaeology, philosophy, religion and ethics;
- arts, architecture, music, design and cultural studies;
- interdisciplinary and practitioner traditions that ordinary academic taxonomies omit.

## Per-domain research cell

For each field/subfield record:

1. native canonical problems;
2. objects, states and representations;
3. operations, transformations and interventions;
4. invariants, constraints and conservation laws;
5. observables, interfaces and probe families;
6. equivalence, quotient and comparison notions;
7. identifiability and impossibility results;
8. uncertainty, error, dependence and measurement models;
9. scale, aggregation and coarse-graining;
10. resources, costs and control limits;
11. authority, institutions, incentives and decision rules;
12. provenance, history and path dependence;
13. failure modes, obstructions and recovery mechanisms;
14. strongest quantitative/formal methods and executable artifacts;
15. candidate mappings to V1/V2 and reasons those mappings may fail.

## Search routes

Every material cell uses:

- native terminology and canonical textbooks/reviews;
- primary-source mechanism search;
- changed-vocabulary search using the structural coordinates above;
- citation ancestry and forward pressure;
- repository collision audit against ORION V1;
- remote-neighbour search across unrelated fields;
- strongest donor-composition search;
- hostile “already solved” and “false analogy” rounds.

## Structural signature

A provisional machine signature is:

`S(K) = (O, R, Q, I, T, Inv, Obs, Id, Eq, Scale, Unc, Dep, Res, Auth, Prov, Hist, Cost)`

where `Q/I` separate observations/queries from interventions and `Eq` stores native equivalence/comparison families. Every populated coordinate carries provenance and an applicability status; `UNKNOWN` and `NOT_APPLICABLE` are distinct.

## Coverage and saturation state

Track a vector rather than one percentage:

`Sat_t = (taxonomy_gap, native_route_gap, primary_source_gap, changed_vocabulary_gap, repo_collision_rate, new_coordinate_rate, new_relation_rate, new_obstruction_rate, new_failure_rate, parent_contraction_rate, open_routes)`.

A bounded saturation terminal requires:

1. all declared taxonomy branches traversed;
2. native and changed-vocabulary routes completed or explicitly censored;
3. material donors reconstructed and dispositioned;
4. repository collisions resolved;
5. strongest donor products tested for surviving residuals;
6. two complete changed-vocabulary passes add no material coordinate, relation, obstruction, transition, failure, parent contraction or benchmark family;
7. unresolved routes enumerated rather than counted as negative evidence.

The terminal is `BOUNDED_SATURATION`, never exhaustive human knowledge.
