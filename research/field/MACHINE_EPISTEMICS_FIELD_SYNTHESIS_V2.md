# Machine Epistemics — Provisional Field Synthesis V2

**Status:** provisional field hypothesis after the human-thinking/lived-knowledge saturation reopen. No novelty, disciplinary, publication or scientific-truth authority is granted by this document.

**Working tagline:** **The control science of AI-driven discovery.**

## 1. Field question

As AI systems move from isolated prediction toward literature search, hypothesis generation, experiment choice, proof, simulation, instrument control, analysis, review and writing, the unit of concern becomes an executable research process.

Machine Epistemics asks:

> **Under what conditions may a machine-mediated research process change what it scientifically commits to, how it frames the problem, or what it chooses to investigate—and how can those changes remain warranted, replayable, revisable and authority-bounded?**

This question is broader than model accuracy and narrower than a general philosophy of machine knowledge.

The human-thinking/lived-knowledge reopen adds one correction to V1: scientific inquiry does not consist only of explicit claims and planned actions. It can depend on self-monitoring, procedural competence, learned perceptual discrimination, distributed cognition, criticism, surprise, reframing and the ability to turn failures into transferable lessons. Machine Epistemics should model those functions only where they change a scientific decision; it should not anthropomorphize machines or imitate human psychology for its own sake.

## 2. Primitive object

A **Machine-Epistemic Episode** remains a bounded research episode:

`E = (P, F, S, O, A, R, M, V, X, H, K)`

where:

- `P` — problem/criterion contract;
- `F` — current problem/representation frame;
- `S` — plural scientific state: claims, alternatives, representations, uncertainty, procedural/competence state where relevant, and a fallible inquiry self-model where used;
- `O` — active scientific obligations and blockers;
- `A` — admissible epistemic/scientific actions;
- `R` — resources, tools, providers, experimental access, time and compute;
- `M` — evidence identity, provenance, dependence, memory and source-mode state;
- `V` — verification, validation, review and evaluator contracts;
- `X` — observations, tool outputs and world interactions, including unexpected events when retained;
- `H` — append-only transition, failure and negative-history record;
- `K` — externally supplied authority/constitution boundary.

A tool execution produces an observation or candidate transformation. An interpretation proposes a scientific-state transition. Neither execution, introspection, novelty nor reviewer agreement is truth by itself.

## 3. Ten foundation laws retained

### L1 — Criterion conservation

A result is not comparable under a silently changed success criterion. Criterion mutation follows a pre-bound deviation rule or creates a new scientific comparison identity.

### L2 — Execution/evidence separation

Successful execution is not scientific support; failed execution is not scientific refutation unless the scientific contract explicitly makes the execution event evidential.

### L3 — Source/evidence binding

Support binds to the actual source/content/execution identity used, not merely a display name, citation string or asserted lineage.

### L4 — Non-amplifying authority

No local computation, evaluator, provenance check, majority vote, confidence score, self-reflection or model output may create scientific/adoption authority above its valid roots.

### L5 — Context-relative transport

Equivalence, comparability, abstraction, approximation, competence transfer and transport are typed and context-bound. One similarity score cannot silently substitute for them.

### L6 — Reopen on invalidated support

A commitment whose complete support family is invalidated must reopen. Independently supported commitments remain standing where their support still satisfies the current criterion.

### L7 — Dependence visibility

Repeated evidence does not become independent evidence. Shared source, data, model, prompt, instrument, transformation, teacher, author or evaluator dependence remains representable when it changes a scientific decision.

### L8 — Censoring visibility

A route not searched, unavailable, unobserved or custodially inaccessible cannot become evidence of absence.

### L9 — Bounded epistemic closure

Stopping states the searched basis, residual uncertainty, omitted/censored routes and omission challenge. Flatness alone is not saturation.

### L10 — Honest unresolved state

`CANNOT_CHECK` is first-class whenever required identity, evidence, relation, evaluator, authority, competence or resource conditions cannot be established. Aggregation cannot promote it to success.

## 4. Five corollaries from human inquiry

The reopened literature does not yet justify new foundation laws. It does expose consequences of L1–L10 that should be tested explicitly.

### C1 — Self-monitoring does not self-validate

A research system may learn a calibrated model of when its reasoning is likely to fail, when more computation is worthwhile, or when external review is needed. But a self-model is itself a model with training data, dependencies and blind spots. Its confidence cannot be its own validator.

### C2 — Problem framing may change, but identity must not

Peircean/Deweyan inquiry, cognitive-control research and representation-change work all pressure a fixed-task view of discovery. A machine may propose that the current frame is inadequate. If the proposal changes registered decisions or success criteria, the comparison identity changes unless continuity is independently justified.

### C3 — Instruction is not competence

Ryle, Polanyi, situated cognition and ordinary practice all warn that explicit instructions can underdetermine intelligent performance. A system that claims procedural transfer should demonstrate context-sensitive state recognition, timing, correction and a failure boundary—not merely quote or regenerate the procedure.

### C4 — Surprise is an opportunity signal, not evidence

Prediction error, anomaly, insight and serendipity can justify opening a diagnostic or hypothesis-generation process. They cannot authorize the resulting explanation. Useful serendipity requires encounter, recognition and follow-up testing; random novelty alone is noise.

### C5 — The unit of cognition can be distributed

Scientific decisions can depend on models, people, instruments, notebooks, databases, interfaces and institutional procedures. Evaluation should follow the information, dependence and authority topology of the whole episode rather than assign epistemic agency only to the base model.

## 5. Parent landscape: what the proposal does not own

Machine Epistemics inherits, rather than renames, mature traditions.

### Formal/computational parents

- computational epistemology and reliable inquiry;
- decision theory, POMDPs, rational metareasoning and control;
- Bayesian experimental design and active learning;
- truth maintenance, belief revision and model-based diagnosis;
- theorem proving, formal verification and abstract interpretation;
- bisimulation, experiment comparison, causal transport and measurement invariance;
- provenance, reproducible computing, metrology, evidence synthesis and systematic review;
- performative prediction, strategic response and evaluator governance.

### Philosophy/history/social epistemology parents

- Peirce and pragmatist inquiry/abduction;
- Dewey's reflective inquiry and problem formulation;
- Popperian fallibilism and mediated falsification;
- Kuhnian exemplars, anomaly and conceptual change;
- Lakatosian research programmes and progressive/degenerating repair;
- Feyerabend/scientific pluralism as a hostile control on universal method;
- Longino and social epistemology on background assumptions, criticism and uptake;
- testimony, epistemic dependence and institutional/scientific authority.

### Cognitive/practice parents

- metacognition, confidence and cognitive control;
- error monitoring and learning from failure;
- memory consolidation/retrieval and adaptive forgetting;
- insight and representation restructuring;
- curiosity, exploration–exploitation and information seeking;
- knowing-how, tacit knowledge and skill;
- embodied/ecological, situated and distributed cognition;
- apprenticeship, demonstration and professional reflection-in-action;
- serendipity and problem-finding research.

### Plural epistemic-practice parents

A human-knowledge atlas must also reconstruct traditions that do not begin from modern Western academic categories: classical Indian source/instrument-of-knowledge debates, Buddhist analyses of perception/concept/language, Chinese practice-centered skill traditions, Jain perspectival reasoning, Arabic/Islamic traditions of logic/demonstration/critique, Africana oral/intellectual repositories, Indigenous/local knowledge systems and ethnoepistemology. These traditions are not one homogeneous alternative epistemology and should be studied in their native terms before any machine translation.

The candidate residual is therefore **not** a new theory of abduction, metacognition, tacit knowledge, provenance, exploration or social knowledge. It is the machine-executable coordination of scientific-state transitions across these parent-owned mechanisms under explicit evidence, context, dependence, preservation and authority constraints.

## 6. Four public control questions

For a field-defining paper, the research programme is more intelligible as four questions than as a long internal module list.

### Q1 — What does the system know, and in what form?

Which claims, alternatives, uncertainties, source/access modes, procedures, skills and dependencies are observable? Which important distinctions are hidden by the current representation? Which apparent confirmations share ancestry?

### Q2 — How may it change what it knows or how it frames the problem?

Which commitments survive a new model, representation, context, instrument, procedure or problem frame? What must be revalidated? When is a changed object comparable to the old one, and for which decision?

### Q3 — How does it know that its own inquiry process is failing?

Can the system distinguish object-level uncertainty from method/representation failure? Is its confidence calibrated? Does criticism produce warranted state change? Can a failure become a scoped lesson rather than a repeated error or an overgeneralized rule?

### Q4 — When may it stop, escalate, explore or open a new problem?

What supports bounded closure? When is a lower-level repair sufficient? When is representation change required? How should unexpected encounters be pursued without rewarding noise, and who has authority to change the research agenda?

These questions map to the contracted K0–K6 implementation but do not make that factorization the ontology of the field.

## 7. Three coupled learning loops

If Machine Epistemics matters to ML, it should eventually change learning itself rather than remain a governance shell around a fixed predictor.

### World learning

Learn models, predictions, policies and causal/structural representations of the external problem.

### Self learning

Learn the calibration, blind spots, failure classes and intervention value of the learner/reasoner itself. Evaluate this loop on independent/delayed outcomes rather than self-agreement.

### Search-space learning

Learn when the current data source, representation, method, objective or action family is inadequate and should be expanded or reformulated.

The loops must remain separable enough that improvement on one cannot silently certify the others.

Candidate ML programmes include metacognitive self-models, typed failure replay, vector surprise, procedural/demonstration learning, affordance-conditioned policies, distributed-system evaluation, explicit representation-restructuring actions, plural model portfolios, exploration preservation and observer-aware learning.

These are research hypotheses, not established new algorithms.

## 8. A broader source ecology

A paper-centric autonomous scientist overrepresents what human communities have already made explicit in scholarly documents. A broader source ecology can include, with different authority and reliability contracts:

- papers, books, standards, datasets and proofs;
- manuals, recipes, checklists and incident reports;
- laboratory notebooks and troubleshooting histories;
- demonstrations, training examples and expert critique;
- oral/testimonial knowledge with source/community identity;
- artifacts, instruments and material interaction;
- environmental observations, accidents and unplanned events.

This does **not** make every source scientific evidence of equal weight. It makes source mode and mode of epistemic access explicit rather than allowing “retrievable text” to become the hidden universal knowledge type.

## 9. Bounded saturation, not civilizational saturation

No serious finite project can establish that “the knowledge-structure space of human civilization is saturated.” Even an enormous literature census misses lost knowledge, tacit practices, inaccessible communities, changing languages, proprietary/custodial material, future conceptualizations and knowledge that is enacted rather than recorded.

The defensible target is bounded saturation over a declared coverage universe:

`Discipline × SourceMode × KnowledgeForm × AcquisitionMode × Context × HistoricalEpoch × EpistemicPractice`.

A saturation claim requires repeated changed-vocabulary/adversarial passes that add no material coordinate **within that declared universe**, plus explicit censored regions and reopening conditions. A new material source/acquisition mode reopens the terminal.

## 10. Empirical programme

A credible field hypothesis must survive cases in which mature parents are strong.

Candidate field-level experiments should include:

1. exact known-answer cases for criterion/source/transport invariants;
2. strongest parent-composed baselines under matched information/resources;
3. self-model calibration and external-review-trigger cases;
4. instruction-versus-demonstrated-competence transfer;
5. hidden-dependence and distributed-cognition cases;
6. surprise capture with stochastic-noise hostile controls;
7. representation-restructuring cases where local search provably cannot distinguish alternatives;
8. false-Jump controls where simple repair is sufficient;
9. prospective serendipity/problem-finding tests;
10. cross-domain naturalistic episodes with blinded semantic adjudication;
11. negative cases in which parent composition ties or wins;
12. explicit `CANNOT_CHECK` cases.

Primary field outcomes are not “reflection quality” or “creativity scores.” They are protected scientific decisions: false completion, unsafe transport, overconfidence under dependence, unnecessary escalation, missed opportunity, recurrence after failure, unwarranted refusal and authority laundering.

## 11. Field falsifiers

The field proposal contracts if:

- strongest mature-parent compositions reproduce all protected cross-layer decisions;
- the common abstraction disappears when native domain/practice semantics are restored;
- human/practice source extensions add logging language but no decision value;
- self-models do not improve calibrated action/review decisions over simpler uncertainty baselines;
- surprise/serendipity machinery only increases distraction or false novelty;
- procedural/tacit annotations do not predict transfer failure beyond ordinary context variables;
- the seven-family implementation adds complexity without justified reach;
- external reviewers cannot agree on a stable residual after parent ownership is made explicit.

If these falsifiers fire, the correct outcome is an integration discipline for reliable agentic science, not a new field.

## 12. Explicit non-claims

Machine Epistemics does not currently claim:

- consciousness or phenomenology as an engineering requirement;
- a metaphysical machine self;
- one universal scientific method;
- a universal measure of novelty, intelligence, knowledge or epistemic value;
- that human reasoning is normatively optimal;
- that every recipe, oral tradition or accident is scientific evidence;
- that all cultural epistemologies can be flattened into one schema without loss;
- saturation of all human knowledge;
- a novel ML algorithm merely because the learning objective is redescribed;
- disciplinary independence before parent-composed baselines and external evaluation support it.

## 13. Proposed curriculum

A mature Machine Epistemics curriculum would combine:

1. scientific claims, evidence and uncertainty;
2. AI agents and scientific workflow systems;
3. decision theory, metareasoning and active experimentation;
4. formal relations, abstraction and transport;
5. truth maintenance, belief revision and diagnosis;
6. provenance, dependency and selective invalidation;
7. verification, validation and authority;
8. metacognition, confidence and error learning;
9. representation change, insight and problem formulation;
10. procedural/tacit skill and distributed cognition;
11. serendipity, exploration and problem finding;
12. social epistemology, testimony, criticism and governance;
13. plural epistemic practices and source/custody ethics;
14. epistemic stopping and bounded saturation;
15. reproducible scientific-control experiments across at least two materially different domains.

## 14. Relationship to ORION-V2 and publication

ORION-V2 is one candidate experimental framework, not the field itself. K0–K6 are one factorization and must contract if parent/native mechanisms make them redundant.

The specialist P-A–P-D papers must carry any original formal/empirical evidence; P-E remains contingent on prospective value. A flagship Perspective should synthesize the field hypothesis only after enough specialist evidence exists to make the residual more than a manifesto. It should expose parent ownership, negative terminals and field falsifiers rather than hide them.
