# Machine Epistemics — Provisional Field Synthesis V1

**Status:** provisional scientific field hypothesis. No novelty, disciplinary, publication, or scientific-truth authority.

**Tagline:** **The control science of AI-driven discovery.**

## 1. Field question

As AI systems move from assisting isolated scientific tasks to planning, retrieving, experimenting, proving, simulating, analyzing, reviewing, and writing across entire research loops, the hard systems problem changes.

The central question is no longer only:

> Can AI perform scientific tasks?

Machine Epistemics asks:

> **Under what conditions may a machine-mediated research process change what it scientifically commits to, and how can those changes remain warranted, replayable, revisable, and authority-bounded?**

This question treats the scientific process itself as an executable object without treating execution as truth.

## 2. Primitive object

A **Machine-Epistemic Episode** is

`E = (P, S, O, A, R, M, V, X, H, K)`

where:

- `P` — problem/criterion contract;
- `S` — plural scientific state: claims, alternatives, representations, uncertainty;
- `O` — active scientific obligations and blockers;
- `A` — admissible epistemic/scientific actions;
- `R` — resources, tools, providers, experimental access, time and compute;
- `M` — evidence identity, provenance, dependence and memory state;
- `V` — verification, validation, review and evaluator contracts;
- `X` — observations, tool outputs and world interactions;
- `H` — append-only transition and negative-history record;
- `K` — authority/constitution boundary.

A tool execution proposes information. An interpretation proposes a scientific-state transition. A transition may be admitted only under the current evidence, semantic, preservation, resource and authority obligations.

## 3. Core transition form

`T : (E_t, a_t, x_t) -> (E_{t+1}, receipt_t)`

A valid transition receipt should bind at least:

- episode/criterion identity;
- action and executor identity;
- observation/result identity;
- interpretation contract;
- affected claims and alternatives;
- evidence/provenance/dependence delta;
- assumptions and context;
- obligations discharged/created;
- commitments preserved/reopened;
- authority ceiling;
- replay identity;
- terminal or next-action status.

## 4. Ten foundation laws

### L1 — Criterion conservation

A result is not comparable under a silently changed success criterion. Criterion mutation either follows a pre-bound deviation rule or creates a new scientific comparison identity.

### L2 — Execution/evidence separation

Successful execution is not scientific support; failed execution is not scientific refutation unless the scientific contract explicitly makes the execution event evidential.

### L3 — Source/evidence binding

Support binds to the actual source/content/execution identity used, not merely a display name or citation string.

### L4 — Non-amplifying authority

No local computation, evaluator, provenance check, majority vote, confidence score, or model output may create scientific or adoption authority above its valid roots.

### L5 — Context-relative transport

Equivalence, comparability, abstraction, approximation, and transport are typed and context-bound. One similarity score cannot silently substitute for these relations.

### L6 — Reopen on invalidated support

A commitment whose complete support family is invalidated must reopen. Independently supported commitments remain standing where their support still satisfies the current criterion.

### L7 — Dependence visibility

Repeated evidence does not become independent evidence. Shared source, data, model, prompt, instrument, transformation, author, or evaluator dependence must remain representable when it changes a scientific decision.

### L8 — Censoring visibility

A route not searched, unavailable, or censored cannot become evidence of absence.

### L9 — Bounded epistemic closure

Stopping must state the searched basis, residual uncertainty, omitted/censored routes, and omission challenge. Flatness alone is not saturation.

### L10 — Honest unresolved state

`CANNOT_CHECK` is a first-class outcome whenever required identity, evidence, relation, evaluator, authority, or resource conditions cannot be established. Aggregation cannot promote it to success.

## 5. Candidate quantitative programme

Machine Epistemics should be judged by formal and measurable problems rather than terminology.

### 5.1 Epistemic observability

Given receipts and observations, which latent failure/knowledge states are distinguishable? Define observational equivalence classes and minimum discriminating interventions.

### 5.2 Justified reachability / scientific controllability

Given resources and admissible actions, which scientifically warranted states are reachable without violating preservation or authority constraints?

### 5.3 Evidence dependence and effective support

Estimate how dependence topology changes effective evidential strength and false-authority risk. Compare naive counts, clustered support, graphical dependence, and adversarial common-cause cases.

### 5.4 Minimum sufficient escalation

For an ordered family of interventions, identify the lowest level capable of resolving an obstruction. Measure false escalation, missed escalation, cost, preservation and justified reach.

### 5.5 Context-relative transport

Represent relation type, witnesses, counter-probes, loss/error, expiry, epoch and decision context. Study exact versus approximate transport and downstream decision stability.

### 5.6 Selective reopening

Given support/provenance hypergraphs and changed premises, compute the minimal set of commitments requiring reopening while retaining independently supported descendants.

### 5.7 Epistemic stopping

Under finite budgets and heterogeneous search routes, study stopping rules that trade omission risk against resource cost without laundering incomplete coverage into completeness.

### 5.8 Evaluator stability and performativity

Model criterion churn, evaluator dependence, strategic response and environment change caused by publication/deployment itself.

### 5.9 Replay identity

Define sufficient content-bound identity for deciding whether two scientific episodes are actually the same experiment/comparison rather than superficially similar reruns.

## 6. Parent fields and boundary

Machine Epistemics treats the following as parents, not as inventions:

- AI for Science and agentic/autonomous science;
- Bayesian experimental design and active learning;
- POMDPs, rational metareasoning and control;
- truth-maintenance systems and belief revision;
- model-based diagnosis and fault isolation;
- knowledge representation, theorem proving and formal verification;
- bisimulation, abstraction, causal transport and experiment comparison;
- provenance standards, reproducible computing and workflow systems;
- systematic review, metrology, evidence synthesis and research methodology;
- philosophy of science, social epistemology and scientific governance.

The candidate residual is the **cross-parent control semantics of machine-mediated scientific commitments**.

## 7. Relationship to ORION-V2

ORION-V2 is one candidate experimental framework for Machine Epistemics, not the field itself.

The field must remain meaningful if ORION-V2 is replaced by another implementation. Conversely, ORION-V2 may contract heavily while Machine Epistemics survives as a broader research programme.

The seven contracted V2 interface families can be read as one experimental factorization:

- identity/contracts;
- plural scientific state;
- context-relative relation/transport;
- evidence/provenance/revalidation;
- action/diagnosis;
- frontier/escalation;
- evaluation/parity/saturation/authority.

These are not automatically the final ontology of the field.

## 8. Empirical programme

A credible field requires more than one benchmark and more than one domain.

Minimum field-level empirical evidence should include:

1. controlled synthetic worlds with exact known answers;
2. frozen V1/V2 non-regression campaigns;
3. remote-domain scientific tasks with different vocabulary and mechanisms;
4. adversarial criterion/provenance/dependence failures;
5. non-identifiable cases requiring discriminating probes;
6. cross-generation representation/transport cases;
7. naturalistic AI-for-science or scientific-agent tasks;
8. independent blinded semantic evaluation;
9. strongest parent-composed baselines under matched resources;
10. negative cases where the parent composition is sufficient and Machine Epistemics adds nothing.

## 9. Field-level falsifiers

The field hypothesis fails or contracts if:

- all important decisions are reproduced by a straightforward composition of existing parents;
- the common abstraction disappears when native domain semantics are restored;
- the formal objects do not transfer across at least two materially different scientific domains;
- measured benefit is only additional logging/provenance rather than scientific decision quality;
- false closure or authority laundering is not improved;
- the framework adds complexity without protected justified reach;
- independent reviewers cannot agree on a stable field boundary.

## 10. Proposed basic curriculum

A Machine Epistemics curriculum would contain:

1. scientific claims, evidence and uncertainty;
2. AI agents and scientific workflow systems;
3. decision theory, metareasoning and active experimentation;
4. formal relations, abstraction and transport;
5. truth maintenance, belief revision and diagnosis;
6. provenance, dependency and selective invalidation;
7. verification versus validation and authority;
8. evaluator design, dependence and performativity;
9. epistemic stopping and saturation;
10. reproducible scientific-control experiments;
11. machine/human delegation and governance;
12. domain practicum in at least two sciences.

## 11. Publication architecture

Machine Epistemics should not be established by one manifesto alone.

- specialist primary papers P-A–P-D must establish the reusable technical evidence;
- contingent P-E survives only if prospective opportunity value is measurable;
- the flagship field paper synthesizes the surviving results and neighboring literature;
- the flagship should normally be a peer-reviewed Perspective/Review, while new primary evidence stays in the specialist articles;
- external field recognition remains separate from internal ORION-V2 closeout.
