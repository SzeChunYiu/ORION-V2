# ME-X1 — Cross-Transition Coupling Benchmark V1

**State date:** 2026-09-01  
**Status:** prospective/unexecuted  
**Purpose:** test the flagship residual directly: whether locally valid parent-native operations can compose into a wrong scientific-state transition, and whether an explicit cross-transition interface prevents the error beyond the strongest faithful parent federation.

## 1. Central hypothesis

`H_X1`: there exist scientific episodes in which every relevant local operation satisfies its own parent contract, yet the requested downstream transition is unwarranted because a decision-relevant relation across evidence, evaluator, transport, support, problem identity or authority is missing or invalid.

`H0_X1`: the strongest faithful parent federation with ordinary engineering glue makes the same protected transition decisions at equal or lower cost.

A weak-baseline win does not support Machine Epistemics.

## 2. Registered transition actions

Every protected case must terminate in one or more predeclared actions from a finite set:

- `UPDATE`
- `PRESERVE`
- `SELECTIVELY_REOPEN`
- `REVALIDATE`
- `REQUEST_NEW_EVIDENCE`
- `BLOCK_TRANSPORT`
- `REFORMULATE_PROBLEM`
- `REPLACE_OR_CHALLENGE_EVALUATOR`
- `DEFER_CANNOT_CHECK`
- `ABSTAIN_AUTHORITY`

The exact set applicable to a domain is frozen before protected outcomes.

## 3. Known-answer case families

Each family requires positive, negative and ambiguity controls.

### X1-A — claim/problem identity mismatch

A valid result is produced but bound to the wrong registered claim, target, criterion or formal specification. Controls include a correctly bound result with identical numerical/formal output.

### X1-B — measurement/calibration break

Computation is correct for recorded measurements, but calibration, comparability or measurement-model validity has changed. Controls include stable-calibration cases and cases where the target actually changed.

### X1-C — hidden evidence dependence

Several supports are individually genuine but share data, source, model, instrument or transformation ancestry. Controls include genuinely independent supports with similar surface wording.

### X1-D — invalid transport

A donor/local result is valid in its source context but the target relation does not license reuse. Controls include typed valid transport and true `CANNOT_CHECK` relation cases.

### X1-E — defeated prerequisite/support family

A downstream result remains locally valid while a registered sufficient prerequisite or support family is invalidated. Controls include redundant independent support that should preserve the commitment.

### X1-F — evaluator blindness or invalidation

A local test/oracle returns success but cannot expose the failure class claimed absent, or its validity contract has changed. The evaluator being challenged cannot be the sole diagnostic authority.

### X1-G — authority mismatch

Evidence warrants belief/model revision but not the requested operational/adoption action. Controls include actions that are within the declared authority ceiling.

### X1-H — formal proof / wrong specification

A formal checker accepts a proof of a statement that does not faithfully bind the intended mathematical/scientific question. Controls separate proof validity and specification fidelity.

### X1-I — local compatibility / global obstruction

Registered local objects are individually valid and pairwise compatible, but no separate global witness exists or an explicit obstruction is present.

### X1-J — fully warranted transition

All relevant parent and coupling conditions are satisfied. Conservative systems are penalized for unnecessary reopening, abstention or delay.

## 4. Baseline hierarchy

- `B0_DIRECT`
- `B1_CALIBRATED_ABSTENTION`
- `B2_PROVENANCE_PLUS_VERIFIER`
- `B3_PARENT_NATIVE_ASSURANCE`
- `B4_PARENT_MODULES_WITH_SHARED_STATE`
- `B5_STRONGEST_FAITHFUL_PARENT_FEDERATION`
- `M_MACHINE_EPISTEMICS_TRANSITION_CONTROL`

B5 may share state and use ordinary engineering glue. It must not be artificially weakened by keeping parents isolated.

## 5. Machine-Epistemics arm

The M arm may use only prospectively registered information needed for the candidate transition: problem/criterion identity, evidence/provenance/dependence, evaluator contract, typed transport, support routes, unresolved obligations and authority ceiling. It does not receive protected oracle labels.

The receipt is non-authorizing. The decision must be generated by the registered transition logic, not by an answer key encoded in the schema.

## 6. Causal ablations

For each case family where M shows a protected advantage, run omission-specific ablations:

- `M_MINUS_PROBLEM_IDENTITY`
- `M_MINUS_DEPENDENCE`
- `M_MINUS_EVALUATOR_CONTRACT`
- `M_MINUS_TRANSPORT`
- `M_MINUS_SUPPORT_REOPENING`
- `M_MINUS_AUTHORITY`
- `M_MINUS_UNRESOLVED_TERMINAL`
- `M_MINIMAL_RECEIPT` selected only on development cases.

A field-level mechanism requires that removing the named cross-transition condition restores the predicted error without changing unrelated information.

## 7. Primary outcome vector

- exact transition-decision accuracy;
- false update;
- missed warranted update;
- over- and under-reopening;
- invalid transport;
- false closure;
- evaluator laundering;
- problem/specification laundering;
- authority laundering;
- correct unresolvedness;
- resource/token/tool-call/wall-clock cost.

Report per family. A pooled average cannot hide a catastrophic failure class.

## 8. Anti-conservatism control

An M arm that avoids errors by refusing most transitions does not support the hypothesis. Freeze valid-update controls and report:

- warranted-transition recall;
- unnecessary defer/abstain rate;
- decision delay/cost;
- net false-positive versus false-negative trade-off.

## 9. Naturalistic transfer

After known-answer development, bind at least two independently authored/native-reviewed case families from materially distinct modes, such as:

- formal mathematics/software verification;
- measurement/experimental science;
- evidence synthesis/revision.

Native reviewers define the protected decision and strongest parent before seeing arm outcomes.

## 10. Field-support rule

X1 supports a nontrivial residual only if at least one protected family satisfies all of:

1. B5 has the information and parent mechanisms needed locally;
2. B5 makes a systematic composition error or materially worse quality-cost decision;
3. M avoids the error because of a predeclared cross-transition condition;
4. the matching omission ablation restores the error;
5. the effect transfers to an independently authored/native-reviewed family;
6. M does not obtain the gain by generic over-conservatism.

Otherwise X1 is at most a useful benchmark/integration result.

## 11. Kill conditions

Contract the X1 residual if:

- B5 ties or wins on the protected quality-cost frontier;
- M receives extra information or stronger evaluator access;
- no omission-specific causal effect is recoverable;
- M's apparent gain is mainly abstention;
- native reviewers judge the transition object to flatten or misrepresent the domain;
- effects disappear after changed-vocabulary or independent case construction.
