# ME-X5 — Cross-Domain Field Residual Test V1

**State date:** 2026-09-01  
**Status:** prospective/unexecuted  
**Role:** principal empirical discriminator for whether Machine Epistemics is more than an integration/benchmark programme.

## 1. Central question

Does one stable scientific-transition object and at least one causal cross-transition mechanism survive native-domain reconstruction and outperform the strongest faithful parent federation in more than one materially different epistemic mode?

This study cannot establish an *established* field; external community uptake is still required. It can support or kill the scientific residual needed for an emerging-field claim.

## 2. Required epistemic modes

At minimum include three independently constructed/native-reviewed modes:

1. **formal mode** — mathematics, theorem proving, software/formal verification;
2. **experimental/computational physical-science mode** — measurement, simulation or experimental model revision;
3. **evidence/measurement revision mode** — evidence synthesis, calibration, transport or dynamic validation.

A fourth social/observational domain is optional only when protected decisions and evaluator contracts can be made sufficiently crisp.

## 3. Native-domain ownership gate

Before a case enters protected evaluation, a rotating native-domain reviewer must record:

- native scientific objects and vocabulary;
- strongest native methods;
- valid/invalid transition outcomes;
- native failure classes;
- evaluator/measurement assumptions;
- which ME abstractions are lossy, redundant or invalid;
- strongest plausible parent composition for the case.

Cases failing native recovery do not count against or for Machine Epistemics; they are framework-mapping failures.

## 4. B5 construction

`B5_STRONGEST_FAITHFUL_PARENT_FEDERATION` is a real competing system, not a strawman. It may:

- use each parent method in its native role;
- share state when scientifically necessary;
- use ordinary engineering interfaces/glue;
- invoke domain-native evaluators and repair logic;
- condition its behavior on context and resources.

M receives no protected information B5 lacks. The ME residual cannot be defined by artificially forcing B5 modules to remain isolated.

## 5. Machine-Epistemics arm

M may use the subset of candidate interfaces relevant to each case:

- target/observation boundary;
- problem/criterion identity;
- provenance/dependence;
- discrepancy-locus hypotheses;
- evaluator/diagnostic-evaluator separation;
- typed transport;
- support-family reopening;
- minimum-sufficient escalation;
- generative-regime preservation contracts;
- atlas/gluing and horizon state;
- external authority boundary.

Unused components remain inactive and their cost is still reported.

## 6. Protected outcome vector

Pre-register per-domain outcomes rather than one pooled scalar:

- false scientific transition;
- missed warranted transition;
- target/measurement/model/problem/evaluator responsibility error;
- invalid transport;
- wrong reopening/preservation;
- false closure;
- unnecessary escalation;
- missed necessary escalation;
- unresolved-state calibration;
- authority violation;
- semantic/native-fidelity violation;
- resource cost.

A pooled gain cannot compensate for catastrophic failure in one epistemic mode.

## 7. Required mechanism evidence

An emerging field residual requires at least one predeclared mechanism satisfying:

1. the mechanism is identifiable across at least two native modes;
2. M shows a protected quality-cost residual beyond B5 in those modes;
3. a targeted ablation removes the residual or restores the predicted failure;
4. the gain is not explained by additional information, compute, evaluator access or stronger abstention;
5. native reviewers agree that the same scientific function is being tested rather than merely the same ORION label.

## 8. Changed-vocabulary test

A separate reviewer should receive native case descriptions without Machine-Epistemics terminology and independently classify the scientific decision structure. If the common object disappears when ORION vocabulary is hidden, field-level recurrence is not supported.

## 9. Negative-control requirements

Include cases where:

- one mature parent is fully sufficient;
- B5 is expected to win due to lower overhead;
- the correct terminal is unresolved;
- no representation/model escalation is needed;
- local compatibility does not imply globality;
- the apparent multi-layer issue disappears after native problem reconstruction.

## 10. Statistical/reporting rule

Report per-domain effect estimates and quality-cost frontiers. Any cross-domain aggregation must preserve direction and reveal heterogeneity. A field claim cannot rely only on a pooled average with one or more domains showing clear inferiority.

## 11. Field-support ladder

- `R0_NO_RESIDUAL`: B5 ties/wins or common object dissolves.
- `R1_BENCHMARK_INTEGRATION_VALUE`: useful cross-domain benchmark/interface, no new mechanism.
- `R2_EMERGING_INTERDISCIPLINARY_RESIDUAL`: stable object + causal mechanism + residual beyond B5 in multiple modes + independent adjudication.
- `R3_ESTABLISHED_FIELD`: **not grantable by this experiment**; requires independent community/institutional uptake over time.

## 12. Kill conditions

Contract the field claim if:

- B5 ties or wins the protected frontier;
- common object is unstable under native reconstruction;
- only one domain shows a residual;
- targeted ablations fail to support the claimed mechanism;
- M gains come from extra information/resources or over-abstention;
- independent changed-vocabulary reviewers cannot recover a common object;
- integration costs or failure surface dominate benefits.
