# Primary Source Ledger — Wave 03

**Status:** source identities and mechanism summaries for native reconstruction. Inclusion is not evidence that the ORION synthesis is novel or correct.

## Formal generalization and abstraction

### Goguen and Burstall — Institutions

- Joseph A. Goguen and Rod M. Burstall, “Institutions: Abstract Model Theory for Specification and Programming,” JACM 39(1), 1992.
- Native contribution: signatures, sentences, models, satisfaction and invariance under notation change.
- ORION use: parent of logic-independent native-theory packaging and satisfaction-preserving transport.
- Boundary: institution theory does not by itself provide scientific evidence, resource, authority, performativity or target-validation semantics.

### Cousot and Cousot — Abstract interpretation

- Patrick Cousot and Radhia Cousot, “Abstract Interpretation: A Unified Lattice Model for Static Analysis of Programs by Construction or Approximation of Fixpoints,” POPL 1977.
- Native contribution: sound abstraction, abstract domains, transformers and fixpoint approximation.
- ORION use: parent of sound one-sided envelopes and quotient validation.
- Boundary: a scientific abstraction still requires target decisions, native semantics and authority.

### Rubenstein et al. — exact causal transformations

- Paul K. Rubenstein et al., “Causal Consistency of Structural Equation Models,” arXiv:1707.00819, 2017.
- Native contribution: exact transformations between causal models at different levels, indexed by interventions.
- ORION use: parent of scale/action consistency and macro/micro transport.
- Boundary: causal consistency is not generic semantic or measurement comparability.

## Information comparison and identifiability

### Blackwell — comparison of experiments

- David Blackwell, “Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments,” Annals of Mathematical Statistics 24(2), 1953.
- Native contribution: decision-theoretic ordering of experiments through garbling/informativeness.
- ORION use: parent of directional, task-relative information comparison.
- Boundary: equality on registered tasks is weaker than Blackwell equivalence.

### Pawlak — rough sets

- Zdzisław Pawlak, “Rough Sets,” International Journal of Computer & Information Sciences 11, 1982.
- Native contribution: attribute-relative indiscernibility and lower/upper approximations.
- ORION use: parent of finite probe-relative uncertainty sets.
- Boundary: indiscernibility under current attributes is not semantic identity.

## Local/global and scale

### Abramsky and Brandenburger — contextuality as global-section obstruction

- Samson Abramsky and Adam Brandenburger, “The Sheaf-Theoretic Structure of Non-Locality and Contextuality,” New Journal of Physics 13, 2011; arXiv:1102.0264.
- Native contribution: compatible local empirical data may lack a global section.
- ORION use: parent of explicit local/global obstruction terminals.
- Boundary: transferring the formal obstruction to another domain requires a faithful native model, not analogy alone.

## Dependence and evidence

### Hedges, Tipton and Johnson — dependent effect sizes

- Larry V. Hedges, Elizabeth Tipton and Matthew C. Johnson, “Robust Variance Estimation in Meta-Regression with Dependent Effect Size Estimates,” Research Synthesis Methods 1(1), 2010.
- Native contribution: inference when effect-size estimates are dependent.
- ORION use: parent evidence that dependence must enter assurance quantitatively.
- Boundary: the Wave 03 equicorrelation design-effect adapter is not robust variance estimation and must not be presented as such.

### Kish — design effects

- Leslie Kish, Survey Sampling, 1965, and later design-effect work.
- Native contribution: complex designs and clustering alter variance/effective information; intracluster correlation enters design effect.
- ORION use: parent of the finite effective-count reference calculation.
- Boundary: evidence clusters outside survey designs need target-native covariance models.

### de Kleer and Williams — multiple faults

- Johan de Kleer and Brian C. Williams, “Diagnosing Multiple Faults,” Artificial Intelligence 32(1), 1987.
- Native contribution: multiple-fault candidates and sequential measurements to localize failures.
- ORION use: reinforces plural responsibility and discriminating probes.
- Boundary: scientific responsibility includes evidence, authority and workflow not contained in the engineering model alone.

## Provenance and reticulation

### W3C PROV

- W3C Recommendation, “PROV-DM: The PROV Data Model,” 30 April 2013.
- Native contribution: domain-agnostic entities, activities, agents, derivations, bundles, alternate/specialization relations and collections.
- ORION use: strongest default provenance parent.
- Boundary: provenance does not automatically establish semantic transport or scientific correctness.

### Phylogenetic networks

- Guohua Jin et al., “Inferring Phylogenetic Networks by the Maximum Parsimony Criterion,” Molecular Biology and Evolution 24(1), 2007.
- Native contribution: networks represent histories involving horizontal transfer beyond a bifurcating tree.
- ORION use: parent analogy for explicit multi-parent scientific inheritance.
- Boundary: biological evolutionary probabilities must not be imported into scientific artifact lineage without target evidence.

## Performative response

### Lucas — policy evaluation critique

- Robert E. Lucas Jr., “Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique,” Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy 1, 1976.
- Native contribution: policy changes can alter behavioural relationships, invalidating reduced-form extrapolation.
- ORION use: parent of strategic/performative response and certificate expiry.
- Boundary: the full rational-expectations/economic model is not a universal scientific-evaluation model.

### Perdomo et al. — performative prediction

- Juan Perdomo, Tijana Zrnic, Celestine Mendler-Dünner and Moritz Hardt, “Performative Prediction,” ICML/PMLR 119, 2020.
- Native contribution: deployment-dependent distributions, performative risk and performative stability.
- ORION use: parent of finite response maps, stable policies and retraining dynamics.
- Boundary: scientific evaluation additionally separates validity, authority and protected outcomes.

## Frontier portfolios and search

### March — exploration and exploitation

- James G. March, “Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning,” Organization Science 2(1), 1991.
- Native contribution: organizational allocation between exploration of new possibilities and exploitation of known certainties.
- ORION use: parent of portfolio and policy diversity.
- Boundary: scientific opportunities require falsifiers, evidence and external agenda authority.

### Lehman and Stanley — novelty search

- Joel Lehman and Kenneth O. Stanley, “Abandoning Objectives: Evolution Through the Search for Novelty Alone,” Evolutionary Computation 19(2), 2011.
- Native contribution: behavioural novelty can supply stepping stones in deceptive search.
- ORION use: parent of diversity/option-value coordinates.
- Boundary: novelty is not importance, truth, scientific value or authority.
