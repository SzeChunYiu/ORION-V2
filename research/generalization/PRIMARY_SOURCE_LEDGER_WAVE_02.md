# Primary Source Ledger — Wave 02

This ledger records parent identities used to derive the formalization. It is not a claim that the literature search is saturated. Full native reconstruction remains required before scientific use.

## A. Meta-formalization parents

### A1 — Institution theory

Joseph A. Goguen and Rod M. Burstall, “Institutions: Abstract Model Theory for Specification and Programming,” *Journal of the ACM* 39(1), 1992, 95–146. DOI: `10.1145/147508.147524`.

Native object used:

- signature category;
- sentence and model functors;
- satisfaction relation;
- satisfaction condition under signature morphisms.

Parent ownership:

- logic-independent specification;
- notation-change invariance;
- heterogeneous logical structure.

ORION may not claim these objects.

### A2 — Arbitrary institutions and theory translations

Donald Sannella and Andrzej Tarlecki, work on specifications and theorems in arbitrary institutions, plus the wider theory-morphism and heterogeneous-specification literature.

Native object used:

- theory translation;
- specification structuring;
- preservation across different logical systems.

Open obligation:

- identify the strongest existing operational/institutional morphism product before attributing any integrated transport receipt to ORION.

### A3 — Abstract interpretation

Patrick Cousot and Radhia Cousot, “Abstract Interpretation: A Unified Lattice Model for Static Analysis of Programs by Construction or Approximation of Fixpoints,” POPL 1977. DOI: `10.1145/512950.512973`.

Native object used:

- concrete and abstract domains;
- sound over-approximation;
- abstraction/concretization relations;
- abstract transformers and fixpoint reasoning.

Parent ownership:

- sound abstraction;
- set-valued or ordered approximation;
- safe one-sided inference.

### A4 — Blackwell comparison

David Blackwell’s comparison of statistical experiments and later Le Cam-style experiment comparison.

Native object used:

- directional information order;
- decision-relative comparison;
- garbling and deficiency concepts.

Parent ownership:

- judging information structures by achievable decision performance rather than semantic or syntactic similarity.

## B. Generalized obligation-process parents

### B1 — Workflow-net soundness

Wil M. P. van der Aalst and subsequent workflow-net literature on soundness, completion, dead transitions, and process verification.

Native object used:

- markings;
- enabled transitions;
- proper completion;
- option to complete;
- absence of dead tasks.

Parent ownership:

- generic workflow/process soundness.

ORION candidate extension:

- scientific obligations, evidence gates, authority ceilings, resource semantics, reopening, and immutable adverse history.

### B2 — Systems engineering and V&V

NASA and systems-engineering handbooks covering stakeholder expectations, requirements, logical decomposition, architecture, implementation, integration, verification, validation, transition, and technical management.

Native object used:

- requirements traceability;
- verification versus validation;
- configuration and change control;
- lifecycle assurance.

Parent ownership:

- requirement-to-design-to-V&V lifecycle structures.

### B3 — Stage-gate and process governance

Project, innovation, quality, audit, and internal-control traditions.

Native object used:

- phase entry and exit obligations;
- control owner and approval authority;
- exception, escalation, and audit evidence.

Open obligation:

- reconstruct exact quantitative models rather than treating management practice as one informal donor.

## C. Plural responsibility parents

### C1 — Model-based diagnosis

Raymond Reiter, “A Theory of Diagnosis from First Principles,” *Artificial Intelligence* 32(1), 1987, 57–95. DOI: `10.1016/0004-3702(87)90062-2`.

Native object used:

- system description;
- observations;
- diagnoses as component-abnormality assumptions restoring consistency;
- minimal diagnoses.

Parent ownership:

- consistency-based diagnosis and minimal explanation sets.

### C2 — Multiple-fault and interaction diagnosis

Reliability engineering, FMEA/fault trees, medical differential diagnosis, and later model-based diagnosis work that permits several simultaneous faults and richer failure topologies.

Native object used:

- multiple diagnoses;
- common cause;
- masking/cancellation;
- interaction-only failures;
- diagnostic test design.

ORION correction:

- a universal single-root or earliest-stage rule is not justified across causal topologies.

## D. Correspondence and comparability parents

### D1 — Metrological traceability

NIST and the International Vocabulary of Metrology tradition.

Native object used:

- unbroken documented calibration chain;
- reference identity;
- uncertainty contribution at every link;
- fitness-for-purpose distinction.

Parent ownership:

- measurement traceability and uncertainty chains.

### D2 — Psychometric equating and invariance

Educational-testing equating/linking literature and measurement-invariance work, including Meredith’s factorial-invariance tradition.

Native object used:

- common-item or common-person anchors;
- construct invariance;
- differential item functioning;
- scale linking and uncertainty.

Parent ownership:

- comparable scores across forms and populations under declared assumptions.

### D3 — Political scale linking

Quantitative political-methodology work on ideal-point estimation, anchors, dynamic scales, and cross-time or cross-chamber comparison.

Native object used:

- latent-state identification;
- bridge observations or actors;
- changing policy-space warning;
- theory-driven interpretation of latent dimensions.

### D4 — Causal transportability

Elias Bareinboim and Judea Pearl’s transportability framework and related selection-diagram literature.

Native object used:

- source and target populations;
- selection mechanisms;
- transport formulas;
- conditions under which causal knowledge transfers.

Parent ownership:

- causal transport under explicit structural assumptions.

### D5 — Diachronic semantic alignment

Computational historical-linguistics work, including Hamilton, Leskovec, and Jurafsky on diachronic word embeddings.

Native object used:

- time-indexed semantic representations;
- alignment across historical corpora;
- measured semantic drift.

Open obligation:

- distinguish representational alignment from preservation of a registered scientific decision.

## F. Source-use rules

Every source entering a formal transport must record:

- exact bibliographic or artifact identity;
- whether full text was read;
- native objects and judgments reconstructed;
- assumptions and scope;
- known counterexamples or limitations;
- strongest parent ownership;
- what remains unresolved.

A title, abstract, secondary summary, or recalled citation cannot by itself establish a native theory reconstruction.
