# Finite-sample transport extension — prospective computation record

This theoretical extension is registered after the complete-model primary and complementarity calibrations. It is not a retrospective pre-registration of them and changes none of their inputs or outcomes. Parent coordination remains #313; only this contribution's certificate_transport_v1 directory is written.

Question: replace the complete-distribution/error-table assumption for a conservative transport bound by explicitly modeled IID calibration data. Keep confidence in a statistical bound distinct from the bound on deployment failure and from exact truth/action authority.

CT-10: construct a rational, conservative one-sided binomial upper confidence limit by exact inversion of Pr_Bin(n,p)[K<=k]. Prove coverage for every Bernoulli p, not by finite enumeration. Missing samples, noninteger counts, unknown sampling law, or invalid confidence levels cannot certify deployment risk. Numeric counts alone do not establish IID or checker correctness.

CT-10b: for two fixed deterministic predictors f,g using the same semantic error predicate, their failure-event symmetric difference is contained in the prediction-disagreement event. Thus old labeled calibration plus fresh IID *unlabeled* paired predictions can support a conservative new-operator risk bound u_old+u_disagreement+epsilon under a separately justified joint-distribution TV bound. No free label oracle for the new model; old label cost, both predictor evaluations, sample sizes and confidence budgets are explicit. Covariate-only drift is not joint drift unless conditional-label invariance is justified.

CT-11: establish simultaneous finite-family validity by allocating alpha/m to each fixed candidate; no independence between candidates is assumed. Preserve an adaptive-selection counterexample: four predictors each have true risk 3/4, with independent error coordinates on one shared sampled vector. At nominal alpha=1/2, choosing a predictor with zero observed errors gives an invalid upper bound 1/2 with probability 1-(3/4)^4 > 1/2. Correct simultaneous accounting uses alpha/4.

CT-12: indistinguishable-history boundary. Without a restriction connecting deployment to calibration, two environments can have identical calibration histories and opposite future outcomes. No nontrivial universal deployment guarantee follows merely from sample history or unchanged artifact hashes.

Frozen supplementary computation: n=1..12, all k=0..n, alpha in {1/20,1/10,1/2}, exact inversion on denominator 2^16. Validate binomial-tail certificates and predecessor minimality; verify exact coverage over p in {0,1/16,...,1}; verify count monotonicity and retain the rational rounding. For common semantic binary classification, enumerate all true-label/old-prediction/new-prediction triples on three atoms and verify the pointwise disagreement inclusion. Compute the four-predictor selection counterexample by exact enumeration of all 16 sample vectors. Add zero-failure, no-sample, wrong count, wrong confidence, unknown drift and no-change controls. Actual denominators are recorded by executed counters.

This is standard exact binomial inversion, disagreement/error triangle inequalities, union bounds and an indistinguishability argument, not claimed novel probability theory. It supplies a finite-sample *conditional theorem*, not real OCM validation. Parent-source reconstruction, all costs, local isolated Linux execution, and independent-review limitations remain explicit. An arbitrary user-supplied count tuple or epsilon is not trusted evidence.
