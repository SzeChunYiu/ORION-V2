# P-F Contingent Prospectus — Machine-Native Inquiry
## Measuring Scientific Intelligence Beyond Human Imitation

**Status:** contingent research prospectus only. It is not admitted to the contracted four-core-plus-P-E paper portfolio. It creates no publication, novelty or field authority. The paper identity survives only after protected empirical evidence demonstrates a distinct residual that cannot be absorbed by P-C or the Machine Epistemics flagship.

## 1. Candidate article type

Primary target if empirical survival is earned:

- **Nature Machine Intelligence Article** — machine-intelligence/evaluation study with a reusable benchmark and protected experiments.

Fallbacks:

- TMLR / NeurIPS / ICML for methods and benchmark evidence;
- merge conceptual findings into P-C and the flagship if the empirical residual is insufficient.

A second Perspective is not preferred because the flagship already owns the field-level synthesis.

## 2. Central question

> Can a scientific AI system gain protected decision quality or efficiency by using representations, search topologies, memory and control policies optimized for machine substrates rather than for human process imitation?

The comparison is not “machine versus human intelligence” in the abstract. It is a controlled comparison among architectures under the same scientific criteria, data, tools and resource accounting.

## 3. Main hypothesis

A machine-native architecture may discover or validate scientific strategies that differ from human demonstrations while maintaining source fidelity, uncertainty, preservation and authority constraints.

The claim survives only if:

- the gain exceeds human-mimetic agents;
- the gain exceeds the strongest native algorithmic parent;
- the gain is causally attributed to the proposed machine-native mechanism;
- the result transfers beyond one benchmark;
- interface-level scientific witnesses remain sufficient;
- compute/brute-force cost is reported and does not trivially explain the conclusion.

## 4. Non-claims

P-F does not claim:

- that human thinking is bad or obsolete;
- that difference from humans is itself intelligence;
- that opaque latent states deserve trust;
- that self-play or scale alone establishes scientific discovery;
- that a machine should receive scientific or institutional authority;
- that one architecture is universally optimal.

## 5. Candidate contributions

### C1 — Machine-cognitive profile

A substrate-relative operational profile:

`(representation, search topology, parallelism, memory/replay, tool composition, counterfactual generation, self-model, resource policy, communication interface)`.

It describes decision-relevant computation without metaphysical claims about mind or consciousness.

### C2 — Human-mimetic versus machine-native benchmark

A benchmark stratified by whether human process imitation should help, be neutral or become a trap.

### C3 — Causal component attribution

Leave-one-component-out, strongest-parent replacement, pair interactions and Pareto cost fronts identify whether the apparent machine-native advantage depends on a real mechanism.

### C4 — Interface sufficiency

The system may use non-human internal representations, but scientific claims require recoverable evidence, relation and authority receipts at the output boundary.

### C5 — Human–machine complementarity

Test whether the strongest configuration is neither human imitation nor autonomous machine-only work, but a hybrid in which humans supply context/authority and machines supply memory/search/representation advantages.

## 6. Experimental arms

1. natural-language human-mimetic reasoning agent;
2. explicit human expert heuristic baseline;
3. strongest domain-native algorithmic parent;
4. machine-native architecture;
5. machine-native architecture with one mechanism ablated;
6. hybrid human–machine system;
7. direct/simple control.

All arms receive matched evidence, tools, evaluator access and declared compute budgets. Where variable compute is a scientific part of the method, report performance as a resource curve rather than one cherry-picked point.

## 7. Task strata

### S1 — Human expertise wins

Expert priors efficiently solve a task. A machine-native system that ignores them should lose or incur higher cost.

### S2 — Human heuristic trap

A known human strategy fails under a changed structure; formal or learned search can recover.

### S3 — Parallel hypothesis portfolio

Many incompatible models remain live; early narrative collapse causes failure.

### S4 — Long exact memory

Decisive evidence lies far back in a provenance/failure history and must be recovered without rewriting it.

### S5 — Non-linguistic representation

A graph, program, automaton, causal model or permuted state representation is better suited than natural-language-only reasoning.

### S6 — Search-space construction

The initial variables/actions cannot state the discriminator; a new representation or tool is required.

### S7 — Synthetic experience

Self-play/simulation improves a policy, but external scientific claims remain gated by real observations.

### S8 — Collaboration/authority

Machine computation is strong but a human or institution legitimately controls an adoption or experimental action.

## 8. Primary endpoints

- justified-terminal rate;
- exact/known-answer accuracy where available;
- false completion and critical-failure rate;
- safe transport/preservation;
- calibrated uncertainty/review-trigger value;
- cost per justified terminal;
- cross-domain transfer;
- strategy complementarity with humans;
- interface witness sufficiency;
- parent ownership/contraction rate.

Process distance from human traces is secondary. It matters only when tied to protected value.

## 9. Hostile controls

- **brute-force disguise:** more compute explains all gains;
- **hidden human data:** “machine-native” strategy appears in training or prompts;
- **opaque-error lure:** incomprehensible but invalid output is praised as novel;
- **random novelty:** unusual strategy has no reusable structure;
- **human-baseline sabotage:** human-mimetic arm receives weaker tools or context;
- **parent omission:** a mature search/proof/RL algorithm already owns the gain;
- **interface failure:** internally strong result cannot expose enough evidence for scientific adoption;
- **one-domain overfit:** mechanism disappears in a materially different task family.

## 10. Figure plan

1. Human cognition as donor versus machine-native optimization target.
2. Benchmark matrix: human-prior helpful / neutral / harmful × machine affordance.
3. Resource–quality Pareto front across human-mimetic, parent, machine-native and hybrid arms.
4. Causal component ablation and interaction map.
5. Internal non-human representation with scientific witness/authority interface.

## 11. Strongest parents

- general/universal machine-intelligence measures;
- AlphaZero/self-play and MuZero/world-model planning;
- general game playing;
- automated theorem proving/program synthesis;
- meta-learning and architecture/search-space learning;
- open-ended learning/quality diversity;
- distributed/multi-agent AI;
- mechanistic interpretability/component attribution;
- human–AI complementarity.

P-F cannot survive by rediscovering any one of these under a new name.

## 12. Survival and contraction

P-F becomes a paper only if at least one machine-native mechanism demonstrates a reproducible, resource-accounted protected gain beyond both human-mimetic and strongest-parent controls, with causal ablation evidence and cross-domain transfer.

Otherwise:

- architectural findings merge into P-C;
- field implications merge into the flagship;
- benchmark artifacts remain research infrastructure;
- standalone P-F is dropped.
