## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is moving from isolated scientific prediction toward persistent research processes that retrieve sources, construct models, choose experiments, operate instruments and revise claims. The knowledge needed to govern these processes exists across epistemology, control, statistics, causal inference, measurement, numerical analysis, assurance, cognitive science, social epistemology, security and scientific practice—and parts of it are already converging. The unresolved problem is how their validity conditions interact when one executable research episode crosses those boundaries. We propose **Machine Epistemics** as a falsifiable higher-order field hypothesis: a typed federation that keeps parent theories recoverable while testing a small set of cross-parent constraints on how machine-mediated science changes commitments, representations, methods and agendas. Four foundations compete: strongest parent federation, selective interfield theories, an absorptive supertheory, and domain-only federation. The programme succeeds only if the higher theory preserves parent-native validity, generates independently testable consequences and improves protected scientific decisions under matched resources; otherwise it should contract. Machine cognition need not imitate human thought, but every adopted scientific transition requires an external warrant proportionate to its claim.

## From scientific models to executable inquiry

AI has supported scientific prediction, classification, simulation and design for decades, and automated scientific discovery has been an intellectual programme since at least the equation-discovery era [1]. The more consequential transition is that increasing portions of inquiry are assembled into persistent executable systems. A research system can search literature, select tools, write and run code, propose experiments, control instruments, evaluate outcomes, update a model and draft a scientific conclusion [2–5]. The object of evaluation is no longer only a model output. It is a sequence of actions that changes the state of an investigation.

That sequence can fail while every local component appears competent. A retrieval system can return genuine papers but bind a claim to the wrong passage. Several reviewers can agree because they share a foundation model, prompt or source. A numerically stable program can accurately solve a scientifically inadequate mathematical model. A benchmark can be reproducible yet unable to expose the error denied by a broad claim—autonomous-laboratory outputs have been contested on exactly this terrain [6]. A correct abstraction can preserve one decision while destroying another. An exploratory observation can be narrated retrospectively as confirmatory evidence. A technically accessible dataset can remain unauthorized for the proposed use. A signed artifact can be authentic and malicious or scientifically wrong. An external document can act as an untrusted instruction channel to an autonomous tool-using agent [8]. A machine can discover a powerful non-human strategy whose scope is not established by performance alone [9].

These are failures of **scientific-state transition**: errors in changing what an investigation accepts, how it represents a problem, which earlier results remain valid, what it chooses to test, or what action it is permitted to take.

We use *Machine Epistemics* for the proposed science of such transitions in machine-mediated and hybrid research. Its central question is:

> Under what conditions may a machine-mediated research process change a scientific commitment, representation, method or agenda—and which prior commitments must be preserved, reopened or left unresolved when it does?

This is a field hypothesis, not a declaration that the field is already founded.

## Parent sciences are the foundation material

Machine Epistemics has an unusually dense intellectual neighbourhood. That density is an advantage if the parents are reconstructed and absorbed rather than renamed.

Formal learning and computational epistemology study identifiability, convergence and limits of inquiry [10]. Belief revision, truth-maintenance systems and model-based diagnosis study changing commitments, alternative support and responsibility for discrepancy [11–14]. Decision theory, metareasoning, Bayesian experimental design and active learning study which action or computation to perform under uncertainty and resource constraints [15–17]. Control, cybernetics and resilience engineering study observability, feedback, fault response, graceful degradation and recovery [18–20]. Causal transportability and measurement invariance study when judgments survive changes of population, instrument or standard [21, 22], and metrology supplies the traceability discipline that separates an unbroken calibration chain from fitness for purpose [23]. Abstract interpretation and behavioural-equivalence research study sound relations among computational representations [24, 25]. Boundary-object research shows why shared labels and machine-readable mappings can still lose local scientific meaning [26].

Statistics contributes an entire workflow rather than only a final estimator: model construction, implementation, predictive checking, sensitivity, comparison and troubleshooting [27–29]. Simulation-based calibration can reveal whether an inference implementation produces the posterior implied by an encoded model [29]. Numerical analysis separates model adequacy, problem conditioning, discretization, algorithmic stability and finite-precision behaviour [30, 31]. Scientific-software testing addresses the oracle problem through exact, partial, differential, invariant, statistical and metamorphic checks [32, 33]. Severe-testing traditions ask whether a method could actually have exposed the error that a claim denies [34].

Provenance and reproducibility reveal lineage but do not establish scientific correctness [35, 36]. Dependence-aware evidence synthesis asks whether apparent confirmations share data, assumptions, instruments or institutions [37]. Assurance cases and formal argumentation connect bounded claims, premises, inference rules, evidence, attacks and defeaters [38, 39]. Distributed-systems theory separates agreement, safety and liveness from application validity, showing why multi-agent consensus is not scientific truth [40, 41].

Human inquiry supplies further constraints. Pragmatist traditions treat inquiry as a response to genuine doubt and allow the problem formulation itself to change [42, 43]. Knowing-how, tacit skill, situated action and distributed cognition show why explicit instructions can underdetermine scientific competence [44–47]. Metacognition distinguishes task performance from monitoring that performance [48]. Social epistemology studies testimony, criticism, credibility and institutions [49, 50]. Historical and sociological work on experiment, instruments and epistemic cultures shows that material systems and local practices shape what can be observed and compared [51]. Plural and Indigenous epistemologies further expose source forms, standpoint conditions, custodial relations and authorities omitted by an open-data default [52, 53]; the FAIR data principles alone do not supply permission or collective authority for reuse [7].

Security and governance become scientific concerns when research systems act. Secure provenance can establish identity but not truth [54]. Prompt injection and poisoned retrieval can turn evidential content into an unauthorized control channel [8]. Privacy and confidentiality constraints can limit reproducibility.

Machine-native intelligence contributes a different parent set. Automated discovery, theorem proving, program synthesis, self-play, evolutionary search and machine-designed intermediate representations show that valuable computation need not imitate a human verbal trace [1, 9, 55–58]. Difference from humans is not evidence of correctness, but human process imitation should not define intelligence.

A convergent strand is already forming around exactly this surface. Epistemic control has been formulated for machine-learning-based science in terms of tracking and tracing conditions [59]; spec-driven architectures assemble preregistration, registered reports, specification analysis, reproducibility and provenance standards into an epistemic-control architecture for empirical research [60]; provenance-to-trust arguments ground trust in autonomous science in reopenable records of reasoning, action and measurement [54]; research-evaluation studies use epistemic control to analyse where AI assistance shifts authority across research stages [61]; and epistemic engineering treats epistemic change as a property of distributed human–tool systems [62]. The term *machine epistemology* itself is established prior art for the study of machine learning as a form of inquiry [63]. Machine Epistemics therefore cannot claim to have discovered the control problem, the integration architecture, the provenance-to-trust programme or the name. Its proposed residual is narrower: whether the fragments share a stable scientific object—cross-mode scientific-state transitions—whose laws can be tested, and whether any cross-parent constraint earns its keep beyond the strongest parent federation. Philosophy has reached the same junction from the trust side: epistemic dependence on opaque, non-agential AI systems has been argued to outrun current social epistemology [71], while published rebuttals hold that existing frameworks suffice under less demanding accounts of taking responsibility [72]—the precise disagreement that the foundation experiment below renders testable rather than rhetorical. A parallel call to rebuild philosophy of science around AI integration, pluralism and falsifiable AI outputs [73] confirms the convergence while leaving the transition-level question open.

The presence of these parents does not invalidate a new field. Interfield theories are valuable when mature sciences address different aspects of a phenomenon but lack a tested account of their relationship [64]. The challenge is therefore not to find empty intellectual territory. It is to determine whether these sciences can be absorbed into a higher-order theory that preserves their local strengths, explains their relations and generates consequences unavailable from their strongest federation.

## Four competing foundations

A credible field proposal must compare rival interpretations of the same knowledge surface.

### F0: strongest parent federation

On the first interpretation, no new higher-order theory is required. Mature methods remain in native representations and are connected through strong workflow, data and expert-routing interfaces. Metareasoning handles action choice [15]; causal transport handles reuse [21]; metrology handles comparability [23]; assurance handles claims [38]; security handles adversaries [8]; governance handles permission [53].

F0 is not a strawman. It is the scientific lower bound, and recent spec-driven architectures show how far a deliberate composition of these parents already reaches [60]. Its possible weakness is that cross-parent failures can fall between local interfaces and programme-level closure can remain ad hoc.

### F1: selective interfield theories

A second interpretation treats specific relations among parent fields as scientific objects, in the tradition of interfield theories [64]. Typed bridge theories can connect execution and evidence, computation and model validity, transport and revalidation, uncertainty and action, exploration and confirmation, or support and authority.

F1 can be genuinely explanatory without claiming one universal theory. Its risk is bridge proliferation: pairwise theories may not compose, leaving a catalogue of adapters.

### F2: absorptive supertheory

A third interpretation treats machine-mediated inquiry as a class of state-changing scientific systems. Parent sciences remain recoverable native views within a typed federation. A small set of cross-parent constraints aims to explain how a scientific transition is identified, warranted, transported, invalidated, reopened and closed.

F2 is the aspirational Machine Epistemics theory. It does not replace local science. It should defer whenever a specialized parent is sufficient and more efficient. Its distinctive burden is prospective: F2 must generate valid bridge relations, failure boundaries, experiments or decisions beyond F0.

### F3: domain federation without universal transition laws

A fourth interpretation preserves domain-native methods and authority while allowing minimal interoperability through source identity, local boundary objects [26] and translation receipts. It denies that stable cross-domain transition laws exist.

F3 is a serious pluralist control. It is preferable whenever common abstractions erase native meanings or create misleading coherence—a concern raised directly against high-profile autonomous-science claims [6].

## Candidate constraints on scientific-state change

The final theory should be far smaller than the parent atlas. The following are candidates, not settled laws.

### Bind the identity of the transition

A scientific transition must state what changed: subject, problem, criterion, source, evidence, representation, evaluator or authority epoch. Silent mutation cannot inherit the old decision automatically. Belief-revision and truth-maintenance traditions already enforce this locally [11, 12]; the cross-parent question is what happens when the change spans sources, computation and authority at once. Legitimate calibration or conceptual iteration is possible, but it requires an explicit relation to earlier states and a record of which claims remain comparable.

### Factor the warrant and prevent cross-type amplification

A scientific transition can depend on multiple warrant layers:

`governed source -> semantic interpretation -> scientific model -> mathematical/numerical/software realization -> test or oracle -> dependence/robustness -> argument -> transport -> decision and authority`.

These links fail differently. Provenance does not prove the model [35]. Numerical stability does not establish target-world adequacy [30]. A formal proof does not validate an empirical specification. A partial oracle does not support errors it cannot detect [32]. Scientific evidence does not manufacture permission [53].

The candidate higher-order principle is that transition scope cannot exceed the materially weakest necessary warrant link, and one warrant type cannot silently authenticate another. A standalone warrant-factorization paper is justified only if this principle yields a nontrivial theorem or protected decision beyond mature assurance theory [38, 39].

### Conserve type, uncertainty and dependence through composition

Relation family, direction, approximation, uncertainty form, dependence and translation loss must remain visible. Approximate transport cannot become exact through a longer chain without a new warrant [21, 24]. Ignorance cannot become a precise probability without an additional assumption. Several dependent confirmations cannot become independent through reviewer or agent count [37]. Authenticated content cannot become scientifically trustworthy through a signature alone [54].

The implementation remains plural. Causal, measurement, formal, procedural, numerical, semantic and authority relations are not interchangeable merely because they share a common receipt format.

### Require error-detection adequacy

A pass supports only the errors that the evaluator or oracle had a warranted capacity to reveal [32, 34]. Evaluator identity and custody are necessary but insufficient [54]. A benchmark that passes both correct and defective systems cannot justify a broad absence claim. No usable oracle yields an unresolved state, not success.

### Reopen selectively

When a source, computation, relation, evaluator or authority root fails, commitments should reopen only when no complete valid support family survives. Independently supported conclusions remain standing. This connects severe testing [34], assurance [38] and software validation [33] with truth maintenance and belief revision [11, 12]. Provenance-based trust proposals that make the record reopenable are the closest contemporary programme here [54]; the open question is whether reopenability of the record translates into selective reopening of scientific commitments.

### Keep exploration distinct from confirmation

Exploratory and constructive actions can expand the observable or conceptual state of inquiry. They may create an instrument, representation, dataset, model system or problem formulation needed for later science. Their outputs do not thereby confirm an explanation. A scientific controller should distinguish exploratory, discriminative, confirmatory, constructive, calibrative and replicative actions.

This constraint limits overly rigid obligation-driven control: the current vocabulary cannot define every legitimate future action because some actions construct the state in which a better obligation becomes expressible. Pragmatist accounts of inquiry, on which the problematic situation itself evolves, point in the same direction [42, 43].

### Recover parents and defer locally

For parent theory `P_i` and higher theory `T`, the programme requires an embedding and context-bound recovery:

`P_i -> T -> P_i`.

Critical native judgments should be preserved unless explicit counterevidence or scope change justifies revision. Recovery has direct precedent in abstraction theory, where exact embeddings with computable back-translations are the price of sound reasoning at a higher level [24, 25]. A broad theory unable to recover local scientific competence is an overgeneralization. A higher layer activated on every simple case is a performance drag.

### Permit machine-native cognition while requiring external scientific witnesses

Machine cognition may use programs, latent states, graphs, populations, parallel search or learned intermediate languages unlike human reasoning [9, 55–58]. Requiring a human-like chain at every step would constrain the machine to one interface rather than scientific validity.

The corresponding boundary is an external witness proportionate to the adopted transition: relevant source and evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, computational and test validity, relation to prior commitments and authority. This does not require revealing every activation. It requires that non-human computation remain scientifically accountable at the point where its result changes science—a demand that provenance and epistemic-control programmes already voice in their own terms [54, 59–61].

### Close only relative to a declared universe

No finite system can establish exhaustion of all possible knowledge. Closure is relative to a declared search and evaluation universe, its censored routes, residual uncertainty and omission challenges. Route-level stopping research in systematic-review automation owns this problem for one route class [65]; metascience owns the empirical study of the wider system [66]. A material new parent, source mode, evaluator, threat or domain can reopen the terminal. `CANNOT_CHECK` remains a valid scientific outcome.

## How the field could be founded—or fail

A new field should not be established by a manifesto. The programme freezes F0–F3 and compares them under matched sources, tools, compute, human expertise and evaluator access. The precedent to avoid is a field proposal whose neighbourhood was overlooked by its framers [67, 68]; the honest posture is the reverse—map the neighbours first, then test the residual [59–63].

Protected cases combine parent problems that should expose the need—or lack of need—for a higher theory. Examples include consensus on a reproducible but scientifically wrong computation; calibration revision that preserves some historical claims and reopens others; exploration required before a useful hypothesis can be stated; action under imprecise or conflicting uncertainty; semantic transport with a decisive local constraint; scientifically useful but unauthorized data; a machine-native strategy requiring an external witness; hidden shared assumptions among apparently diverse evidence routes; and simple native cases in which the integrated theory should remain inactive.

F2 faces three decisive tests.

### Parent recovery

Known-answer and native-verdict suites determine whether F2 preserves the sciences it claims to absorb. Parent failure cannot be assumed whenever a mapping disagrees; the problem may be a faulty bridge or overgeneralized theory [24].

### Prospective generativity

Before outcome access, each foundation proposes cross-parent predictions, experiments, bridge theorems, failure boundaries, measurements or opportunities. Retrospective explanations cannot count. F2 must generate something scientifically valuable beyond F0 under the same information. Performative-evaluation research is the model: predictions about how the evaluated system responds to deployment are prospective and falsifiable [69].

### Theory dominance

Superiority is a non-compensatory partial order over parent fidelity, protected decision quality, generativity, cross-domain scope, explanatory integration, robustness, efficiency, auditability and recursive extensibility. Critical false completion, source corruption, unsafe transport, criterion drift or authority violation cannot be purchased by broader scope or higher average performance.

F2 becomes an absorptive-supertheory candidate only if it preserves native parents, strictly improves a protected cross-parent problem, demonstrates a generative or integrative gain, survives another domain, attributes the gain to a mechanism and states where local parents remain preferable.

The alternatives are scientifically legitimate. F1 may survive as an interfield theory [64]. F0 may prove sufficient. F3 may be the correct pluralist architecture. The common abstraction may be deceptively unifying or redundant drag. These are not project failures; they are the possible findings of the foundation experiment.

## Machine intelligence beyond imitation

Machine Epistemics should constrain scientific accountability, not prescribe a cognitive architecture. Human inquiry is an invaluable source of mechanisms, counterexamples, values, skills and collaboration interfaces [42–51]. It is not known to be an optimal universal algorithm.

A meaningful comparison separates human-positive, human-neutral and human-trap task strata. Human-positive tasks depend on embodied skill, local meaning, testimony or institutional authority. Human-neutral tasks admit several architectures. Human-trap tasks may reward parallel search, exact replay, program synthesis or non-linguistic representations [9, 55–58, 70]. Human-mimetic, machine-native, hybrid, expert and strongest-parent systems should compete under matched resources.

Difference alone is not the endpoint. A machine-native method must improve a protected scientific transition and survive controls for compute, leakage, numerical accuracy, oracle weakness, benchmark underspecification, semantic mismatch and governance. The best outcome may be a hybrid system or a map of conditions under which different architectures should be used.

## A research programme rather than a closed doctrine

The field, if it survives, should be built around falsifiable specialist sciences rather than one monolithic framework.

- Structural donor discovery tests whether machines can find and correctly absorb remote parent knowledge.
- Context-relative relation science tests when results can be reused, composed or selectively invalidated [21, 24].
- Scientific-control research tests obligations, self-models, exploration, escalation and recovery [15–17, 20].
- Dependence-aware assurance tests corroboration, argument, evaluator adequacy and dynamic response [37–39, 69].
- Opportunity research tests prospective problem finding rather than retrospective interestingness [65, 66].
- Machine-native intelligence research tests non-human mechanisms under external scientific witnesses [9, 55–58].

Each paper has a parent-sufficiency and merge terminal. None receives publication identity simply because it appears in the programme.

A field would also require infrastructure: native parent suites; cross-domain benchmarks; source, computation, evaluator and authority receipts; curricula spanning formal, empirical, social and systems traditions; independent demarcation review; and mechanisms for reopening the foundation when new scientific practices or machine substrates appear.

The central ambition is upward but disciplined: absorb what humanity already knows, preserve the conditions under which it is valid, discover relations the parent sciences leave unresolved, and enable machines to develop forms of scientific intelligence that are not limited to human imitation. The field deserves its name only when this synthesis produces independently testable scientific progress.

## Box 1 | What Machine Epistemics does not claim

- It does not claim that all scientific reasoning has one optimal form.
- It does not claim that human cognition is the target architecture.
- It does not claim that machine-native cognition is superior by being different.
- It does not treat provenance, reproducibility, proof, confidence or consensus as truth [35, 40, 41, 54].
- It does not infer authority, consent or legitimacy from scientific evidence [53].
- It does not claim saturation of all human knowledge.
- It does not require every internal machine state to be human-readable.
- It does not claim to have introduced epistemic control, spec-driven architectures, provenance-grounded trust or machine epistemology [59–63].
- It does not establish a field before F0–F3 are tested.
