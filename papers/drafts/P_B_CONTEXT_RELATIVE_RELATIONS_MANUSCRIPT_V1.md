# Same Position, Different Worlds
## Context-Relative Scientific Relations, Competence and Transport

**P-B manuscript V1 — design manuscript; protected results open**

**Status:** revised after the human-thinking/lived-knowledge saturation reopen. No novelty/publication authority.

## Abstract

Scientific objects can be equivalent for one purpose and non-equivalent for another. Two models may agree observationally yet differ causally; two measurements may be linkable only under anchors and uncertainty; two framework versions may preserve a decision while changing representation; and two written procedures may be propositionally equivalent while requiring different practical competence under changed tools or materials. Mature parent fields already provide specialized relation theories, including bisimulation, experiment comparison, causal and Markov equivalence, measurement invariance, metrological traceability, abstract interpretation, robust/stochastic simulation, ontology alignment, provenance and procedural learning. We investigate whether a shared **typed coordination interface** can preserve those native semantics while making cross-context scientific reuse auditable. A relation receipt `Relation(left,right|context)` binds relation family, registered judgments/actions, source and knowledge form, native witnesses, preserved/lost distinctions, uncertainty/error, competence or affordance conditions where material, counter-probes, epoch/expiry, authority and selective-reopening consequences. The paper explicitly rejects a universal embedding distance and a universal “tacit knowledge” score. Its strongest new hostile case is `TEXT_EQUIVALENT__COMPETENCE_NOT_TRANSFERRED`: the same explicit instruction can fail to transport executable skill. The paper survives only if protected cross-parent evaluation shows that typed coordination reduces unsafe reuse or unnecessary reopening beyond the strongest parent-specific composition while preserving every native parent verdict.

## 1. Why relation type matters

Scientific reuse crosses versions, representations, populations, instruments, environments and procedures. Persistent identity is not enough. A DOI, object ID or lineage chain tells us that two artifacts are historically related; it does not tell us which scientific claims remain valid.

Generic similarity is also insufficient. High textual or embedding similarity does not establish observational, causal, measurement, procedural or authority equivalence. The human/practice reopen makes this especially clear: a procedure can be described in the same words while competence depends on material-state cues, timing, tools, recovery behaviour or social handoffs that the text does not encode.

P-B therefore treats a relation claim as a scientific object whose meaning is always **typed, directional and context-relative**.

## 2. Parent relation families

The paper begins by reconstructing, not replacing:

- exact/behavioral equivalence and bisimulation;
- testing/simulation relations;
- Markov and causal equivalence;
- Blackwell/Le Cam comparison of experiments;
- sufficient statistics;
- abstract interpretation and sound abstraction;
- lumpability and stochastic simulation;
- measurement invariance/equating/linking;
- metrological traceability and uncertainty;
- causal transportability;
- ontology/model evolution;
- provenance/version lineage;
- procedural/imitation learning and skill-transfer evaluation;
- affordance/action-feasibility representations.

Each family answers a different question. P-B's hypothesis is only that executable research systems may need a **coordination layer that prevents those answers from being silently conflated**.

## 3. Relation receipt V1

For objects `K1`, `K2` and context `C`, define:

`R = Relation(K1, K2 | C)`

with fields:

- object identities and source projections;
- relation family/type;
- context/purpose/registered judgments;
- registered actions/interventions;
- knowledge form(s) being transported;
- source/acquisition mode when material;
- native witness/certificate;
- required assumptions/anchors;
- preserved judgments;
- lost or unresolved distinctions;
- error/uncertainty/tolerance;
- counter-probes;
- directionality;
- composition rules;
- competence/affordance conditions where relevant;
- translation/interpretation loss where relevant;
- epoch/validity interval;
- recoverability mapping;
- affected commitments on invalidation;
- authority ceiling;
- terminal status.

Statuses can include exact, conservative, approximate, partial, unresolved, incomparable and competence-unverified.

## 4. Core hypotheses

### H1 — Context necessity

A relation valid for one registered judgment need not be valid for another. Erasing context produces false transport.

### H2 — Loss visibility

Approximate or decision-relative mappings can be useful if removed distinctions and error are explicit. Hidden loss drives false equivalence.

### H3 — Cross-generation comparability is more than identity

Persistent identity can coexist with scientific non-comparability caused by calibration, representation, population, criterion, competence or authority drift.

### H4 — Relation composition must be conservative

A chain inherits the weakest valid semantics. Approximation, unresolved assumptions, translation loss and competence uncertainty cannot disappear through composition.

### H5 — Invalidated relations trigger selective reopening

When a relation expires or an anchor fails, only commitments that depend on that relation without an alternative valid support family should reopen.

### H6 — Knowledge-form mismatch is decision-relevant

`PROPOSITIONAL_EQUIVALENCE` does not imply `PROCEDURAL_EQUIVALENCE`, `PERCEPTUAL_EQUIVALENCE`, `CAUSAL_EQUIVALENCE` or `AUTHORITY_EQUIVALENCE`.

### H7 — Tacit dependency is useful only if predictive

A `TacitDependency` marker has scientific value only if it predicts protected transfer failure or the need for additional demonstration/state information beyond ordinary context features. It is not a metaphysical residual category.

## 5. Knowledge-form transport

### 5.1 Propositional transport

Do two representations preserve the registered statements, predicates or decisions?

### 5.2 Causal/interventional transport

Do observational agreements survive interventions or changed environments?

### 5.3 Procedural/competence transport

Can the target execute the claimed action family under the target's materials, tools, timing and perturbations?

A competence receipt may bind:

`CompetenceReceipt = (task_family, context, action_trace, checkpoints, correction_behavior, demonstrated_range, failure_boundary, source_mode)`.

This is not required for every relation. It becomes relevant only when procedural competence is part of the scientific decision.

### 5.4 Perceptual/state-recognition transport

Does the target preserve the discriminators by which a practitioner or instrument recognizes a state? A verbal label may survive while the sensory/measurement mapping changes.

### 5.5 Authority transport

A result can remain scientifically comparable while adoption, execution or dissemination authority changes. Authority is never inherited from similarity alone.

## 6. Known-answer parent suites

Before any cross-parent claim, P-B must pass native suites from each parent theory.

Examples:

- bisimulation-equivalent and non-equivalent transition systems;
- observationally equivalent but interventionally distinct causal models;
- measurement-linking cases with anchor failure;
- exact versus approximate abstractions;
- provenance-related objects with changed semantics;
- stochastic transport chains with accumulating error;
- procedural tasks whose explicit instructions remain constant while material/tool conditions change.

The coordination interface fails if it changes a native parent verdict.

## 7. Protected cross-parent benchmark

### Case A — Version + measurement drift

A model version changes representation while instrument calibration changes. Persistent identity and lineage remain intact. Test whether an old threshold decision survives.

### Case B — Causal + observational reuse

Two models agree on observed distributions but differ under intervention. A prediction may transfer while a policy does not.

### Case C — Approximate abstraction + authority

Certified abstraction error is sufficient for control but not for a high-precision claim. The lower authority ceiling must remain.

### Case D — Reticulate provenance

A result has several parents and alternative support families. One relation expires; only dependent claims reopen.

### Case E — Multi-epoch composition

One approximate link and one unresolved anchor prevent exact terminal promotion across versions.

### Case F — Instruction equivalent, competence not transferred

The same written procedure is used under changed material/tool/environment conditions. One arm has only text; another has demonstrations/intermediate state cues/recovery cases. Test whether the relation layer correctly refuses `COMPETENCE_EQUIVALENT` when transfer is unverified.

### Case G — Observation/concept/language separation

An underlying observation remains stable while conceptual categories or labels change. The relation receipt should distinguish signal transport from semantic mapping rather than treating one as proof of the other.

### Case H — Distributed handoff

A procedure moves from one human–tool–artifact configuration to another. Text and nominal action sequence are preserved, but a critical state cue exists only in the original interface. Expected terminal: procedural relation unresolved or failed.

## 8. Baselines

1. persistent-ID/provenance only;
2. embedding similarity;
3. one manually selected parent relation family;
4. strongest parent-composed relation product;
5. P-B typed coordination without competence fields;
6. P-B full typed coordination where knowledge-form fields are enabled only for relevant cases.

The parent product may use the same native libraries and expert knowledge. P-B cannot win because it has more metadata.

## 9. Metrics

Primary:

- native parent verdict preservation;
- false exact-equivalence rate;
- unsafe transport rate;
- correct partial/incomparable classification;
- uncertainty/error conservation;
- competence-transfer false-positive rate;
- selective-reopen precision/recall;
- authority/source preservation;
- false `TacitDependency` rate.

Secondary:

- useful reuse rate;
- unnecessary refusal;
- certificate size;
- adjudication burden;
- composition cost;
- added predictive value of competence/tacit fields beyond ordinary context variables.

## 10. Hostile controls

### C1 — Same identifier, changed meaning

Keep identity but change population/calibration. Refuse naive reuse.

### C2 — High similarity, wrong relation

Text/embeddings match; decision semantics differ.

### C3 — Low lexical similarity, exact registered relation

Representations differ but all registered decisions are preserved. Permit declared reuse.

### C4 — Approximate chain laundering

An approximate link inside a longer chain prevents exact terminal promotion.

### C5 — Alternative support survives

One relation fails but an independent support family remains. Global reopening is an error.

### C6 — Context expiry

An old relation certificate is reused after its epoch. Require revalidation or `CANNOT_CHECK`.

### C7 — Correct recipe text, wrong dough state

A nominal procedure is followed while material state differs from the assumptions. Textual conformity is not competence evidence.

### C8 — Tacit mystification

A case is fully explained by observable context/tool variables. Adding a `TacitDependency` flag provides no additional prediction. The flag should contract.

### C9 — Demonstration overfit

A system reproduces a demonstration under identical conditions but fails under minor perturbation. One successful imitation is not a demonstrated competence range.

## 11. Formal directions

### 11.1 Relation refinement under fixed context

Study partial orders among relation receipts when the registered decision interface is fixed.

### 11.2 Conservative composition

Define sufficient conditions for `R12 ∘ R23` to preserve registered judgments while accumulating error, unresolved assumptions and competence conditions.

### 11.3 Selective reopen semantics

Given claims dependent on relation receipts, characterize minimal reopening under invalidation with explicit alternative support families.

### 11.4 Counter-probe completeness

For decision-relative equivalence classes, characterize when a finite probe set can expose every distinction relevant to an expanded interface.

### 11.5 Competence-transfer discriminators

For procedural relations, study the minimum perturbation/demo set needed to distinguish memorized instruction following from robust context-sensitive competence.

## 12. Relationship to Machine Epistemics

P-B tests a reusable scientific-transition law: **what survives a change of representation, context, source or practice?** It should remain independently valuable if the Machine Epistemics field label is rejected.

The human/practice extension does not expand P-B into a general theory of tacit knowledge. It supplies hostile cases showing that some scientifically important relations are about executable competence rather than propositions alone.

## 14. Honest negatives

- strongest parent composition handles all benchmark cases;
- competence fields add context metadata but no predictive value;
- `TacitDependency` cannot be reliably identified;
- relation selection remains too expert-dependent;
- cross-parent composition laws are domain-specific;
- the surviving artifact is only an interchange format.

## 15. Survival gate

P-B survives only if protected evaluation demonstrates at least one cross-parent combination in which typed coordination materially reduces unsafe reuse or unnecessary reopening/refusal relative to strongest parent composition while preserving every native verdict. Any claimed value from competence/tacit fields must survive ordinary-context baselines.

Until those results exist, this is a **design manuscript**.
