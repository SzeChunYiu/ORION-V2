# Finding the Same Scientific Problem in Different Worlds
## Structural Donor Discovery Across Papers, Practices and Representations

**P-A manuscript V1 — design manuscript; protected results open**

**Status:** scientific architecture revised after the human-thinking/lived-knowledge saturation reopen. No novelty/publication authority. The paper survives only through protected incremental evidence over the strongest parent composition.

## Abstract

Scientific research can rediscover problems that already have mature solutions under different terminology, representation, discipline or source ecology. Literature-based discovery, citation/embedding retrieval, computational analogy and formal abstraction already address important parts of this problem. We study a narrower composition question: can a machine reconstruct a donor in its native terms, preserve only the distinctions required by a declared scientific decision interface, search remote domains and non-paper knowledge sources for structurally relevant donors, generate counter-probes that expose invalid analogies, and contract false novelty without laundering a useful practical analogy into scholarly or scientific ownership? The revised pipeline distinguishes **structural donor**, **scientific parent** and **target-valid warrant**. A recipe, repair guide or incident record can donate a control structure without becoming evidence for a target scientific claim; conversely, a remote formal theory can own the scientific problem despite low lexical similarity. The method uses five hard gates: native reconstruction, decision/transition-preserving reduction, typed relation witnessing, counter-probe falsification and target-specific revalidation. The correct terminal may be parent subsumption, useful lossy analogy, practice-derived design donor, no qualifying parent under bounded search, or `CANNOT_CHECK`. The paper survives only if protected evaluation shows material gain in remote-parent discovery, native-judgment preservation, counter-probe value, safe source-mode transfer or correct novelty contraction beyond information- and budget-matched parent compositions.

## 1. The problem is larger than literature retrieval

Scientific vocabulary fragments structurally similar problems. Researchers can overclaim novelty because the nearest parent uses different terminology or arose in another discipline. The converse is equally dangerous: two topics can look similar while differing in the transition, intervention or decision structure that matters scientifically.

The first ORION-V2 donor programme treated this primarily as a literature problem. The human-thinking/lived-knowledge reopen adds a second fragmentation axis: **mode of knowing and mode of acquisition**. Useful structure can live in papers and theorems, but also in standards, manuals, recipes, troubleshooting histories, incident reports, demonstrations, laboratory practice and environmental encounters. These sources cannot be treated as interchangeable. They differ in authority, reliability, explicitness and what they can support.

P-A therefore asks a two-sided question:

1. Can a machine find materially relevant remote donors despite vocabulary, representation and source-mode distance?
2. Can it preserve enough native semantics to reject false analogies and avoid granting the donor more scientific authority than its source warrants?

The desired output is not “similarity”. It is a typed disposition such as:

- `SCIENTIFIC_PARENT_SUBSUMES`;
- `INSTANCE_OF_KNOWN_GENERAL`;
- `STRUCTURAL_DONOR_USEFUL`;
- `LOSSY_RELATION_WITH_COUNTERPROBE`;
- `PRACTICE_DONOR_NOT_SCIENTIFIC_PARENT`;
- `NEAREST_MISS`;
- `NO_QUALIFYING_PARENT_FOUND_UNDER_BOUNDED_SEARCH`;
- `CANNOT_CHECK`.

## 2. Claims under test

### C1 — Native reconstruction before abstraction

A candidate donor is represented in its own vocabulary before any common envelope is compiled. The reconstruction binds entities, relations, states, actions, transitions, admissible judgments, assumptions, source passages or observations, and source/acquisition mode.

This prevents a target vocabulary from silently rewriting what the donor actually claims or does.

### C2 — Decision/transition-preserving minimal envelope

Given a registered interface of judgments and actions, compile the coarsest finite envelope that preserves those registered outputs and successor distinctions to the declared tolerance. The envelope is decision-relative, not universal semantic equivalence.

### C3 — Structural search beyond lexical/citation proximity

Search candidates using typed structural signatures in addition to lexical, citation, graph and embedding routes. Candidate matches remain linked to the native source.

### C4 — Counter-probes are obligations for lost distinctions

For every lossy or many-to-one block that could affect the registered decision, generate a query, intervention or observation capable of distinguishing native states that the envelope collapses.

### C5 — Source-mode conservation

A structural match does not upgrade source authority. A practical procedure may donate an action/checkpoint structure; an oral account may preserve a local observational pattern; an incident report may expose a failure topology. None becomes a scientific parent or target evidence merely because its structure is useful.

### C6 — Conservative target adaptation

Source and target mapping to the same envelope does not authorize target use. Target roles, calibration, tools, competence requirements, authority and epoch are bound separately.

### C7 — Novelty contraction is a positive scientific result

Finding that a proposed contribution is already owned by a mature parent—or is only a practice-derived design pattern rather than a new scientific principle—is successful contraction, not failed publication.

## 3. Native donor object

Let a native donor be

`N = (Q, A, J, T, P, G)`

where:

- `Q` — native states/entities;
- `A` — native admissible actions/interventions;
- `J` — native registered judgments;
- `T` — transition/causal/process structure;
- `P` — provenance/source identity;
- `G` — source/knowledge/acquisition metadata when material.

`G` may identify, for example:

- scholarly theorem/paper;
- institutional standard or checklist;
- manual/recipe;
- demonstration/apprenticeship episode;
- oral/testimonial source;
- incident/failure record;
- environmental observation.

The metadata is not a hierarchy of truth. It records what kind of warrant and failure conditions the source carries.

For registered decision interface `I`, seek `φ_I: Q -> Z` such that:

- every registered judgment is constant on each block of `Z`;
- action-labelled successor blocks are preserved to tolerance;
- every removed distinction is enumerable;
- non-singleton blocks create counter-probe obligations unless irrelevance is itself justified.

A donor relation receipt then binds:

`(source_native, target_native, interface, mapping, preserved, lost, source_mode, authority, counter_probes, status, provenance)`.

## 4. Search universe

The benchmark search universe is deliberately heterogeneous.

### 4.1 Scholarly/codified scientific donors

Papers, monographs, standards, patents, datasets, proofs and formal specifications.

### 4.2 Institutional and engineering practice

Operator handbooks, incident reports, safety procedures, design standards, repair procedures and troubleshooting logs.

### 4.3 Procedural/skill sources

Recipes, demonstrations, expert annotations, laboratory demonstrations and apprenticeship traces where legal/ethical access permits.

### 4.4 Environmental encounter

Unexpected observations, failed experiments, side effects and anomalies that can reveal a structural distinction not present in the current query.

The scientific novelty audit still requires appropriate scholarly/native parent search. Non-paper sources expand the donor universe; they do not prove absence of prior scholarship.

## 5. Pipeline

### Gate 1 — Native reconstruction

Recover the donor's own objects, judgments, transition structure, assumptions and source identity. If the donor is a practical procedure, reconstruct what the competent episode actually depends on rather than assuming the text exhausts the skill.

### Gate 2 — Registered-interface reduction

Compile only distinctions needed for the declared target decision. Expose what is removed.

### Gate 3 — Multi-route donor search

Run lexical/citation/embedding and structural routes under matched access. Include deliberate source-mode variation for designated benchmark strata.

### Gate 4 — Counter-probe falsification

Attack every proposed relation through cases that share words but not structure, share structure but not words, or preserve propositions while losing competence/authority.

### Gate 5 — Target-specific recovery

Rebind target calibration, materials/tools, actors, permissions and assumptions. A relation useful for one decision may remain invalid for another.

## 6. Protected evaluation

### Corpus

A hidden/protected benchmark should span at least four distant scientific/engineering domains and at least two non-paper source modes. Each target has expert-adjudicated:

- local parents;
- remote parents;
- tempting false analogies;
- useful non-scientific structural donors;
- cases with no qualifying donor under the bounded search.

### Arms

1. lexical/BM25;
2. citation-neighborhood;
3. embedding retrieval;
4. expert-domain search;
5. mature analogy/design method;
6. formal/typed structural search;
7. strongest parent-composed union;
8. P-A integrated source-ecology pipeline.

All arms receive matched query/tool/time/source-access budgets where possible. Human expert interventions are counted.

### Strata

- same words / same structure;
- same words / different structure;
- different words / same registered structure;
- local parent only;
- remote decisive scholarly parent;
- useful practice donor that is **not** a scientific parent;
- same written procedure / different competence requirements;
- lossy relation;
- invalid analogy;
- no qualifying parent;
- censored/inaccessible route.

## 7. Primary endpoints

- remote decisive-parent recall;
- false-analogy rate;
- donor-native judgment preservation;
- source/provenance correctness;
- correct source-mode/authority classification;
- correct novelty contraction;
- counter-probe validity;
- target-transfer false-positive rate.

Secondary:

- search cost;
- expert adjudication burden;
- time to decisive donor;
- compression ratio;
- unnecessary target refusal;
- fraction of useful structural donors discovered outside conventional literature.

A gain in recall cannot compensate a native-judgment, provenance or authority failure.

## 8. Hostile controls

### H1 — Lexical trap

Two scientific problems share vocabulary but differ in transition/decision structure. Reject.

### H2 — Remote synonym

Two problems share registered structure with almost no vocabulary. Retrieve without fabricating stronger equivalence.

### H3 — Future-query break

The current envelope merges native states that a later registered query distinguishes. Expose the earlier relation as interface-relative.

### H4 — Same envelope, invalid target

Source and target share an abstract envelope but target calibration/material/tool/assumption differs. Refuse target execution or require revalidation.

### H5 — Recipe as useful donor, not scientific parent

A recipe supplies a reusable structure—intermediate state checks, tolerance bands and recovery—but it is not evidence for a target scientific mechanism. Expected disposition: `STRUCTURAL_DONOR_USEFUL__NO_SCIENTIFIC_PARENT_AUTHORITY`.

### H6 — Demonstration/text mismatch

Two procedures have semantically equivalent text, but expert demonstration reveals different state discriminators. Do not infer competence equivalence from text.

### H7 — Censored novelty route

A scholarly database or community source is inaccessible. Novelty becomes unresolved; the system cannot infer novelty from retrieval failure.

### H8 — Custodial knowledge

A source is scientifically relevant but access/reuse is restricted by legitimate custody or consent. Preserve the restriction; `retrievable` is not equivalent to `free to operationalize`.

## 9. Parent ownership

P-A does not claim generic discovery, analogy, abstraction, knowledge representation or ethnography. Parent families include:

- literature-based discovery and systematic retrieval;
- citation/embedding/graph retrieval;
- structure mapping and computational analogy;
- TRIZ/design-by-analogy;
- institution/theory morphisms;
- abstract interpretation;
- automata minimization/bisimulation;
- causal abstraction and experiment comparison;
- ontology/model transformation;
- procedural learning and imitation learning;
- knowledge-organization and provenance methods.

The residual claim, if any, is the controlled composition of **native reconstruction + cross-source structural search + decision-relative reduction + counter-probes + conservative authority + target adaptation**.

## 11. Honest negative outcomes

- strongest retrieval + analogy + formal abstraction composition matches P-A;
- source-mode expansion finds interesting examples but no protected decision gain;
- practical/tacit donor extraction is too expert-dependent;
- structural search raises false analogy more than it improves recall;
- correct source/authority classification eliminates most apparent transfer gains;
- the useful artifact is a donor-audit protocol rather than a new scientific method.
