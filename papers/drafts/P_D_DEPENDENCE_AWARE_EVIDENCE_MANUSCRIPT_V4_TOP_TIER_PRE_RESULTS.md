# When Agreement Is Not Independent Evidence
## Dependence-Aware Scientific Assurance and Dynamic Evaluation for AI-Mediated Research

**P-D manuscript V4 — top-tier pre-results Article draft**  
**Primary target archetype:** broad AI reliability/evaluation Article  
**Status:** complete evidence-independent manuscript with frozen estimands, result grammar, figures and contraction terminals. No empirical superiority, theory ownership or submission readiness is claimed.

## Abstract

AI-mediated research increasingly aggregates reviewers, models, experiments, retrieval systems and automated evaluators as if agreement were cumulative evidence. That inference fails when apparent confirmations share data, source traditions, prompts, models, instruments, assumptions, software or institutional incentives. It also fails when a correctly identified evaluator cannot expose the error relevant to a claim, when evidence is connected to a conclusion through a defective argument, or when publication and deployment change the evaluation environment. We study an integrated assurance object combining typed support families, source and assumption dependence, robustness/discordance routes, defeasible claim–argument–evidence structure, test and software-oracle adequacy, distributed episode topology, evaluator response and authority. A claim remains supported only if a complete valid support family survives; criticism counts only when its target and uptake are visible; agreement never grants authority. We prospectively compare the method with reviewer vote, provenance deduplication, standard dependence models and the strongest assurance/argumentation federation. Primary outcomes are false corroboration, calibration, genuine-support preservation, defect correction, selective reopening, dynamic-evaluator failure detection, false authority and resource cost. The proposal survives only if it improves these decisions beyond mature parents without becoming performatively conservative or bureaucratic.

## Introduction

Scientific assurance is often summarized by counts: several reviewers agree, multiple papers report the effect, several models reach the same conclusion, or a benchmark repeatedly passes. These counts can be misleading. Reviewers may share the same foundation model or source. Analyses may reuse one dataset or preprocessing stack. Experiments may share a defective calibration. Replications may inherit the same code or measurement assumption. A review panel may communicate before voting. A benchmark can become a training target. An apparently independent criticism may be generated by the system being criticized.

Provenance can reveal lineage, but exact lineage does not interpret the scientific consequence of dependence. A result can be replayed perfectly and remain wrong. Statistical dependence models, meta-analysis, robustness analysis, triangulation, assurance cases, formal argumentation, social epistemology, distributed cognition, performative prediction and security evaluation each address parts of this problem. P-D asks whether their relations must be represented together when machine-mediated research moves from evidence to a scientific or operational decision.

The proposed assurance state has four layers.

First, **support families** represent alternative sufficient routes to a claim. Second, **dependence and robustness routes** expose shared sources, assumptions, instruments, models and interactions. Third, **argument and test adequacy** expose why evidence is supposed to support the claim and what errors the evaluator or oracle could detect. Fourth, **dynamic evaluation and authority** expose whether the act of evaluation changes the environment and whether the system is permitted to act.

The paper tests four claims. Dependence-aware support should reduce false corroboration without erasing genuine independent evidence. Argument and test/oracle adequacy should improve defect correction beyond review count or provenance. Selective reopening should localize invalidation. Dynamic evaluation should detect when static passes no longer transport after strategic or environmental response.

## Results architecture

### Typed support families distinguish repeated evidence from independent corroboration

For claim `q`, sufficient support families are represented as:

`F(q) = {S_1, ..., S_k}`.

A family can contain evidence items, relations, computations, tests, assumptions and authority roots. A claim remains supported after revocation only when at least one complete family remains valid.

Dependence edges include:

- exact duplicate;
- shared raw data or preprocessing;
- shared model/checkpoint/prompt;
- shared retrieval corpus or source tradition;
- shared instrument/calibration;
- shared demonstration/teacher;
- shared hidden assumption;
- sequential derivation;
- communication/social influence;
- common evaluator or institutional incentive;
- unknown dependence.

**Primary result slot PD-R1.** Report false corroboration, calibration and genuine-independent-support preservation for naive count/vote, provenance deduplication, standard dependence parent, strongest F0 federation and P-D.

**Required sentence form:**

> P-D reduced false corroboration from [a] to [b] while preserving [c]% of genuinely independent support gains. Over-conservative errors concentrated in [dependence/unknown stratum], and [parent] remained superior in [case family].

### Robustness and discordance require assumption-level route structure

Methods with different names can share the decisive assumption. Conversely, valid methods can disagree because they answer differently scoped questions. A robustness route records:

`RobustnessRoute = (method, data, instrument, model, assumptions, error_profile, result, scope, dependence)`.

Agreement is credited only relative to declared diversity. Discordance is preserved as a state requiring scope clarification, a discriminator or `CANNOT_CHECK`; it is not averaged automatically.

**Primary result slot PD-R2.** Report shared-assumption detection, robustness classification and discordance handling.

**Required sentence form:**

> Among [n] nominally diverse routes, [x] shared the decisive assumption. P-D correctly preserved [y] scoped disagreements and incorrectly suppressed/aggregated [z].

### Assurance depends on arguments and defeaters, not evidence lists

A scientific assurance case is represented as:

`ScientificAssuranceCase = (`
`bounded_claim, argument_graph, premises, strict_and_defeasible_rules, evidence, assumptions, contexts, attacks, preferences, defeaters, counterclaims, uncertainty, revalidation_plan, authority_ceiling)`.

The system preserves multiple acceptable extensions where the argumentation parent does not select one uniquely. Formal acceptability cannot establish the truth of premises or scientific model adequacy.

Hostile cases include:

- strong evidence supporting a different claim;
- valid components failing to justify a system conclusion;
- circular support;
- hidden preference deciding which argument wins;
- multiple preferred extensions;
- accepted argument with a false premise;
- defeasible empirical inference exported as strict proof.

**Primary result slot PD-R3.** Report defect detection and correction relative to evidence lists, free-form review, native assurance/argumentation parents and P-D.

**Required sentence form:**

> Explicit argument and defeater structure exposed [n] defects missed by evidence-list baselines. The strongest assurance parent [matched/did not match] P-D; integrated value arose from [specific cross-parent link] rather than diagram completion.

### A bound evaluator can still be an inadequate evaluator

Evaluator custody records case, subject, criterion, implementation and epoch before outcome access. P-D additionally records:

`TestSensitivityProfile = (`
`target_claim, relevant_error_classes, evaluator_or_test, perturbation_or_alternative_family, detection_capability, selection_history, blind_spots, scope)`.

For computational claims, `OracleAdequacyProfile` records exact, partial, differential, statistical, invariant or metamorphic oracle type, independence and covered faults.

A pass cannot support an error class the test could not expose. No available oracle yields `CANNOT_CHECK`, not success.

**Primary result slot PD-R4.** Report weak-pass detection, oracle common-mode failures and claim overreach.

**Required sentence form:**

> Although evaluator identity was correct in all cases, [n] passes were non-identifying for the registered error. P-D blocked [x] overbroad transitions and produced [y] false alarms in adequate negative controls.

### Criticism requires uptake rather than reviewer existence

`CriticismReceipt = (`
`claim, critic_identity, critic_dependence, objection, target_assumption, evidence, response, state_delta_or_reason_no_change, authority)`.

Outcomes include revision, weakened support, exposed assumption, new counter-probe, evidence-based rejection, `CANNOT_CHECK` or logged-without-action. The last outcome is not counted as successful independent review.

**Primary result slot PD-R5.** Report defect correction, response quality and false independent-review credit for vote count, free-form critique, review receipt and P-D.

**Required sentence form:**

> Both arms received matched objections, but explicit uptake changed warranted state in [x] versus [y] cases. Review theatre remained in [n] cases where [failure].

### Selective reopening preserves alternative support

When an evidence item, relation, computation, evaluator or authority root fails, the system recomputes complete support families. Claims with surviving independent families remain supported; dependent claims reopen or require revalidation.

**Primary result slot PD-R6.** Report selective-reopening precision/recall and unnecessary invalidation cost.

**Required sentence form:**

> P-D reopened [x/y] claims whose sufficient support was lost and preserved [z] claims with valid alternatives. Global invalidation incurred [cost], whereas no-reopen baselines produced [critical failures].

### Distributed episodes expose hidden state and support loss

Scientific decisions can depend on humans, models, tools, instruments, artifacts and institutional procedures. A distributed cognitive episode records where information enters, changes, is checked, is lost and becomes authoritative.

`DCE = (actors, tools, artifacts, observations, transformations, communications, local_states, support_edges, authority_edges, time)`.

Protected failures include lost warnings at handoff, tacit-interface loss, common-source critics and provenance-perfect semantic bugs.

**Primary result slot PD-R7.** Report distributed-state-loss detection and correct support attribution.

### Evaluation can change the environment

Let evaluator environment be `E_t`. Publication, deployment or evaluation action `a_t` may change it:

`E_{t+1} ~ G(E_t, a_t)`.

Cases include benchmark release and optimization, policy-induced behavioural response, evaluator gaming, measurement intervention and adaptive adversaries. Stable non-performative controls ensure the method does not invent revalidation everywhere.

**Primary result slot PD-R8.** Report post-response invalidity detection, false performativity alarms and criterion/epoch handling.

**Required sentence form:**

> After [intervention], [x] static passes failed to transport. P-D detected [y] and falsely reopened [z] stable controls. The dominant missed response was [type].

### Evidence remains separate from values and authority

Scientific support, evidence certainty, expected effects, values, resources, feasibility, recommendation and legitimate authority remain distinct. A technically accessible source can be unauthorized. High confidence cannot allocate resources or authorize publication/intervention without an external root.

**Primary result slot PD-R9.** Report false authority, governed-data decisions and conditional action under uncertainty.

**Required sentence form:**

> P-D prevented [n] evidence-to-authority promotions while preserving [m] legitimate externally authorized conditional decisions. Errors arose from [authority/custody ambiguity].

### Cost and component attribution

Evaluate FULL, MINUS-support-families, MINUS-dependence, MINUS-argument, MINUS-test/oracle-adequacy, MINUS-uptake, MINUS-dynamics, PARENT replacements, F0 federation and SIMPLE controls.

**Primary result slot PD-R10.** Classify components as necessary, synergistic, contextual, parent-replaceable, redundant drag, harmful or unresolved, with quality–cost curves.

## Discussion

P-D challenges a common intuition in scientific automation: more reviewers, replications, models or tests necessarily mean more assurance. What matters is how information-generating routes differ, what assumptions they share, whether the tests could expose the claimed errors, how objections change warranted state and whether the evaluator remains valid after the system responds.

A positive result would support a composition-level assurance object. Mature parents would still own dependence modelling, robustness analysis, argumentation, assurance notation, severe testing, software oracles, social criticism, performativity and governance. The integrated contribution would be an experimentally demonstrated improvement in transitions that cross those parents—especially false corroboration, argument overreach, local invalidation and authority.

The strongest threat is parent sufficiency. A carefully configured assurance case plus formal argumentation, dependence graph, severe testing and provenance may already make the same decisions. If so, P-D should become a reference implementation or benchmark rather than a new evidence theory.

Another threat is bureaucracy. Explicit graphs and receipts can increase transparency while adding inconsistency, delay or ritual compliance. Structured methods have no presumption of value. Simple exact cases should bypass the full system, and native specialist methods should remain active when sufficient.

Discordance is particularly important. Scientific disagreement can be evidence of error, scope difference or unresolved uncertainty. Forcing agreement would make the system look decisive while destroying information. P-D therefore evaluates correct unresolved states, not only consensus.

Dynamic evaluation extends this caution to time. A benchmark can be valid at release and fail after optimization. A policy can alter behaviour. A disclosed defense can change attacks. Freezing criteria prevents outcome-conditioned rewriting, but the result remains epoch-bound; a new environment receives a new evaluation identity.

## Methods

### Benchmark families

1. duplicate-source reviewers;
2. true independent-support positive control;
3. provenance-perfect scientific bug;
4. alternative support survives revocation;
5. shared calibration or preprocessing;
6. self-review common cause;
7. assumption-level robustness and scoped discordance;
8. criticism uptake versus review theatre;
9. assurance circularity/false premise/multiple extensions;
10. weak evaluator and oracle problem;
11. distributed state handoff;
12. benchmark publication response;
13. stable non-performative control;
14. governed-data and authority cases;
15. adaptive security/evaluator response.

### Arms

- naive count or majority vote;
- provenance-only deduplication;
- standard dependence/meta-analysis parent;
- robustness/triangulation parent;
- assurance/argumentation parent;
- performative/security parent;
- strongest F0 federation;
- P-D FULL;
- component and parent replacements;
- SIMPLE direct controls.

All arms receive matched information and resources. Expert configuration of F0 is prospective.

### Outcomes

Primary:

- false corroboration;
- calibration and genuine-support preservation;
- defect-correction rate;
- weak-pass/oracle-overreach detection;
- selective-reopening precision/recall;
- dynamic invalidity detection;
- false authority;
- critical scientific errors;
- resource-adjusted Pareto status.

Secondary:

- dependence annotation burden;
- argument construction/review burden;
- false performativity alarms;
- unresolved-state correctness;
- distributed-state detection;
- over-conservatism/refusal;
- implementation/failure surface.

### Independence and evaluator custody

Evaluator identities, source access, communication and shared model/data histories are recorded. Same-model reviewers are dependent by default. Independent semantic adjudicators are blinded to arm identity. Where community/custodial authority is material, authorised reviewers control the decision rather than the research system.

### Statistical analysis

Dependence is modelled at case, source, evaluator and route levels. Primary estimands are frozen by benchmark family; results are not pooled across incompatible semantics solely to produce one score. Hard authority and integrity failures are non-compensatory. Stochastic systems receive repeated runs.

### Reproducibility and restrictions

Release code, constructed cases, graphs, prompts/configurations, receipts and analysis where permitted. Sensitive or governed cases use controlled summaries or authorized evaluation; inability to publish raw material is explicit and not treated as evidence absence.

## Limitations frozen before results

- dependence can be unknown or unidentifiable;
- argument graphs can encode reviewer choices;
- severity and oracle adequacy can be difficult to quantify;
- dynamic response is domain-specific;
- governance and authority cannot be standardized universally;
- structured assurance can create burden and false confidence;
- independent expert review can share hidden traditions;
- naturalistic ground truth may remain contested.
