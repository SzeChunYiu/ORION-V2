# Finding the Same Scientific Problem in Different Worlds
## Structural Donor Discovery, Reduction and Conservative Generalization

**P-A manuscript V0**

**Status:** draft candidate; no novelty/publication authority. Absorbs C01 + C03 + C11 by the frozen Wave-06 paper contraction.

## Abstract

Scientific research often rediscovers problems that already have mature solutions under different terminology, representations or domains. Existing literature-based discovery, analogy, graph matching and embedding methods can retrieve related work, while formal abstraction and theory-morphism methods can preserve selected structure once the relevant donor is known. We study a narrower combined question: can a machine reconstruct a source-bound native theory, compile only the distinctions required by a declared scientific decision interface, search remote domains for structurally matching donors, preserve donor-native judgments, generate counter-probes that expose invalid analogies, and contract false novelty without claiming universal equivalence? We propose a donor-faithful pipeline with five hard gates: native reconstruction, decision/transition-preserving reduction, typed relation witnessing, counter-probe falsification and target-specific recovery. The method explicitly allows the correct terminal to be parent subsumption or `CANNOT_CHECK`. The paper's survival condition is protected evidence that this integrated process improves remote-parent discovery, donor-judgment preservation, counter-probe quality or correct novelty contraction over the strongest composition of lexical/citation/embedding search, analogy methods and formal abstraction tools under matched budget.

## 1. Problem

Scientific vocabulary fragments structurally similar problems. A researcher can therefore overclaim novelty because the nearest parent is remote in language or field. The converse is equally dangerous: a superficial analogy can collapse scientifically important differences.

P-A treats donor discovery as a **two-sided control problem**:

1. maximize recall of materially relevant remote parents;
2. minimize false analogy and invalid transfer by preserving native judgments and exposing lost distinctions.

The target is not a universal ontology. It is a source-bound method that can say:

- the candidate is subsumed by a known parent;
- the candidate is a special case or instance;
- the candidate has a useful but lossy structural relation;
- no adequate parent was found under the bounded search;
- the comparison cannot be assessed.

## 2. Contributions under test

### C1 — Native theory reconstruction before abstraction

A donor is represented in its own scientific vocabulary before any common envelope is compiled. The reconstruction binds entities, relations, states, transitions, admissible judgments, assumptions and source passages.

### C2 — Decision/transition-preserving minimal envelope

Given a registered interface of judgments/actions, compile the coarsest finite envelope that preserves those registered outputs and action-labelled successor blocks.

This is deliberately decision-relative, not universal semantic equivalence.

### C3 — Structural donor search

Search candidates using typed structural signatures in addition to lexical/citation/embedding routes. Structural matches must remain linked to native donor sources.

### C4 — Counter-probe generation

For every lossy or many-to-one block, generate a probe/query/intervention capable of distinguishing at least two native states that the envelope collapses.

### C5 — Conservative target adaptation

A source and target mapping to the same envelope does not authorize target use. Target roles, calibrations, tests, authority and epoch must be bound separately.

### C6 — Novelty contraction as a positive result

The system treats `SUBSUMED`, `INSTANCE_OF_KNOWN_GENERAL`, `NEAREST_MISS`, or parent-product ties as successful scientific contraction rather than failed paper generation.

## 3. Parent ownership

P-A must not claim generic discovery or abstraction. Parent families include:

- literature-based discovery and systematic retrieval;
- citation/embedding/graph retrieval;
- structure mapping and computational analogy;
- TRIZ/design-by-analogy and engineering design methods;
- institution/theory morphisms;
- abstract interpretation;
- automata minimization/bisimulation quotients;
- model/ontology transformation and bidirectional transformation;
- causal abstraction and sufficient-statistic/experiment-comparison theories.

The residual claim is the *controlled composition* of native reconstruction, remote structural search, decision-relative reduction, counter-probes, novelty contraction and target adaptation.

## 4. Formal object

Let native theory `N = (Q, A, J, T, P)` contain native states `Q`, actions `A`, registered judgments `J`, transitions `T` and source/provenance `P`.

For registered interface `I`, find partition/abstraction `φ_I: Q -> Z` such that:

- every registered judgment is constant on each block of `Z`;
- action-labelled successor blocks are preserved to the declared tolerance;
- every removed distinction is enumerable;
- non-singleton blocks admit counter-probe obligations unless the interface explicitly proves irrelevance.

A donor relation between source and target is then not `similarity(source,target)`, but a typed receipt containing:

`(source_native, target_native, interface, mapping, preserved, lost, counter_probes, status, provenance)`.

## 5. Donor-search protocol

### Corpus

Use a hidden/protected corpus spanning at least four distant scientific/engineering domains. Each target problem has expert-adjudicated near parents, remote parents, tempting false analogies, and cases with no qualifying donor.

### Arms

1. lexical/BM25;
2. citation-neighborhood;
3. embedding retrieval;
4. expert-domain search;
5. mature analogy/design baseline;
6. typed structural search;
7. strongest parent-composed union;
8. P-A integrated pipeline.

### Budget matching

Freeze query/tool/time/compute budget. Count human expert interventions. Do not allow P-A more source access than baselines.

### Strata

- same words / same structure;
- same words / different structure;
- different words / same registered structure;
- local parent only;
- remote decisive parent;
- lossy relation;
- invalid analogy;
- no parent under bounded search.

## 6. Endpoints

Primary:

- remote decisive-parent recall;
- false-analogy rate;
- donor-native judgment preservation;
- source-passage/provenance correctness;
- correct novelty contraction;
- counter-probe validity;
- target-transfer false-positive rate.

Secondary:

- search cost;
- expert adjudication burden;
- time to decisive parent;
- compression ratio of the decision envelope;
- unnecessary target refusal.

Non-compensatory: a gain in remote recall cannot compensate a donor-judgment or source-identity failure.

## 7. Key hostile controls

### H1 — Lexical trap

Two problems share vocabulary but differ in transition/decision structure. P-A must reject the analogy.

### H2 — Remote synonym

Two problems share decision structure but almost no vocabulary. Structural search should retrieve the donor without fabricating stronger equivalence.

### H3 — Future-query break

Two native states are merged by the current decision interface. A new query distinguishes them. The system must expose that the old envelope was interface-relative.

### H4 — Same envelope, invalid target

Source and target share an envelope but target calibration or assumption fails. Target execution must be refused.

### H5 — Novelty cannot be assessed

Retrieval route is censored. The system may not convert `CANNOT_ASSESS` into novelty.

## 8. Expected negative outcomes

The strongest valid result may be:

- parent retrieval + formal abstraction already matches P-A;
- structural search improves recall but increases false analogy;
- automated reduction is useful only as an artifact, not new science;
- counter-probes are too expensive or expert-dependent;
- cross-domain target adaptation cannot be reliably judged;
- P-A contributes only novelty contraction, not discovery gain.

These outcomes should contract claims rather than be hidden.

## 9. Relationship to Computational Scientific Control

P-A supplies one CSC subsystem: **search-space expansion under source/semantic preservation**. It tests whether a scientific controller can change vocabulary/domain while keeping native decision semantics and refusing unjustified transfer.

The paper should remain independently readable without the CSC field claim.
