# Machine Epistemics
## Toward a Science of AI-Driven Inquiry and Scientific Change

**Flagship Perspective V10 — project-wide evidence-independent manuscript**  
**Status:** conceptual/scientific content complete before protected field-evidence and external demarcation.  
**Primary target hypothesis:** Nature Machine Intelligence Perspective, subject to current content-type and policy fit.  
**Field status:** hypothesis, not established discipline.

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is shifting from isolated prediction toward persistent research systems that retrieve evidence, construct models, choose experiments, alter representations, operate instruments and revise scientific claims. The relevant knowledge is distributed across epistemology, statistics, causal inference, control, metareasoning, measurement, formal methods, assurance, security, cognitive science and scientific practice. We propose **Machine Epistemics** as a falsifiable higher-order research programme for the scientific transitions that connect these local competencies. Its candidate object is not one universal reasoning algorithm but an ecology of changes in evidence, commitments, representations, methods, experiments and research agendas, together with constraints on when those changes are warranted, transported, preserved or reopened. Four foundations compete: strongest parent federation, selective interfield theories, an absorptive transition theory and domain-only federation. A new field is justified only if cross-transition constraints preserve parent-native validity and generate protected scientific decisions beyond the strongest parent composition. Otherwise Machine Epistemics should contract to an interdisciplinary programme or integration engineering. A further frontier is whether machines can learn reusable operators of scientific development itself from populations of successful and failed research trajectories without turning historical popularity into scientific truth.

# From models to executable inquiry

AI has long contributed predictions, classifications, simulations and candidate designs. The scientific object is now changing. Increasingly, an AI-enabled research process can search literature, select tools, write and run code, propose or prioritize experiments, operate instruments, compare models, update a scientific claim and communicate a result [1–5]. The unit that must be evaluated is therefore not only a model output. It is a sequence of state-changing actions inside an investigation.

Such a sequence can fail even when every local component looks competent. A genuine paper can be attached to the wrong claim. Several agents can agree because they share a model, corpus or evaluator. A numerically stable program can solve the wrong scientific model. A reproducible benchmark can lack sensitivity to the failure denied by a broad conclusion [6]. A valid abstraction for one decision can be invalid for another. A formally correct proof can certify the wrong empirical specification. A technically accessible dataset can remain unauthorized for a proposed use. A signed artifact can be authentic yet scientifically wrong or operationally malicious. A machine may discover a high-performing non-human representation whose scope is not established by performance alone [8,9].

These are not simply “reasoning mistakes.” They are failures in **scientific change**: what an investigation accepts, how it represents a problem, what it chooses to measure or test, which earlier conclusions remain valid, and what it is permitted to do.

We use *Machine Epistemics* for the proposed science of these changes in machine-mediated and hybrid inquiry. The central question is:

> **Under what conditions may a machine-mediated research process change a scientific commitment, representation, method, experiment or agenda—and what must be preserved, reopened or left unresolved when it does?**

The term names a field hypothesis, not a founded field.

# A dense parent neighbourhood is a feature, not a defect

Machine Epistemics sits inside a crowded intellectual landscape. Any serious proposal must begin by conceding that landscape rather than renaming it.

Formal learning and computational epistemology study identifiability, convergence and limits of reliable inquiry [10]. Belief revision, truth-maintenance and model-based diagnosis study changing commitments, alternative supports and responsibility for discrepancy [11–14]. Decision theory, metareasoning, experimental design and active learning study which action or computation should be performed under uncertainty and cost [15–17]. Control, cybernetics and resilience engineering study observability, feedback, fault response and recovery [18–20]. Causal transportability and measurement invariance formalize conditions under which judgments survive changes of population, intervention or instrument [21–23]. Abstract interpretation and behavioural equivalence study sound relationships between representations [24,25]. Boundary-object research explains why shared labels and mappings can coexist with incompatible local meanings [26].

Statistics supplies workflow-level disciplines of model construction, checking, sensitivity, comparison and repair [27–29]. Numerical analysis separates scientific model adequacy from conditioning, discretization, algorithmic stability and finite precision [30,31]. Scientific-software testing addresses partial and absent oracles through invariants, metamorphic relations and differential tests [32,33]. Severe-testing traditions ask whether an evaluator could actually have exposed the error a claim denies [34].

Provenance and reproducibility expose lineage but do not establish scientific validity [35,36]. Dependence-aware evidence synthesis asks whether apparently distinct confirmations share data, assumptions, instruments or institutions [37]. Assurance and argumentation connect claims, assumptions, evidence, attacks and defeaters [38,39]. Distributed-systems theory distinguishes consensus from application validity [40,41]. Social epistemology, tacit knowledge, situated action and distributed cognition supply additional constraints on testimony, competence, coordination and local scientific practice [44–51]. Data governance makes clear that availability and scientific usefulness do not automatically create permission [52,53]. Security research adds the fact that scientific content can itself become an untrusted instruction channel in tool-using agents [8].

A related AI-and-science literature is already moving toward epistemic control, provenance-grounded trust and spec-driven research architectures [54,59–63]. The term *machine epistemology* also predates this programme [63]. Machine Epistemics therefore cannot claim discovery of epistemic control, scientific provenance, assurance integration or the general idea that machine learning is inquiry.

The proposed residual is narrower and testable: **do multiple mature parent sciences share a stable higher-order object—scientific transitions across modes—and do any constraints over those transitions improve protected scientific decisions beyond the strongest parent federation?**

# Four competing foundations

A field proposal should specify what would make it unnecessary. We therefore treat four foundations as genuine competitors.

## F0 — strongest parent federation

Mature methods remain native. Metareasoning chooses actions; causal inference and transport govern intervention claims; metrology governs comparability; assurance governs arguments; security governs adversarial channels; domain science governs substantive validity. Carefully engineered interfaces connect these components.

F0 is the scientific lower bound. If it reproduces every protected Machine-Epistemics decision at equal or lower cost, the higher theory has no independent residual.

## F1 — selective interfield theories

Some cross-parent relations may deserve theories of their own: execution-to-evidence, measurement-to-transport, uncertainty-to-action, support-to-authority, or exploration-to-confirmation. This follows the tradition of interfield theory [64]. F1 is attractive if a small collection of bridge theories is enough.

## F2 — absorptive transition theory

The strongest Machine-Epistemics hypothesis is that machine-mediated science forms a class of state-changing systems whose local parents can be embedded and recovered inside a typed higher-order theory. F2 should activate only when cross-parent structure matters. Its burden is prospective: it must generate valid decisions, experiments, bridge constraints or failure boundaries that F0/F1 do not.

## F3 — domain federation without stable universal transitions

A pluralist alternative denies that one higher transition theory is scientifically useful. Domains retain native semantics and are connected only through limited boundary objects, provenance and explicit translation. F3 wins whenever common abstractions erase decision-relevant local structure.

No internal architectural preference is allowed to decide between F0–F3.

# The transition ecology

The programme originally emphasized changes in scientific commitments. The broader V2 research reveals a more useful candidate object: an **ecology of scientific transitions**. These transition classes need not share one implementation or one mathematical formalism.

## 1. Knowledge and source transitions

A system encounters a paper, dataset, observation, expert statement or instrument reading and changes what information is available. The scientific issue is not merely retrieval. Source identity, semantic binding, provenance, dependence, permission and scope must remain distinguishable.

A retrieved item is an observation to interpret, not a truth token.

## 2. Relation and transfer transitions

Knowledge from one domain may be structurally useful in another. But structural relevance is plural: isomorphism, behavioural equivalence, causal transport, decision sufficiency, measurement comparability, safe approximation and rough analogy are different relations with different failure conditions.

A transfer must therefore identify the relation, context, preserved judgments, losses and counter-probes. Generic semantic similarity cannot silently stand in for causal or formal transport.

This connects classical structure mapping and analogy [74], anti-unification and abstraction [75], information-theoretic compression [76], formal concept analysis [77], causal transport [21] and abstraction theory [24]. The Machine-Epistemics question is not whether these methods exist, but whether a protected controller can choose and compose them without flattening their native semantics.

## 3. Conceptual and representation transitions

Scientific progress sometimes requires not a new answer but a new distinction. A machine may need to split a concept, merge categories, reparameterize a model, introduce a bridge, change an observable or revise the representation in which a question is posed.

A representation change earns scientific credit only when it does more than introduce vocabulary. It should preserve predecessor cases that remain valid, make its losses explicit, generate a new checkable consequence or solve a witnessed representational obstruction, and survive counterexamples.

The minimal diagnostic is a **representation collision**: two cases that the current representation treats as the same but for which the registered scientific decision must differ. A collision proves only that the representation is too coarse for that decision; it does not prove that a new formalism is required.

## 4. Method and formalism transitions

When local search, one missing observation, a scope restriction or a representation change is insufficient, a scientific system may need a new operator, measurement method, instrument, primitive, relation or formal calculus.

Formalism genesis is therefore an escalation of last resort. The order should be:

`strongest parent -> missing observation/variable -> local scope repair -> representation change -> candidate new formal object`.

A candidate formalism should provide semantic models, recover valid predecessor theory, generate checkable consequences and survive model/countermodel search. Formal novelty that changes no protected decision is notation, not new science.

## 5. Action and experiment transitions

Scientific systems choose observations, queries, proofs, simulations, measurements, interventions and constructive actions. Classical experimental design, metareasoning, active learning and control already own much of this space [15–20].

The cross-transition issue is how action choice responds when the blocker is itself uncertain: missing evidence, invalid evaluator, non-identifiability, wrong representation, wrong method, or unavailable authority imply different next actions.

Exploration must also remain distinct from confirmation. Constructing an instrument, model system, dataset or representation can be scientifically valuable without confirming the hypothesis that motivated it.

## 6. Evidence and evaluator transitions

A result changes meaning when its support, dependence structure, evaluator, calibration or environment changes. The same numerical score under a different evaluator epoch is not automatically the same scientific evidence.

Machine-mediated science must therefore ask not only whether a test passed, but what error the test could detect. Apparent corroboration must also remain dependence-aware: ten agents sharing a model, corpus and evaluator do not provide ten independent confirmations.

When support fails, reopening should be selective. A conclusion with an independent sufficient support route should remain standing when another route is defeated.

## 7. Agenda and meta-policy transitions

Scientific systems also change what they choose to work on. They may redirect a project, reformulate a problem, switch from direct solution to transfer search, decide that a new measurement is needed or abandon an underdetermined question.

This level includes problem finding and opportunity discovery, but novelty, surprise and diversity are not equivalent to scientific value. Adoption of a research agenda also has an authority boundary: a machine may propose an opportunity without possessing institutional authority to set the agenda.

# Candidate cross-transition constraints

The transition ecology is useful only if some constraints recur across several transition classes. Current candidates remain hypotheses.

## Bind identity before inheriting validity

Every transition should state what changed: subject, problem, criterion, source, evidence, representation, method, evaluator, authority or epoch. Silent mutation cannot automatically inherit the old conclusion.

## Do not amplify one warrant type into another

A scientific transition often depends on a chain:

`source -> interpretation -> model -> computation -> test -> robustness/dependence -> transport -> decision -> authority`.

Provenance does not prove the model [35]. Numerical stability does not prove target-world adequacy [30]. Formal derivation does not validate an empirical specification. Statistical support does not manufacture permission.

A candidate higher-order constraint is that adopted claim scope cannot exceed a materially necessary weak link unless a new warrant repairs it.

## Preserve relation type, uncertainty and dependence

Approximate transport does not become exact through composition without an additional warrant. Unknown dependence does not become independence because agents have different names. Ignorance does not become a calibrated probability by formatting it as one.

## Require evaluator sensitivity

A pass supports only error classes the evaluator had a warranted capacity to expose [32,34]. Failed or unavailable oracles support an unresolved state, not success.

## Reopen selectively

When a source, relation, computation, evaluator or authority root fails, reopen only commitments whose complete valid support families have been defeated. This is a bridge among truth maintenance, severe testing, assurance and provenance [11,12,34,38].

## Recover parents and defer locally

A higher theory should preserve critical parent-native judgments. If a mature parent solves a case with less cost or stronger guarantees, the higher layer should defer. Machine Epistemics is not justified by always doing more work.

## Keep machine-native cognition externally witnessable

Internal computation need not imitate human verbal reasoning. Machine-native programs, latent structures, parallel search or learned representations are acceptable if the scientific transition they support has an external witness proportionate to the claim [9,54–58].

## Close only relative to a declared universe

No finite search establishes universal exhaustion. Scientific closure is relative to declared source modes, search routes, evaluators, domains, resources and omission challenges. `CANNOT_CHECK` is a valid terminal.

# Learning scientific development itself

A further question is more ambitious: can machines learn not only domain knowledge but **how scientific development tends to proceed**?

Science-of-science research has identified population-level regularities in teams, novelty, citation, interdisciplinarity and research dynamics [66,78–80]. Such regularities are not themselves scientific truth criteria. Citation impact, prizes and historical fame are noisy social outcomes, and failed or unpublished research is systematically missing.

The V2 programme therefore treats scientific-development learning as a prospective, bias-aware hierarchy rather than a library of famous “breakthrough recipes.”

## Level D0 — research episodes

Represent source-bound scientific trajectories including successful, failed, partial, abandoned and redirected paths: problem states, methods, data, experiments, revisions, resources, corrections and outcomes.

## Level D1 — discovered development operators

Infer recurring transitions from populations rather than hard-coding them. Candidate operators might include decomposition, counterexample-guided repair, representation change, abstraction, measurement, instrument construction, transfer, experiment redesign or scope restriction—but the corpus must be able to discover a different vocabulary.

Each operator needs preconditions, contraindications, failure modes and resource consequences.

## Level D2 — operator families and meta-policies

Learn when to choose or sequence operators. A strategy such as “explore then exploit” should be a hypothesis to test, not a universal rule imported from management or reinforcement learning [79].

## Level D3+ — recursively higher principles

A higher abstraction is retained only if it improves held-out prediction, cross-field or cross-epoch transfer, prospective research decisions, generativity, resource use or compression without critical loss.

The recursion stops when a new abstraction level adds no material protected residual under new domains, epochs and hostile omissions. The strongest terminal is a **recursive stability candidate**, not ultimate truth.

The programme's current SD10 corpus work—multiple lawful source modes, explicit missingness and bias auditing—establishes infrastructure for this question, not a meta-scientific law. Later SD20–SD80 results must determine whether any operator or meta-policy deserves a standalone paper.

# How Machine Epistemics could be founded—or falsified

A field cannot be founded by conceptual coherence alone. The programme therefore treats the four foundations as prospectively competing models.

## Test 1 — parent recovery

Known-answer and native-verdict suites ask whether a higher representation preserves the science it claims to absorb. Failure of a bridge does not prove failure of the parent.

## Test 2 — protected cross-transition decisions

Cases combine multiple scientific modes: reproducible but scientifically wrong computation; changing calibration that selectively invalidates earlier results; hidden dependence among apparent confirmations; an exploratory action required before a useful hypothesis can be expressed; scientific evidence without action authority; a machine-native strategy requiring an external witness; or a representation relation that is safe for one query and unsafe for another.

F2 earns credit only if it changes a registered scientific decision beyond the strongest information-matched F0/F1 composition without critical regression.

## Test 3 — prospective generativity

Before outcome access, foundations propose predictions, experiments, counter-probes, bridge theorems, measurements or new decision boundaries. Retrospective explanation is insufficient.

## Test 4 — cross-domain recurrence

A single-domain improvement supports a scoped engineering claim. A field-level residual requires recurrence across materially different domains and independent evaluation.

## Test 5 — resource adjustment and local deference

A higher layer that ties F0 while consuming substantially more compute, expert time or implementation complexity should contract. Simple native cases are explicit negative controls.

## Test 6 — external demarcation

Independent reviewers from parent disciplines decide whether the surviving object is better described as a new field, an interdisciplinary research programme, a subfield of an existing parent or integration engineering. Internal reviewers cannot self-grant the field.

Valid outcomes include:

`CANDIDATE_FIELD_DEMARCATION_SUPPORTED`, `USEFUL_INTERDISCIPLINARY_RESEARCH_PROGRAMME`, `SUBFIELD_OF_EXISTING_PARENT`, `INTEGRATION_ENGINEERING_ONLY`, `RENAME`, or `CANNOT_CHECK`.

# What a machine-epistemic system would look like

The framework is intentionally implementation-plural. A minimal system requires interfaces rather than one monolithic algorithm.

1. **Identity and problem contract** — what problem, criterion, subject and epoch are being addressed?
2. **Plural scientific state** — claims, alternatives, open obligations, uncertainty and blockers.
3. **Evidence and dependence** — source identity, interpretation, support topology and defeaters.
4. **Relations and representations** — typed transport, preserved decisions, losses and counter-probes.
5. **Actions and execution** — queries, proofs, simulations, experiments, constructions and receipts.
6. **Concept/method revision** — when lower-level repair fails, how is representation or method changed?
7. **Evaluation and reopening** — what can the evaluator actually detect, and which commitments must reopen?
8. **Learning and meta-policy** — what methods or development operators are reusable under which conditions?
9. **Authority boundary** — what is the machine allowed to adopt, publish or act upon?

LLMs, theorem provers, causal models, retrieval systems, simulators, program synthesis, laboratory controllers and humans can all populate these interfaces. None is the field by itself.

# Failure modes of the programme

A higher-order programme can fail in several scientifically useful ways.

**Parent sufficiency.** F0 or an F1 bridge composition matches every protected decision more simply.

**Vocabulary inflation.** New terms redescribe old mechanisms without a protected consequence.

**Overconservatism.** The control layer rejects legitimate scientific updating or becomes dominated by `CANNOT_CHECK` despite sufficient evidence.

**State explosion.** Explicit alternatives, dependencies and obligations become computationally prohibitive without decision-safe compression.

**False formalism invention.** The system creates new primitives when a local patch, missing variable or existing parent formalism was sufficient.

**False transfer.** Structural similarity creates unsafe cross-domain reuse.

**Meta-science leakage.** Learned “scientific development” principles reduce to field identity, citation popularity, institution or era.

**Authority confusion.** Strong evidence is treated as permission to act.

**Machine-native opacity.** An internal strategy improves benchmarks but cannot support an external witness appropriate to the scientific transition.

Every one of these is a valid contraction route rather than a reason to protect the theory.

# A research programme rather than a manifesto

The decisive Machine-Epistemics programme is therefore not “build ORION and show it wins.” It is:

1. reconstruct strongest parents;
2. freeze F0–F3 and the information/resources they receive;
3. construct known-answer and hostile transition cases;
4. preserve parent, simple-control, null and adverse outcomes;
5. test cross-transition constraints and local deference;
6. test prospective generativity;
7. repeat in materially different scientific domains;
8. study learned development operators only with population bias controls and held-out fields/epochs;
9. require independent field demarcation;
10. contract the programme whenever a simpler parent explanation suffices.

The programme does not seek one final calculus of science. Different scientific transitions may remain governed by different mathematics. The candidate field-level object is the **responsibility for scientific change across those mathematics**.

# Conclusion

AI-driven science makes an old epistemological problem executable: not merely *what should we believe?*, but *what may a machine-mediated scientific process change, on what warrant, and what follows from that change?*

Mature sciences already answer pieces of this question. Machine Epistemics is justified only if the relationships among those pieces form a stable and useful scientific object. The strongest present hypothesis is not a universal reasoning architecture but a transition ecology covering knowledge acquisition, structural transfer, conceptual and formal change, experiment selection, evidence revision, scientific memory and research meta-policy.

That hypothesis should be easy to kill. If strongest parent federation handles the same cases, use it. If only local bridge theories are needed, prefer them. If common abstractions erase domain semantics, choose plural federation. If recursive science-of-science learning only rediscovers citation and field biases, discard it.

But if cross-transition constraints repeatedly preserve parent competence, diagnose failures that local methods miss, generate new protected scientific decisions and support machine-native cognition without surrendering external warrant, then the object may deserve a field of its own.

The aim is therefore not to declare Machine Epistemics. It is to make the hypothesis precise enough that science can decide whether it should exist.
