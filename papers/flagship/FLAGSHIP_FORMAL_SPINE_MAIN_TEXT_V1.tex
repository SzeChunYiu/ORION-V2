\subsection{Formal object of Machine Epistemics}\label{formal-object-of-machine-epistemics}

For a bounded research episode, let
\[
E_t=(P_t,S_t,O_t,A_t,R_t,M_t,V_t,X_t,H_t,K_t),
\]
where $P_t$ binds the problem and criterion; $S_t$ the plural scientific state; $O_t$ unresolved obligations; $A_t$ admissible actions; $R_t$ resources; $M_t$ evidence, provenance and dependence; $V_t$ evaluator contracts; $X_t$ observations; $H_t$ transition history; and $K_t$ the external authority boundary. When decision-relevant, extend this base state as
\[
\widetilde E_t=(E_t;\Gamma_t,\Pi_t,\mathcal A_t),
\]
where $\Gamma_t$ is the current generative regime, $\Pi_t$ the locality/perspective frame, and $\mathcal A_t$ the current bounded epistemic atlas.

The elementary object of study is then a scientific-state transition
\[
T_t:(\widetilde E_t,a_t,x_t)\mapsto(\widetilde E_{t+1},\rho_t),
\]
where $\rho_t$ is a receipt binding the action, observation, interpretation, evidence delta, assumptions, obligations discharged or created, commitments preserved or reopened, replay identity and authority ceiling. Machine Epistemics asks whether this transition is warranted; it does not replace the native algorithm that produced $a_t$.

Capability and transport claims are local to a declared context
\[
C=(\text{environment},\text{task family},\text{resources},\text{system boundary},
\text{substrate/interface},\text{timescale},\text{criterion}).
\]
Accordingly,
\[
\text{successful execution}\not\Rightarrow\text{warranted scientific transition}.
\]
Likewise, if $\mathfrak E^{\star}$ denotes only an ideal class of possible epistemic regimes,
\[
\mathcal A_t\neq\mathfrak E^{\star},
\qquad
\text{pairwise compatible}\not\Rightarrow\text{global section witnessed}.
\]
These are definitions and scope boundaries. The conservation, transport, reopening and closure rules proposed below remain candidate constraints to test, not established universal axioms.
