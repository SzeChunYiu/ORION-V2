# Flagship Box — Recursive Scientific Development

Machine Epistemics need not stop at modeling one scientific transition. A deeper empirical programme treats scientific development itself as a population of trajectories and asks which transition mechanisms recur, under what conditions they help, and when they fail.

Let `e_i` denote a source-bound scientific-development trajectory and `A_k` a level-`k` abstraction over such trajectories. The recursive hypothesis is

\[
A_{k+1}=G_k(A_k;D),
\]

where `G_k` proposes a higher-order regularity from corpus `D`. A higher level is retained only if it adds held-out predictive, transfer, prospective-decision or resource value, or compresses lower-level structure without losing registered critical distinctions.

Possible levels include episodes, development operators, operator families, meta-policies and higher principles. Their content is learned rather than fixed. Population-scale observations such as atypical knowledge combinations, exploration/exploitation sequences or team-size effects are candidate regularities to reproduce and challenge, not universal rules.

A bounded recursive-stability candidate satisfies approximately

\[
A^*\simeq G(A^*)
\]

under declared held-out domains, epochs and hostile omission tests. This is not an `ultimate truth` label: new science, new source modes or new counterexamples may reopen it.

The causal boundary is essential. Bibliometric regularity is observational:

\[
P(\text{impact}\mid h)\neq P(\text{valid breakthrough}\mid do(h)).
\]

A learned development principle earns intervention status only if prospectively applying it changes a protected scientific outcome beyond strongest meta-science and parent controls.
