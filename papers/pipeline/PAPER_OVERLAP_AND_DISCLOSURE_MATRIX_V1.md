# 3. Shared code/data rules

Sharing infrastructure is allowed and expected, but simultaneous manuscripts must disclose when they use:

- the same source corpus;
- the same task family;
- the same evaluator;
- the same agent/model outputs;
- the same formal fixtures;
- the same human adjudicators.

A distinct analysis of identical data is not automatically a distinct paper.

Before submission, mark every figure/table/result as:

```text
UNIQUE_TO_PAPER
SHARED_METHOD_ONLY
SHARED_DATA_DISTINCT_ESTIMAND
PREVIOUSLY_PUBLIC_RESULT_SUMMARIZED
RELATED_MANUSCRIPT_RESULT__DISCLOSE
```

## 4. Figure overlap

No main scientific figure may appear substantially unchanged in two simultaneously submitted papers without explicit editor disclosure and a scientifically necessary reason.

Shared architecture/background schematic should instead be redrawn to answer each paper's unique question or relegated to referenced methods.

## 5. Text overlap

Parent/background descriptions should be rewritten for each paper's question rather than copied wholesale. Formal definitions that must be identical should be cited/cross-referenced when one paper is public, or disclosed as related manuscript material while simultaneous.

## 6. Submission sequence preference

Current scientific preference, subject to result timing:

1. specialist Articles when protected results close;
2. flagship after at least two specialist evidence streams and external demarcation;
3. #51 according to its independent journal route.

This ordering reduces circular self-citation and prevents the Perspective from promising unpublished efficacy evidence.

## 7. Related-manuscript cover-letter disclosure

For any Nature-Portfolio submission, the cover letter should identify every related ORION-V2 manuscript simultaneously under review/in press and state:

- title/paper ID;
- target/venue status;
- shared data/methods;
- distinct primary question/outcome;
- whether manuscript copy is supplied as requested by the editor.

No paper is hidden merely because disclosure might lower perceived novelty.
