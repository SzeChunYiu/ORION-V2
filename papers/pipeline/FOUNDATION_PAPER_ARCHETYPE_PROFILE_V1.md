## 1. Resolved target and archetype

### Preferred target

**Nature Machine Intelligence — Perspective**.

Current official guidance defines Perspectives as scholarly discussions of fast-moving or emerging topics that present an original, forward-looking vision, are more thought-provoking/speculative than Reviews, remain balanced, and normally use roughly 3,000–4,000 main-text words with up to about 100 references. They are peer reviewed.

### Fallback

**npj Artificial Intelligence — Perspective or Review**.

Current official guidance describes Perspectives as technical scholarly discussions that may advocate a controversial/speculative position and normally stay near 3,000 words with up to about 70 references; Reviews are broader balanced surveys, normally 3,000–4,000 words and about 100 references.

### Dominant archetype

`field-defining Perspective / scientific manifesto / conceptual synthesis`

Secondary archetypes:

- review/synthesis;
- formal research agenda;
- systems-science demarcation paper.

The flagship is **not** a primary-results Article. It may synthesize published literature and define testable field hypotheses, but it should not report unpublished ORION-V2 benchmark outcomes as established results.

## 3. Deep nearest-neighbour readings

### 3.1 Machine behaviour — closest field-demarcation analogue

**Opening move.** The paper does not merely say AI is important. It changes the unit of analysis: intelligent machines should be studied empirically as actors with behavioural patterns and ecologies, not only as engineered artefacts.

**Demarcation move.** It explicitly says the field overlaps with but is distinct from computer science and robotics. It then explains why engineering expertise alone is insufficient and why behavioural-science methods are needed.

**Parent-framework move.** Instead of inventing a complete taxonomy, the paper adapts Tinbergen's four questions from ethology, then adds scales of inquiry (individual machines, collectives, human–machine systems).

**Figure grammar.** The conceptual figures answer different reader questions: what belongs to the field; which disciplines meet; what questions organize studies; at what scale studies occur.

**Boundary/outlook.** Technical, legal and institutional barriers are treated as scientific constraints.

**Critical counterexample.** A Nature correspondence later argued that the proposed field duplicated decades of cybernetics. This is a high-value warning for Machine Epistemics: parent genealogy must be treated as a core argument, not a related-work paragraph.

### 3.2 Sustainability Robotics — closest recent Nature Machine Intelligence manifesto

**Opening move.** Starts from a large societal/scientific objective and a concrete failure: robotics work related to sustainability is fragmented and lacks a unifying framework.

**Compact synthesis.** The entire discipline is made memorable through three principles (minimally invasive, universally accessible, symbiotic) and two dimensions (sustainable robot design; robotic solutions for sustainability).

**Evidence mode.** The Perspective does not pretend to prove a new empirical law. It demonstrates that existing robotics examples and sustainability needs populate the framework and that coordination across engineering, economics, ethics and policy is useful.

**Lesson.** A field Perspective benefits from a compact organizing structure more than a long list of principles. Ten laws should therefore be secondary to a smaller Machine Epistemics framework.

### 3.3 Scientific understanding with AI — closest conceptual parent-synthesis analogue

**Hook.** Uses an oracle thought experiment: perfect prediction would still leave scientists wanting understanding. The thought experiment makes the conceptual gap concrete before introducing terminology.

**Definition.** Rather than inventing a private meaning of scientific understanding, the authors adopt a definition from philosophy of science.

**Synthesis.** The scattered literature is mapped into three dimensions; existing work and scientist anecdotes populate those dimensions.

**Lesson.** Machine Epistemics should borrow strong parent definitions for evidence, validation, authority, observability, etc. rather than define every concept de novo. The new contribution is the relationship among them in machine-mediated scientific transitions.

### 3.4 Computational social science — closest new-substrate analogue

**Object/substrate.** Digital traces create a new empirical substrate for social science at individual and collective scales.

**Opportunity.** The paper shows what becomes observable and which scientific questions become newly feasible.

**Barrier structure.** Access, privacy, methodological training and institutional governance are first-class field-building constraints.

**Lesson.** Machine Epistemics needs to identify what has changed historically enough to make the field timely. The strongest answer is not “LLMs are popular”; it is that scientific reasoning, experimentation, evaluation and revision are becoming executable, persistent machine processes whose internal epistemic transitions can now be instrumented and controlled.

### 3.5 Science of science — closest mature transdiscipline analogue

**Operational field object.** The field studies science itself through large-scale data on scientists, institutions and ideas.

**Mechanism organization.** The Review is structured by scientific mechanisms/questions: networks, problem selection, novelty, careers, team science, citation dynamics.

**Outlook.** It explicitly distinguishes universal patterns from domain-specific variation and recognizes limits to cross-domain generalization.

**Lesson.** Machine Epistemics should be defined by measurable transition objects and failure mechanisms, not by a collection of AI tools. It must also expect domain-specific adaptation rather than universal one-size-fits-all rules.

## 4. Cross-paper foundation pattern

Across the strongest examples, the reusable structure is not `name -> literature survey -> agenda`. It is a four-proof sequence.

### Proof F1 — A new or newly executable object exists

The paper identifies an object/process/substrate whose study has become scientifically consequential:

- behaviour of intelligent machines;
- sustainability-oriented robotics as a coordinated design/problem domain;
- computer-assisted scientific understanding;
- digital traces of human behaviour;
- large-scale data on science itself;
- disease networks rather than isolated genes.

For Machine Epistemics, the candidate object is the **machine-mediated scientific-state transition** inside a bounded research episode.

### Proof F2 — Existing disciplinary decomposition leaves a coordination gap

This proof must be stronger than “many fields are relevant.” It must show a decision or failure that falls between their normal boundaries.

Candidate Machine Epistemics examples:

- provenance proves lineage while scientific validity remains wrong;
- workflow execution succeeds while the scientific inference is invalid;
- active learning chooses an informative experiment while authority or criterion binding forbids the transition;
- formal equivalence holds under one context while a scientific claim needs another relation;
- multiple AI reviewers agree because they share hidden sources/models;
- a representation change improves a benchmark but silently invalidates earlier commitments;
- search appears flat while censored routes make closure unjustified.

The field claim fails if mature parents already make these joint decisions under straightforward composition.

### Proof F3 — A compact organizing framework exists

A foundation paper needs a small framework that readers can remember and use.

**Recommended Machine Epistemics four-problem framework:**

1. **Observe the epistemic state** — What is actually known, unresolved, censored, dependent, comparable or non-identifiable?
2. **Control scientific transitions** — Which action/interpretation may change the scientific state, what must be preserved, and what must reopen?
3. **Assure evidence and authority** — Which support is independent/valid, which evaluator is stable, and what authority ceiling applies?
4. **Govern search-space change and closure** — When is ordinary repair sufficient, when is escalation justified, and when may the episode stop?

The existing ten candidate laws should become a secondary table/box mapped under these four problems rather than ten equal top-level sections.

### Proof F4 — A falsifiable research programme follows

The field must yield more than principles. It should generate measurable questions such as:

- epistemic observability and non-identifiability;
- justified reachability/controllability;
- dependence-aware support and assurance calibration;
- conservative transport across representation/epoch changes;
- selective reopening under invalidated support;
- minimum sufficient escalation;
- bounded epistemic stopping under heterogeneous/censored search;
- evaluator stability under performative response.

The agenda should name benchmarks, units, failure metrics and negative controls, while acknowledging external scientific/ethical authority.

## 5. Anti-columbusing / demarcation gate

The flagship should include a conspicuous section or boxed table titled approximately **“What Machine Epistemics does not rename.”**

At minimum it must credit:

- cybernetics/control theory — dynamical control, feedback, observability/controllability;
- decision theory/POMDPs/metareasoning — action selection under uncertainty/resource constraints;
- active learning/optimal experimental design — informative queries/experiments;
- truth-maintenance systems/ATMS and belief revision — dependency-aware belief maintenance/revision;
- model-based diagnosis — failure hypotheses and discriminating tests;
- formal methods/abstract interpretation/bisimulation — proof, refinement, abstraction and relation semantics;
- causal inference/transportability — causal identification and transport;
- psychometrics/metrology — measurement invariance, linking and traceability;
- provenance/workflow/reproducibility systems — execution lineage and repeatability;
- evidence synthesis/meta-analysis — evidence accumulation and dependence;
- mechanism design/performative prediction/strategic classification — response to evaluation/intervention;
- systematic review/information retrieval — evidence acquisition and stopping;
- AI for Science/agentic/autonomous science — domain-facing scientific automation;
- philosophy of science/social epistemology — normative concepts of evidence, explanation, understanding and authority;
- metascience/science of science — empirical study of science and scientific institutions.

**Field falsifier:** if an information-matched composition of these parents reproduces the same protected scientific-transition decisions across domains with no material residual, the correct conclusion is `INTEGRATION_ENGINEERING_ONLY` rather than a new scientific discipline.

## 6. Recommended flagship architecture for Nature Machine Intelligence

Target main-text length: **3,000–4,000 words** before references/legends.

### Opening (~350–450 words)

Start from the historical transition: AI is no longer only predicting scientific quantities; research processes themselves are becoming executable and persistent. Give 2–3 concrete failure examples showing why task performance is not enough.

End the opening with one question:

> When may a machine-mediated research process legitimately change what science commits to?

### The object of study (~450–550 words)

Define a **Machine-Epistemic Episode** in reader-facing prose. Explain scientific state transition rather than leading with the tuple notation. Use the tuple once, after intuition.

State clearly that a tool output is not a scientific commitment; an interpretation plus evidence/validity/authority conditions is needed.

### Why parent fields do not automatically compose (~600–750 words)

Use a compact parent-ownership table rather than many miniature literature-review subsections.

Focus on 3–5 **composition failures** that no single parent owns. Include the anti-columbusing falsifier here.

### Four control problems (~750–900 words)

Organize the field around the four problems above. Map the ten candidate laws underneath them. Each problem should include:

`reader question -> parent methods -> cross-layer failure -> measurable research question`.

### Methods and research programme (~500–650 words)

Define what counts as Machine Epistemics research: formal results, known-answer/hostile tests, cross-domain transfer, protected evaluations, replay identities and negative controls.

Give a small set of benchmark archetypes instead of ORION-specific paper numbers.

### Barriers, governance and demarcation (~350–500 words)

Discuss evaluator custody, dependence, access to scientific systems/data, human authority, cross-domain validity and risk of creating an unnecessary field label.

### Outlook (~200–300 words)

Return to the historical transition. State the field proposal conditionally. Do not claim establishment.

## 7. Figure grammar for the flagship

### Figure 1 — The new object: a machine-mediated scientific transition

Reader question: **What exactly is being studied?**

Show a bounded research episode with problem contract, scientific state, admissible actions, external observations/tools and transition receipts. Highlight examples where execution success does not warrant a scientific transition.

### Figure 2 — Parent fields and the composition gap

Reader question: **Why is this not merely AI for Science, control theory, provenance or epistemology?**

A relational map/table should show what each major parent already owns and the cross-layer decisions that remain the field hypothesis. This figure doubles as anti-columbusing evidence.

### Figure 3 — Four control problems and measurable questions

Reader question: **How is the proposed field organized and what can it actually study?**

Show the four problems: observe state; control transitions; assure evidence/authority; govern escalation/closure. Under each, show 2–3 measurable research questions/metrics.

### Optional Figure 4 — Field falsification / benchmark programme

Reader question: **What evidence would convince us that the field is real—or unnecessary?**

Show cross-domain recurrence, parent-composed baseline, protected decision residual and negative-control/field-contraction terminals.

Do not use unpublished ORION-V2 result plots in the Perspective.

## 8. What to remove from the current flagship main text

Move to project documentation or eliminate from manuscript-facing prose:

- ORION-V2 K0–K6 module enumeration;
- P-A/P-B/P-C/P-D/P-E paper-programme labels;
- repository architecture or closeout state;
- internal terminal names except when conceptually useful and reader-facing;
- claims that the field is already established;
- long equal-weight lists of ten laws without higher-level organization.

ORION-V2 may be mentioned briefly as one experimental instantiation **only after** external primary evidence exists and only if relevant to the Perspective. It must not function as proof of the field in the current literature-only flagship.

## 9. Foundation-paper editor risks

### RISK-1 — old wine / renamed parents

Highest risk. Directly address with genealogy, strongest-parent products and a falsifiable contraction rule.

### RISK-2 — framework inflation

Too many coordinates/laws can read like a private ontology. Compress to four field problems and make parent-specific algorithms replaceable.

### RISK-3 — ORION self-reference

A field manifesto written around one framework looks promotional. Remove project-specific architecture and make examples system-agnostic.

### RISK-4 — no evidence that the object matters

Use published examples of autonomous/agentic research, automated laboratories, machine review/evaluation and tool-mediated science to show that scientific state transitions are already machine-mediated. Do not convert these examples into evidence that the proposed framework is superior.

### RISK-5 — normative/scientific mixing

Separate empirically/formally testable questions from governance or philosophical norms. Human and institutional authority remains external where appropriate.

### RISK-6 — broad claims without local explanation

Nature Machine Intelligence readers span AI and adjacent sciences. Define unfamiliar epistemic/control concepts locally and explain why each is necessary instead of assuming an ORION vocabulary.
