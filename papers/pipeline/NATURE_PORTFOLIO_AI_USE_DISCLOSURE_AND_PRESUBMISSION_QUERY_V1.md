# Nature Portfolio AI-Use Disclosure and Presubmission Query V1

**Programme:** ORION-V2 / Machine Epistemics  
**Checked against current Nature Portfolio AI policy:** 2026-08-29.  
**Purpose:** handle extensive scientific AI assistance transparently rather than discovering a policy mismatch after formal submission.

## 1. Why an editor query is prudent

The current Nature Portfolio framework distinguishes:

- assistive AI that does not influence scientific judgment;
- evaluative/interpretive AI that can introduce intellectual content but requires human oversight, verification and disclosure;
- prohibited AI use that replaces scholarly judgment, lacks transparency or presents AI-generated core reasoning as human-derived.

ORION-V2 used LLMs far beyond language polishing, including candidate formalizations, experimental-design suggestions, literature synthesis, hostile criticism, software generation and manuscript drafting.

The human authors intend to retain full scientific responsibility and independently review/adopt the final reasoning. Because the workflow sits near the policy's evaluative/interpretive boundary, a presubmission editorial query is recommended for Nature Portfolio targets.

## 2. Draft editor query

> Dear Editors,
>
> We are preparing a manuscript entitled “[TITLE]” for potential submission to [JOURNAL]. We would like to confirm that our disclosed use of large language model tools is compatible with the journal’s current AI policy before formal submission.
>
> LLMs were used as research assistants during literature discovery, generation and comparison of candidate formalizations/methodological options, adversarial critique, software/test generation, and manuscript drafting/editing. They were not treated as authors or autonomous authorities. The final human authors independently review the load-bearing literature, verify the mathematical and empirical claims against proofs/raw receipts, decide which hypotheses and interpretations to adopt, retain negative and parent-sufficient outcomes, and accept responsibility for the originality, accuracy and integrity of the manuscript.
>
> We plan to document this use transparently in the manuscript and to provide a human scientific-adoption/accountability statement if useful to the editors. We will not describe the AI assistance as language-only, and no AI-generated data, citations or results will be presented without human verification.
>
> Given the material use of AI as an interpretive and methodological research aid, would the journal consider this workflow compatible with its current AI policy provided that human scholarly judgment, verification, disclosure and accountability remain explicit as described?
>
> We would be grateful for guidance on the preferred placement and level of detail for the disclosure.

Do not submit this text unchanged if the current policy has changed; refresh immediately before use.

## 3. Draft manuscript disclosure — Nature Portfolio

Recommended Methods or suitable disclosure language, subject to editor confirmation:

> **Use of artificial intelligence tools.** Large language model systems were used as research-assistance tools during this project for literature discovery and synthesis, generation and comparison of candidate formalizations and methodological options, adversarial critique, software and test generation, and drafting/editing of manuscript text. AI systems were not treated as authors or as sources of scientific authority. The human authors independently reviewed and adopted the final research questions, methods, formal claims, empirical interpretations and manuscript conclusions; checked load-bearing literature and citations; verified formal claims against human-readable proofs and/or mechanical checks; reviewed raw or case-level evidence behind headline results; and retained adverse, null, parent-sufficient and `CANNOT_CHECK` outcomes. The human authors take responsibility for the originality, accuracy and integrity of the work.

The actual final disclosure must reflect the real workflow of the specific paper; delete tasks not used and add material tasks that were used.

## 4. Perspective-specific disclosure

For the flagship Perspective, there may be no conventional Methods section. Place the disclosure in the location requested by the editor or in an appropriate end-matter section.

The Perspective author group must independently adopt:

- parent atlas and cited claims;
- F0–F3 foundation competition;
- transition ecology;
- field falsification/demarcation rule;
- recursive-development frontier boundaries.

## 5. Article-specific disclosure

For P-A–P-D, the human authors must additionally inspect:

- protected raw/case-level results;
- primary statistical analysis;
- invalid/missing run classification;
- every headline figure/table;
- result-conditioned abstract sentence;
- Discussion branch selection.

## 6. Prohibited disclosure minimization

Do not use wording such as:

- “AI was used only for grammar”;
- “AI assisted with writing”

if the actual workflow included theory generation, literature analysis, code or scientific criticism.

## 7. AI authorship

No AI tool/model appears in the author list.

Human authorship must be based on actual scholarly contribution and responsibility, not merely willingness to sign off.

## 8. AI-generated imagery

Do not use generative-AI artwork for Nature-Portfolio paper figures. Figures should be deterministic plots/diagrams from frozen data/receipts or human-designed scientific schematics with source files and human review, subject to the current policy at filing time.
