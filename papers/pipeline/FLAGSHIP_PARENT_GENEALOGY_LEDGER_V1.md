## Direct anti-columbusing lessons

### Machine Behaviour precedent

Rahwan et al. (Nature, 2019) successfully framed intelligent machines as a new empirical object and proposed an interdisciplinary science, but a Nature correspondence by Moss et al. argued that the proposal overlooked cybernetics. For Machine Epistemics this is not merely history; it supplies the hostile reviewer template:

> Does the manuscript rediscover an older field after replacing its vocabulary with AI-era terms?

A manuscript that cannot answer this row by row should not use field-establishing language.

### Metrology warning

NIST explicitly states that metrological traceability does not by itself guarantee fitness for purpose and that every link contributes uncertainty. Machine Epistemics should not generalize 'traceability' into a vague synonym for validity. Any cross-domain abstraction must preserve this native distinction.

### Computational epistemology warning

Computational epistemology already asks when reliable learning/inquiry is feasible. Machine Epistemics cannot claim epistemic observability, learnability or convergence in the abstract. Its possible residual must depend on the richer bounded **scientific-process episode** and cross-layer transition conditions.

### Epistemic engineering warning

Epistemic engineering already treats epistemic change as distributed across humans, tools, organizations and control structures. Machine Epistemics cannot claim distributed knowledge construction. Its narrower question is whether scientific commitments in AI-mediated research admit machine-executable transition warrants with measurable failure modes.
