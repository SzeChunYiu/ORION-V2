# 2. Historical collision: “Epistemics” is not a new disciplinary word

Historical work by G. Patrick Meredith used **Epistemics** as a proposed science concerning the structure/transmission of human knowledge and its instrumental aspects. The term also has later uses in discourse studies and other domains.

### Flagship consequence

Do not say:

> “We introduce the term epistemics.”

Preferred:

> “We use the compound *Machine Epistemics* for a deliberately provisional field hypothesis concerning machine-mediated scientific transitions.”

The exact compound's current distinctness is an external naming/demarcation question, not a novelty claim.

---

# 3. Machine epistemology is direct prior art

Gary Wheeler's **machine epistemology** explicitly treats machine learning as a form of inquiry and is already included in the flagship parent atlas.

### Consequence

Machine Epistemics cannot be defined as simply “epistemology for machines” or “the study of how machines know.” That would collide directly with prior terminology and philosophy-of-ML programmes.

The proposed residual must remain transition-level and science-process-level:

```text
how machine-mediated inquiry changes
sources / relations / representations / methods / experiments /
evidence / agendas / commitments / authority-relevant scientific state
```

# 4. Epistemic responsibility in human–AI collaboration

**Dan Lloyd (2025), “Epistemic responsibility: toward a community standard for human-AI collaborations,” Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence.**

This work already argues for epistemic responsibility standards around human–AI collaboration.

### Consequence

The flagship cannot claim originality for the idea that responsibility and accountability must remain explicit in human–AI knowledge production.

Its candidate delta, if any, lies in **typed scientific transition responsibilities and protected cross-parent decisions**, not generic responsibility ethics.

# 5. Epistemic-control architectures for AI research are direct current parents

**Grzegorz Wojarnik (2026), “Spec-Driven AI for Empirical Research: A Scoping Review and an Architecture of Epistemic Control.”**

The public paper explicitly addresses:

- exploration versus confirmation;
- provenance;
- analytic flexibility/specifications;
- reproducibility;
- epistemic-control architecture for AI-assisted empirical research.

### Consequence

A workflow architecture combining preregistration, specifications, provenance and reproducibility is not the flagship residual.

F0/F1 must treat spec-driven epistemic-control architecture as a direct strong parent.

# 6. Verification infrastructure / engineering of science is another close parent

**Jiaqi W. Ma (2026), “Toward an Engineering of Science: Rebalancing Generation and Verification in the Age of AI,” arXiv:2605.10425.**

The paper argues that AI makes generation cheap relative to verification and proposes structured claim/evidence/assumption **blueprints** as epistemic infrastructure for cheaper local/distributed verification.

### Consequence

The flagship cannot claim novelty for:

- the generation/verification asymmetry;
- structured claim/evidence graphs as AI-era science infrastructure;
- the general call to engineer verification systems around AI-generated science.

Machine Epistemics must therefore explain why **scientific transition validity across heterogeneous parent theories** is not exhausted by structured verification infrastructure.

This source should be included in the final contrary/nearest-parent discussion if its then-current version remains load-bearing.

# 7. Critically Engaged Pragmatism challenges universal AI science evaluation

**Carole J. Lee (2026), “Critically Engaged Pragmatism: Scientific Norm and Social, Pragmatist Epistemology for AI Science Evaluation Tools.”**

The work argues that AI science evaluation tools should not be treated as objective arbiters and emphasizes purpose-specific reliability and critical community practices.

### Consequence

This is a direct warning against a universal machine-science evaluator.

The flagship must preserve:

- context/purpose relative evaluation;
- social/institutional criticism;
- external authority/demarcation;
- possibility that no unified evaluator exists.

This strengthens F3/pluralism and the `CANNOT_CHECK`/external-authority boundary.

# 8. Closed-loop AI science already has an epistemology/governance literature

**Zenil et al. (2026), “The future of fundamental science led by generative closed-loop artificial intelligence,” Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence.**

The paper argues for graded autonomy, human relevance, verification, domain-method alignment and plurality as AI closes more of the scientific loop, while warning about recursive bias/epistemic collapse.

### Consequence

The flagship cannot claim novelty for:

- closed-loop AI science creating epistemological problems;
- machine-generated strategies inaccessible to human intuition;
- graded autonomy/human relevance;
- the need for hybrid verification/governance.

Machine-native cognition + external witness must be positioned as a more specific candidate transition constraint, not a first statement of this tension.

---

# 9. Comparative epistemology and epistemic drift widen the philosophy-of-AI neighbourhood

Current 2026 work includes:

- **Matteo Pasquinelli**, comparative epistemology of machine, organism and language in AI models;
- **Søgaard, Rajcic & Scott**, *Epistemic Drift in Mind-Model Systems*;
- broader work on epistemic dependence in AI-mediated learning and policymaking.

These are not direct Machine-Epistemics field collisions, but they show that “AI + epistemics/epistemology” is a crowded conceptual space.

### Consequence

The flagship title must earn distinctness from its scientific object and test programme, not from the novelty of combining “machine” with epistemic vocabulary.

# 10. Contrary/limiting perspectives that must remain visible

The final Perspective should retain at least four serious contraction perspectives:

## A. Parent-field sufficiency

Existing epistemology, social epistemology, statistics, control, causal inference, assurance, security and governance may be enough under careful composition.

This is F0/F1 and must not be treated as a strawman.

## B. Purpose-relative evaluation and social authority

Critical-pragmatist/social-epistemology work warns that evaluation reliability is purpose-specific and socially situated. This directly limits universal evaluator claims.

## C. Verification infrastructure rather than new science

Engineering/spec-driven approaches may solve much of the problem as structured infrastructure without requiring a new field.

## D. Graded human–AI autonomy rather than machine-specific epistemic theory

Closed-loop science literature may frame the problem adequately as domain-sensitive hybrid governance/verification rather than a new higher-order discipline.

## E. Plural/domain epistemologies

F3 remains live when common abstractions erase local scientific meaning, authority or practice.
