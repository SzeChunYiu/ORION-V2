# V2-C04 Research and Manuscript Plan V0

**Working title:** *Obligation-Driven Scientific Problem Solving with Honest Terminals*  
**Status:** flagship candidate; all claims `CANNOT_CHECK`.

## 1. Scientific object

A complete scientific solver should choose among heterogeneous information, computation, experiment, proof, repair, representation and external-authority actions according to explicit scientific obligations. It should not equate answer production with justified solution and should preserve V1 capabilities while allowing non-linear, concurrent and adaptive workflows.

## 2. Candidate thesis

A nested policy over a typed plural scientific state can improve justified terminal decisions or resource-adjusted justified reachability over fixed-stage workflows and the strongest donor-composed adaptive policy, without regressing validity, authority, semantic fidelity, selective reopening, V1 parity or calibrated non-success.

## 3. Candidate architecture under test

- protected constitution/execution substrate;
- problem/frontier contract compiler;
- plural scientific state;
- ordinary action-selection policy;
- coupled hypothesis–experiment/model loop;
- witnessed Jump/escalation subpolicy;
- protected solver/meta-skill revision;
- synthesis, independent validation and adoption.

The paper must treat this as a hypothesis and include contraction to a multi-policy or parent-composed solution.

A weak fixed chain or generic ReAct baseline is scientifically insufficient.

## 6. Experimental programme

### Study S1 — V1 capability parity

Bind the exact V1 capability graph after issue #2. For every family, compare frozen V1, candidate V2 and parent replacements on known-answer and hostile cases.

### Study S2 — Simple/direct controls

Problems where a deterministic/native method is sufficient. Measure unnecessary deliberation, search, escalation and cost. V2 complexity must lose when it should.

### Study S3 — Coupled hidden-world science

Finite mechanistic worlds with candidate hypotheses, noisy/costly experiments and protected ground truth. Require active discrimination, model criticism and honest alternatives.

### Study S4 — Multiple and interaction faults

Serial upstream, independent multiple, common-cause, distributed and interaction-only failures. Test responsibility and repair scope.

### Study S5 — Formal synthesis/refinement

Program, theorem, model or equation tasks with exact checkers, counterexamples and abstraction/language insufficiency controls.

### Study S6 — Computational/physical experiment adapters

Begin with simulations and public experimental environments. Add physical instruments only through domain-specific safety/authority protocols.

### Study S7 — Structural relation and representation cases

Use C02 relations to test observational ties, safe abstraction, global obstruction, context drift and cross-generation transport.

### Study S8 — Frontier opportunity and Jump

No narrow pre-specified solution route; test problem finding, remote donors and J0–J8 minimum escalation.

### Study S9 — Meta-solver revision

Freeze policy/skill changes, then test fresh domains, negative-history retention, non-regression and later-successor ability.

### Study S10 — Expert-owned shadow open research

Use uncontaminated domain-expert questions, matched budgets, exact logs and independent replay. Automated reviewer scores are secondary only.

## 9. Ablations

- remove explicit obligations;
- replace plural model portfolio with one model;
- remove context-relative relations;
- remove dependence modelling;
- remove authority separation;
- remove negative history;
- force linear workflow;
- force scalar reward;
- disable external expert request;
- disable typed terminals;
- disable Jump or allow unconstrained Jump;
- remove V1 parity gate.

## 10. Hostile cases

- direct answer where V2 overthinks;
- censored route misclassified as absence;
- endless search on a structurally non-identifiable target;
- local bug mistaken for framework failure;
- multiple faults forced into one cause;
- local solutions that cannot glue;
- correct content lacking adoption authority;
- correlated validators voting unanimously;
- evaluator gaming;
- representation transition that silently invalidates old claims;
- Jump that improves current tasks and harms future successor ability.
