# V2-C10 Research and Manuscript Plan V0

**Working title:** *Scientific Frontier Opportunity Discovery beyond Novelty and Interestingness*  
**Status:** high-risk/high-value candidate; all claims `CANNOT_CHECK`.

## 1. Scientific problem

Frontier science often begins before a narrow problem contract exists. A scientific system may observe anomalies, contradictions, unexplained regularities, missing connections, model inadequacy or unmet needs and propose new research questions. But novelty, surprise and generated ideas do not by themselves establish scientific importance, testability, tractability or authority to set an agenda.

## 2. Strong parent threats

- problem finding and problem construction in creativity research;
- abductive and anomaly-driven scientific discovery;
- literature-based discovery;
- horizon scanning and technology foresight;
- research portfolio optimization and real options;
- unmet-needs and design research;
- open-ended goal exploration, quality-diversity and POET-style systems;
- science-of-science opportunity and combinatorial-novelty measures;
- active learning and value-of-information experiment generation.

The paper must use or reduce against these parents rather than claim autonomous question generation generically.

## 3. Candidate thesis

A source-bound opportunity object that jointly represents importance, unmet need, falsifiability, decisive-probe availability, expected information, tractability, ethical/resource feasibility, remote-donor structure and external agenda authority may improve expert-validated research opportunity discovery over novelty, anomaly, LBD, portfolio and open-ended-search baselines while controlling false opportunities.

## 5. Required distinctions

- surprising versus scientifically important;
- novel versus previously undiscovered;
- testable versus merely discussable;
- tractable versus resource-infeasible;
- important but unethical/unauthorized;
- local anomaly versus systematic model inadequacy;
- interesting question versus a question with a decisive experiment;
- opportunity proposal versus programme adoption;
- diverse frontier portfolio versus useless dispersion.

## 6. Benchmark families

### B1 — Historically recoverable opportunities

Construct time-stamped cases where pre-discovery literature/data contained enough information to propose a later validated research question, while protecting post-discovery leakage.

### B2 — Synthetic mechanistic worlds

Hide scientifically valuable questions and decisive probes in finite worlds with distractor anomalies and superficially novel artifacts.

### B3 — Literature bridge cases

Disconnected literatures contain a valid bridge, invalid surface analogies and already-known/citation-near controls.

### B4 — Model-criticism opportunities

Incumbent models systematically fail predictive checks; distinguish parameter error, missing variable, representation failure and measurement error.

### B5 — Unmet-needs/design cases

Scientific/clinical/engineering needs with variable importance, testability, feasibility and authority.

### B6 — Portfolio cases

Several incomparable opportunities compete for resources. Preserve a Pareto/real-options set rather than one scalar winner.

### B7 — Negative interestingness controls

Visually surprising patterns, high novelty scores or distant analogies with no scientific test or decision value.

### B8 — Agenda authority cases

Scientifically promising opportunities that require external community, ethical, regulatory or stakeholder adoption.

## 9. Non-scalar selection

Hard constraints apply before portfolio comparison:

- ethical/legal/safety admissibility;
- minimum falsifiability or explicit exploratory status;
- evidence and source provenance;
- feasible decisive probe or honest long-horizon option;
- no protected-outcome leakage;
- proposal authority only.

Eligible opportunities form a Pareto/robust portfolio over importance, information, unmet need, novelty/diversity, tractability, cost, risk and future option value. The current V0 reference model tests only a small deterministic subset.

## 10. Hostile controls

- novelty without testability;
- easy but scientifically trivial question;
- important but resource-infeasible question;
- unethical/unauthorized experiment;
- anomaly explained by measurement failure;
- remote analogy with hidden incompatible assumption;
- already-known result presented as opportunity;
- opportunity discovered using post-cutoff leakage;
- agenda proposal self-adopted;
- portfolio collapsed to one fashionable topic;
- high diversity with no downstream value.
