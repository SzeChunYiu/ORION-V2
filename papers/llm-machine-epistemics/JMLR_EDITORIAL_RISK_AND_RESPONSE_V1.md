# JMLR Editorial Risk and Response V1

**Issue:** #51  
**Purpose:** pre-answer the strongest desk-review and full-review objections without allowing post-outcome novelty rescue.

## Risk 1 — “This is classical state sufficiency with new terminology.”

### Strength

**High.** Predictive states, decisional states, Blackwell sufficiency, reward-predictive states, POMDP/AIS state, compatible finite-state reduction and rate-distortion parents own most mathematical components.

### Allowed response

Concede the mathematics explicitly. The paper's claim is not a new generic state theorem. The candidate contribution is the **assessment task**:

```text
registered current prediction target
+ registered current decision
+ present-equivalence gate
+ representation retention intervention
+ alternate-channel/parametric-reconstruction exclusion
+ identical later evidence
+ future compatibility
+ update AND maintain/selective-reopening scoring
```

The finite theorem only proves why this third assessment axis cannot be inferred from the first two.

### Forbidden response

Do not rename parent mathematics as “epistemic state theory” or expand the theorem after review.

---

## Risk 2 — “Belief-R already tests update/maintain after new evidence.”

### Strength

**High direct-neighbor pressure.**

### Allowed response

Belief-R studies whether outputs should change or remain when new evidence is given. The present paper conditions on an additional causal question:

> after current prediction and current decision have been matched, does **retained representation state** causally determine later revision under the same evidence?

The audit therefore requires a representation intervention/removal claim plus alternate-channel controls rather than only before/after output evaluation.

### Kill condition

If a direct parent is found that already implements the complete matched-current representation intervention + common evidence + update/maintain design, contract/merge.

---

## Risk 3 — “MEMENTO/memory-compression work already shows state loss matters downstream.”

### Strength

**High practical-parent pressure.**

### Allowed response

Generic downstream degradation after compression is parent-owned. The registered question is narrower: a representation can pass **both current language and current responsibility tests** yet fail only after later evidence. MEMENTO also motivates the mandatory alternate-channel gate because apparent eviction may leave information elsewhere.

## Risk 4 — “The no-certification theorem is trivial.”

### Strength

**Medium to high.** The construction is intentionally simple.

### Allowed response

Agree that theorem complexity is not the paper's main novelty. Its role is analogous to an impossibility/counterexample establishing an evaluation blind spot. Publication value must come from a well-defined assessment object, strong controls, and consequences for evaluating representation compression/memory systems.

### Desk-review implication

The Introduction must put the **audit** before `Omega_dyn` or state-minimization machinery.

## Risk 5 — “No real LLM experiment means insufficient practical relevance.”

### Strength

**Medium / venue-dependent.**

### Allowed response

JMLR explicitly accepts theoretical studies and formalization of new learning/assessment tasks. The paper specifies a directly executable protocol for frozen models and external memories without requiring training. The theoretical result stands independently.

### If AE requires bridge evidence

Execute the already-frozen Protocol V3 as a successor evidence addition. Do not change task definitions after outcome access.

## Risk 6 — “The predictive reference is artificially weak.”

### Strength

**Material if unaddressed.**

### Allowed response

The paper explicitly registers the prediction protocol `rho` and includes a stronger **controlled-future state** condition whose target includes the later intervention family. Under that stronger target, the dormant distinction may become current-state-relevant and the P2 premium can contract. This is a required positive control demonstrating task/channel relativity.

### Forbidden response

Do not claim insufficiency relative to all possible future interventions.

---

## Risk 7 — “Deleting a memory field does not prove the information is gone.”

### Strength

**Material causal-validity risk.**

### Allowed response

Protocol V3 requires alternate-channel and parametric-reconstruction checks across prompt/context, KV, hidden activations, summaries, retrieval keys, tool/session state, external memory and fixed model knowledge where observable. Strong P2 attribution is prohibited if removal cannot be established.

## Risk 9 — “The responsibility variable is arbitrary benchmark labeling.”

### Allowed response

Every responsibility must define a decision problem `(Q,A,loss,semantics)` tied to management of a claim/model/evidence/scope/support/identifiability/revision object. Arbitrary auxiliary labels do not qualify merely by notation.

The paper is decision-relative; it makes no philosophical claim that the variable exhausts belief or understanding.

---

## Risk 10 — “Why not simply retain the full history?”

### Allowed response

The theory is explicitly responsibility-relative. Full history is a ceiling, not a default architecture. The audit asks what compression preserves current versus future registered decisions and makes the cost/retention trade-off visible. Unbounded responsibility support eventually defeats nontrivial compression; that is a limitation, not a prescription to retain everything.

---

# Editor-facing acceptance case

A favorable editorial reading should not depend on difficult mathematics. It should be:

1. representation compression/memory is a central ML practice;
2. current prediction/current decision are common evaluation endpoints;
3. these endpoints cannot certify a later revision capability;
4. the paper formalizes that blind spot and supplies an executable causal audit;
5. the strongest parent theories are explicitly credited rather than renamed.
