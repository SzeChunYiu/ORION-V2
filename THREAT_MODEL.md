# ORION-V2 Research and Scientific Threat Model

## Protected assets

- immutable ORION V1 history and exact handoff identity;
- native donor semantics and judgments;
- protected evaluators, holdouts and adoption authority;
- source, mapping, execution and decision provenance;
- negative, harmful, censored and `CANNOT_CHECK` history;
- V1 capability parity;
- paper identity and claim boundaries.

## Primary threats

### T1 — V1 contamination

Prospective V2 work changes V1 code, papers, terminology or terminals before freeze.

**Control:** separate repository, exact handoff gate, non-retroactivity receipt.

### T2 — architecture inheritance by familiarity

V1 atoms/workflows are retained because contributors know them, not because re-derivation supports them.

**Control:** mandatory capability and atom dispositions; no automatic `KEEP`.

### T3 — donor laundering

A mature external mechanism is translated into ORION vocabulary and presented as V2 novelty.

**Control:** native reconstruction, parent ownership, strongest donor product and claim contraction.

### T4 — false structural analogy

Remote domains look similar after assumptions, context or semantics are erased.

**Control:** context-bound relations, native known-answer preservation and distinguishing counter-probes.

### T5 — unconditional embedding collapse

One metric treats role, prediction, observation, intervention, decision, abstraction and semantic identity as the same relation.

**Control:** multi-relational structural theory, partial orders and incomparability.

### T6 — search-route blindness

Unavailable/censored sources are treated as evidence of absence or saturation.

**Control:** route status, coverage vector and fail-closed `CENSORED_ROUTE`.

### T7 — protected-outcome leakage

The theory, mapping, Jump proposal or benchmark is changed after viewing protected outcomes.

**Control:** prospective freezes, content-addressed proposals, evaluator custody and fresh suites.

### T8 — reward/authority hacking

A system improves a score by changing the evaluator, denominator, objective, exclusions, constraints or authority rules.

**Control:** protected constitution, non-compensatory gates and external adoption.

### T9 — correlated validation

Multiple agents/sources are counted as independent although they share models, data, prompts, lineage or social influence.

**Control:** dependence-aware evidence models and independent replay identities.

### T10 — false Jump

Missing evidence, local bugs, censored search or model expansion are mislabeled as framework insufficiency.

**Control:** minimum-responsible-level ladder, lower-level controls and false-escalation benchmarks.

### T11 — V1 capability regression

A more elegant V2 architecture loses bounded search, diagnosis, selective reopening, authority, replay or honest terminals.

**Control:** exact V1 parity suite before frontier evaluation.

### T12 — premature paper identity

Planning candidates are numbered or promoted before parent reduction and evidence.

**Control:** provisional candidate IDs, publication ledger and external identity freeze.

### T13 — implementation before handoff

Schemas or planning artifacts quietly become running V2 code before V1 freeze.

**Control:** source/development/package gates, branch review and issue #2.

### T14 — retrospective narrative

A successful result is explained through an invented research story after the fact.

**Control:** contemporaneous research provenance, negative history and immutable protocols.

## Scientific response

A detected threat creates a retained failure record and may reopen affected mappings, protocols, paper candidates or the entire V2 programme. No threat is discharged merely by passing local tests.
